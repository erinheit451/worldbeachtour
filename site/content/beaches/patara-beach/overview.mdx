Patara Beach runs 18 kilometers along Turkey's Lycian coast in Antalya Province's Kaş district, fronting the village of Gelemiş. Since 3 March 1990, the beach, its dunes, the wetlands behind it, and the ruins of the ancient city buried under the sand have sat inside a Specially Protected Area declared under Official Gazette No. 20449 — a law written to stop hotel and holiday-house construction that was already underway when biologists identified the beach, in 1988, as one of the Mediterranean's most important nesting sites for the loggerhead turtle, Caretta caretta.

The protection is real but incomplete. Conservation group MEDASSET, which monitors the site for the Council of Europe's Bern Convention, counted 27 houses inside the protected zone by 2015 and 255 by 2018, built despite the construction ban. What the law has held is scale: there is no hotel chain on Patara's sand and nothing built at the density of Kalkan, the nearest resort town 20 kilometers away by road. The beach still closes to visitors nightly, 20:00 to 08:00 from 1 May to 31 October, to protect nesting and hatching turtles, and Pamukkale University's monitoring team logged a record 524 loggerhead nests in the 2023 season, up from the previous record of 434 in 2020.

## What to Expect

Most of Patara's 18 kilometers is open, undeveloped sand with no shade and no facilities — the core nesting habitat the 1990 law exists to protect. Development is concentrated at a single managed entrance near Gelemiş, with a boardwalk over the dunes, a ticket booth, sunbed rental, and one café. Behind the dunes at that same entrance lies the excavated ancient city: a lighthouse built in AD 60 under Nero, Hadrian's granary from AD 131, a theater rebuilt under Antoninus Pius in AD 147, and a bouleuterion — the Lycian League's assembly hall — identified during excavation in 1991. One ticket covers both beach and ruins. Nicholas of Myra, later Saint Nicholas, was born in Patara around AD 270, though he spent most of his life in nearby Myra.

Away from the entrance, the beach is quiet by design: the same law that keeps hotels off the sand also keeps crowds thinner than at developed Turkish beaches nearby. The southern stretch, reached only by a long walk, is described in Turkish-language sources as an informally used naturist area with the sparsest signage on the beach.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**Environment** — Patara is one of 21 Turkish beaches still monitored for loggerhead nesting, and its 1990 protection order is one of the country's oldest Specially Protected Area designations. It is also a documented case of a construction ban that international monitors say has been repeatedly violated — a genuine conservation success story complicated by an equally real enforcement failure.

**History** — The beach shares its name and its protection with a Lycian League capital that has been under excavation only since 1988. The lighthouse, granary, and theater are visible from the sand; the harbor they once served silted into the marsh a visitor finds today.

**Access & Safety** — The nightly closure is strictly enforced, with fines revised most years for inflation. Reaching the beach requires a car, dolmuş, or arranged transport; there is no direct public bus.

Patara is a beach defined by a single piece of legislation and by that legislation's limits. The 1990 order kept the coastline free of large-scale resort development for more than three decades; it did not keep it free of unauthorized building, and the gap between those two outcomes is documented in the same conservation reporting that credits the law with protecting the turtles at all.
