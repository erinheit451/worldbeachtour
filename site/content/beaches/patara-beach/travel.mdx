# Patara Beach Travel Guide

Patara Beach is an 18-kilometer stretch of the Turkish Mediterranean coast in Antalya Province's Kaş district — 18 km by Turkish Wikipedia's measure, though MEDASSET's own reports give 12 km — protected since 18 January 1990 under a Specially Protected Area order (Council of Ministers Decision No. 90/77) that bans large-scale construction across the beach, its dunes, and the ancient city buried beneath them. That protection is genuine but not absolute — see the Environment guide for the full record — and it shapes every practical aspect of visiting: no beachfront hotels, a single managed entrance, and a strictly enforced nightly closure during the loggerhead nesting season.

## Getting There

**By Air**

Dalaman International Airport (DLM) is the nearest airport. The dataset figure of 66.6 kilometers is a straight-line distance; the actual road route via the D400 coastal highway runs roughly 106–130 kilometers depending on the routing source, and takes about 1 hour 45 minutes to 2 hours 15 minutes to drive. Antalya International Airport (AYT) is a larger, more distant alternative, well over 200 kilometers east.

**By Car**

From Dalaman, the D400 highway runs south and east along the coast toward Kaş, then continues toward Kalkan. Signs for Gelemiş village mark the turn inland to the Patara beach and ruins entrance. There is no direct public bus service to the beach itself; dolmuş minibuses connect Kalkan, Kaş, and Fethiye to Gelemiş, from which the beach entrance is roughly a 20–25 minute walk.

**From Nearby Towns**

- **Kalkan** — about 20 km by road, the nearest town with a developed tourism and restaurant scene.
- **Kaş** — about 45 km by road, a larger town with more accommodation and a wider dining range.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Turkey's Mediterranean coast runs a dry, hot summer (roughly June–September) and a milder, wetter winter. Loggerhead nesting and the nightly beach closure run from 1 May through the end of turtle season — sources disagree on the exact closing date, giving 15 September, 30 September, and 31 October — so visiting inside that window means planning around the 20:00 gate closure. Outside that window the closure lifts, but the region's tourist infrastructure — the Gelemiş pansiyons, Kalkan's restaurants — also scales down.

## Where to Stay

**Gelemiş** — The village directly behind the beach, about 3 km inland (roughly a 20–25 minute walk to the entrance). Its 2022 census population was 859. Accommodation here is limited to small, family-run pansiyons; the 1990 protection order is the reason there is nothing larger. This is the closest base to the beach itself.

**Kalkan** — About 20 km away by road, a harbor town with a denser concentration of hotels, guesthouses, and restaurants than Gelemiş, commonly used as a day-trip base for Patara.

**Kaş** — About 45 km away by road, larger again, with more accommodation variety and a wider dining scene; also a common overnight base for visitors covering multiple sites on the Lycian coast.

## Costs

<DataCard label="Admission" value="One ticket" unit="Covers beach + ancient city" />

A single admission ticket covers both the beach and the archaeological site behind it; posted hours run roughly 08:00–19:00 in the main season, per site listings — sources vary slightly on the exact closing time — with reduced hours in winter. Reported ticket prices vary by year with Turkey's inflation-linked fee schedule, so travelers should confirm the current rate before visiting rather than rely on a fixed figure. The only food service directly on the beach is a single café/kiosk at the main entrance; Gelemiş, Kalkan, and Kaş offer more dining options at increasing distance and price.

## Safety

The Mediterranean here has a small tidal range; the primary hazard across most of the beach (18 km by Turkish Wikipedia's measure, 12 km by MEDASSET's) is sun exposure rather than surf, since shade and facilities exist only near the main entrance. The dataset records zero shark incidents for this location.

The nightly closure — 20:00 to 08:00, 1 May through the end of turtle season (sources disagree on the exact closing date, giving 15 September, 30 September, and 31 October) — is strictly enforced, with fines that Turkish authorities revise most years for inflation; reported figures in recent seasons have ranged from roughly 387,000 to 699,000 Turkish lira. Visitors should plan to be off the beach well before the 20:00 gate closure during this period.

## Local Notes

**No large hotels on the beach** — Construction has been banned across the protected zone since 1990. Conservation monitoring by MEDASSET has documented unauthorized building inside the zone regardless — 255 buildings in various stages of construction as of an assessment first reported in 2016 and still being cited as of 2020, in addition to 27 houses already inhabited in 2015 — but nothing at hotel scale sits directly on the sand.

**Almost no shade** — Beyond the entrance boardwalk, the beach has no shade structures. Sun protection and water are necessary for any walk along its length.

**The ruins are part of the same ticket** — The ancient city — lighthouse (AD 60), Hadrian's granary (AD 131), theater (rebuilt AD 147) — sits directly behind the entrance dunes and is included in the beach admission.
