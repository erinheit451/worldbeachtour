# Kappad Beach Travel Guide

Kappad Beach sits on the Arabian Sea about 16 kilometres north of Kozhikode (Calicut), Kerala, near the town of Koyilandy (Quilandy). It is a quiet quartz beach known for one thing: a 1975 stone monument stating that Vasco da Gama landed here in 1498 — a claim the voyage's own logbook places offshore and further south. A rocky headland topped by a temple breaks the shoreline, and the Korapuzha backwaters run behind the beach.

## Getting There

### By Air

The nearest airport is **Calicut International Airport (Kozhikode, code CCJ)**, at Karipur southeast of the city, roughly **38 km** from Kappad by road — plan about 1 to 1.5 hours depending on traffic through Kozhikode. It has domestic connections across India and international flights to the Gulf. From the airport, take a prepaid taxi or drive north through Kozhikode toward Koyilandy.

### By Train

**Koyilandy (Quilandy) railway station** is the closest, about **10 km** north of the beach on the Mangaluru–Kozhikode coastal line. **Kozhikode (Calicut) main station**, about 16 km south, has more long-distance services and is the more reliable arrival point. From either station, hire an auto-rickshaw or taxi for the last stretch to the coast.

### By Road

Kappad lies just off **National Highway 66**, the main coastal highway. From Kozhikode, drive north on NH 66 and turn coastward near Thiruvangoor toward Kappad; the trip takes roughly 40 minutes outside peak traffic. **KSRTC and private buses** run frequently along NH 66 between Kozhikode and Koyilandy — get off at the nearest stop and take an auto-rickshaw the final few kilometres.

### By Backwater Boat

A slower, scenic alternative is to reach Kappad down the **Korapuzha backwaters**, which meet the coast behind the beach. Boat services here are informal and seasonal rather than a scheduled ferry, so arrange a ride locally in advance.

## Best Time to Visit

**November to February** is the prime window: dry weather, warm days, and the calmest sea of the year. Wave heights are lowest in November (a monthly mean near 0.16 m), so this is the best stretch for wading and for the west-facing sunset.

**March to May** is hot and humid but still dry, with gentle surf. It is quieter than the winter tourist months.

**June to August** is the southwest monsoon: heavy rain, grey skies, and the roughest sea of the year (July has the highest mean waves, around 0.59 m). Swimming is not advisable, and the beach is largely empty, though the coast is dramatic in the rain.

## Where to Stay

Kappad itself is a small coastal settlement with limited lodging, so rather than name specific properties, here is the honest lay of the land.

- **At Kappad**: a handful of small beachside stays and homestays sit near the sand, but supply is thin; book ahead, especially in the winter season.
- **Kozhikode (Calicut)**, about 16 km south, has the full range — business hotels, mid-range options, and budget lodges — plus the city's food and transport links. Most visitors base here and day-trip to the beach.
- **Koyilandy**, about 10 km north, offers simpler local accommodation near the railway station.

## Costs

Kappad Beach is **free to enter** — there is no admission fee.

- **Food**: the state-built beach restaurant and small vendors handle snacks and tea; for a full meal, Kozhikode's restaurants serve Malabar biryani and seafood at modest local prices.
- **Local transport**: auto-rickshaws for the last leg from the highway or station are inexpensive; a taxi from Calicut airport runs higher.
- **Backwater boat**: negotiated locally and variable; agree the price before setting out.

Overall a day visit is low-cost; the main expense is transport to and from Kozhikode.

## Safety

- **Sea conditions**: the water is calm for most of the year (annual mean wave height about 0.34 m), but the **June–August monsoon** brings rough surf and rip risk, and swimming then is unsafe.
- **The headland rocks**: the rocky outcrop at one end of the beach has slippery rocks and stronger water movement — keep clear of it when swimming.
- **Lifeguards**: Kappad's Blue Flag status requires designated safety and first-aid provision, but do not assume a lifeguard is on duty outside busy daytime periods. Swim within your depth and avoid the water after dark.
- **Sun**: shade is scarce on the open sand; bring water, sunscreen, and a hat.

## Nearby

- **Kozhikode (Calicut) Beach**, in the city, has a long promenade, two old piers, and evening crowds.
- **SM Street (Mittai Theruvu)** in Kozhikode is the bazaar lane known for Kozhikodan halwa.
- **Korapuzha backwaters**, immediately behind Kappad, are worth a boat run for the river-and-coast scenery.
- **Koyilandy**, 10 km north, is a working fishing town and railhead with local markets.
