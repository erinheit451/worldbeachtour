# Alappuzha Beach Travel Guide

Alappuzha Beach is a short, walkable stop in a working Kerala port town, not a resort destination. The beach's own attraction is a 387-meter iron pier that Kerala's Maritime Board describes as dilapidated, with no cargo operations since 1989 — most visitors spend an hour or two here before moving on to the backwater houseboat trips the town is built around. Swimming is not the draw: the water runs deep close to shore with strong currents, and there are no lifeguards.

## Getting There

**By Air.** Cochin International Airport (COK) is the nearest major airport, 73.7 kilometers from Alappuzha Beach in a straight line. The actual road distance, via NH66, is closer to 75–83 kilometers and takes roughly 1.5–2 hours to drive, depending on traffic — the straight-line figure understates the real trip. Wikivoyage cites a KSRTC bus connection from Cochin for about ₹75, taking around 1 hour 45 minutes; taxis and ride-hailing services also run the route.

**By Rail.** Alappuzha Railway Station sits a few kilometers from the beach and is on the rail line connecting to Kochi (Ernakulam) and, further afield, Thiruvananthapuram. From the station, an auto-rickshaw is the practical way to reach the beach itself.

**By Road.** National Highway 66 (NH66) runs along the Kerala coast and passes through Alappuzha, connecting it to Kochi to the north and Thiruvananthapuram to the south. Part of the coastal route near the beach runs on the elevated Alappuzha Bypass — as of March 2025, a section of that bypass was still under construction, and four girders of an unfinished flyover near Vijay Beach Park collapsed that month. No injuries were reported, but it's worth expecting construction activity along that stretch.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Kerala's southwest monsoon runs roughly June through September, and both Wikivoyage and general Kerala guidance treat this as the period to avoid: rough seas, strong currents, and reduced visibility on top of an already-risky swimming environment. The Arabian Sea off this coast averages 1.1 meters of wave height annually, with July the roughest month at a 2.02-meter average and a recorded peak of 2.58 meters; December through February, by contrast, is the calm season, dropping to a 0.64-meter average in January.

Two specific events are worth timing around: the annual Alappuzha Beach Festival, held around New Year and drawing crowds from across the district, and, as a one-off rather than a fixture, Kerala's first international sand art festival, held on this beach on 26 April 2015. Outside those windows, the beach runs at ordinary local-town pace.

## Where to Stay

Alappuzha town has lodging within walking distance of the beach, and the wider area — particularly along the Vembanad Lake backwaters — has houseboat operators offering overnight stays on the water itself, which is the area's signature accommodation experience rather than a beachfront hotel. Because specific hotel and houseboat operator details weren't independently verified for this guide, check current listings and reviews directly rather than relying on any single recommendation.

## Safety

**Water.** Do not treat this as a swimming beach. Wikivoyage and local guidance both describe deep water close to shore and strong currents, with conditions worsening during and immediately after the June–September monsoon. There are no lifeguards. Recorded shark incidents here are zero, but that isn't the relevant hazard — currents and depth are.

**The pier.** Kerala's own Maritime Board lists the 1862 sea pier as dilapidated, with no port operations since 1989. It's walkable and is the beach's main landmark, but treat aged sections with caution.

**Construction.** Part of the Alappuzha Bypass near the beach was still under construction as of March 2025, when four girders of an unfinished flyover collapsed near Vijay Beach Park. No injuries were reported, and authorities ordered a structural inspection, but stay alert around active work zones.

**Cyclones.** The IBTrACS storm-track record shows 8 tropical cyclones passing within range of this coast over the past 50 years, with a maximum recorded intensity of Category 2 — infrequent by Bay of Bengal standards, but not zero.

## Local Tips

**Go for sunset, not sunrise.** This coast faces due west into the open Arabian Sea, so the pier and promenade fill with people at dusk, not dawn.

**The pier is the photo.** Its cast-iron ironwork against the evening sky is the beach's most consistent composition; a government listing calling your subject "dilapidated" is, if anything, part of the story.

**Camel rides are gone.** They were once offered on the sand but have since been banned by local authorities — don't expect to find them.

**Treat the beach as a prelude.** Most visitors use Alappuzha Beach as a stop of a few hours before boarding a houseboat into the Vembanad Lake backwaters, which is the area's main tourism draw and a separate trip in its own right.

**Regional food is inland, not beachfront.** Alleppey fish curry (a coconut-and-kudampuli-soured meen curry) and karimeen pollichathu (grilled pearl-spot fish) are the dishes associated with this coast, served at restaurants and toddy shops in town rather than at stalls on the sand.
