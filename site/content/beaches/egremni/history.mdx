# The Staircase Egremni Keeps Rebuilding

Egremni's history since the mid-1990s is really the history of one staircase: built, destroyed, rebuilt over six years, closed again, storm-damaged, and repaired a second time by people with no official authority to do so. Each phase left the beach a little further from the state that legally owns it.

## Before 2015: A Road, Then a Staircase

Egremni was a remote spot until a sealed road was built in the mid-1990s, connecting it to the rest of Lefkada and turning it into one of the island's premier tourist beaches — one Travel + Leisure would later name among "13 Places Where You Can See the Bluest Water in the World." Access to the beach itself, though, still ran through a mountain path of 347 steep steps, a serious descent with no shortcuts.

## 17 November 2015: The Earthquake

A 6.5-magnitude earthquake struck Lefkada on 17 November 2015. It destroyed Egremni's 347-step staircase and sent a flank of the limestone cliff collapsing onto the beach, burying the sunbathing terrace at its base under rockfall. Because the collapse happened in November, outside the swimming season, no one was on the sand when it hit — a matter of calendar timing rather than any lesser risk in summer.

The quake was not a minor event elsewhere on Lefkada. It killed two elderly women: a 69-year-old struck by a boulder that hit her house, and an 82-year-old killed by a collapsing wall. Egremni's own retelling of the earthquake tends to skip both the actual magnitude and this human cost, reducing a lethal 6.5-Mw event to a scenic backstory about a broken staircase.

## Rebuilding, Twice Delayed

The rebuild took nearly six years and moved in fits and starts. In November 2019, Lefkada mayor Charalambos Kalos announced that the road to Egremni would reopen for the coming tourist season, with construction targeted for completion by late February 2020. The COVID-19 pandemic then disrupted that timeline.

By June 2020, a specialized Slovenian aerial-work crew had arrived to install pre-assembled sections of a new staircase, with officials estimating another 30-45 days of work. At that point, road access to Egremni had already been closed for five consecutive summers running — every summer since the earthquake.

## Reopened for Summer 2021

Land access to Egremni was reported restored by 12 May 2021, roughly five and a half years after the earthquake, with about 420 new steps in place — considerably more than the original 347, reflecting a different, more gradual route down the rebuilt cliff face — and the beach reopened to visitors for the 2021 summer season. Local press described the renovation of the road and steps as an especially difficult job, primarily because of the safety demands of building infrastructure into an active, previously-collapsed cliff.

For a few years, Egremni functioned again as a normal, if strenuous, destination: descend the new staircase, or arrive by boat from Nydri or Vasiliki.

## Closed Again: The 2025 Order

That normalcy did not hold. Following a fresh geological risk assessment, the Municipality of Lefkada formally closed both the road and the staircase again, starting in 2025, citing severe ground instability. Unlike the 2015 closure, which followed an obvious, visible earthquake, this closure followed a study — a less dramatic trigger, but one the municipality judged serious enough to shut the beach's land access down again. No reopening has been announced; the closure itself is reported by the Vasiliki tourism portal vasiliki.eu rather than by a published municipal decision.

## The July 2025 Visit: A Covered Sign

What the closure order looked like in practice, at least to one visitor, surfaced in early July 2025. A visitor who went to Egremni that month reported that the sign prohibiting entry had been covered or hidden, and that a closed vehicle gate had a gap small enough to look official but wide enough for people to pass through. The same account describes a €10-per-vehicle charge collected by private individuals with no receipt or signage — an unofficial toll for a beach that, on paper, no one was supposed to be entering.

## February 2026: Storms and Rockfall

In late February 2026 (reported 25 February), heavy rain caused rockfall that damaged the staircase near its base and blocked the path, according to reports and tourist video from the scene. At the time, the staircase reportedly had gone roughly four years without maintenance or an engineering inspection by municipal personnel — meaning the 2021 rebuild had never been formally checked in the years since it reopened, closure order or not.

## July 2026: Repaired, But By Whom

By 1 July 2026, the fallen stones had been cleared and the bent railings straightened. The work was not done by the municipality. It was completed by what local reporting describes only as a private, non-municipal initiative — no engineer, agency, or named contractor has been credited or held accountable for it. There has been no official statement from the authorities on the safety or structural integrity of the repair. The Vasiliki tourism site vasiliki.eu instead recommends boat access from Vasiliki over the stairs, effectively declining to vouch for the very staircase visitors are still climbing down.

The pattern is not new. In August 2018, midway through the post-earthquake rebuild, Lefkada Today reported that the port authority was fielding calls from swimmers about earth and rubble falling onto the beach, while tour boats kept advertising Egremni in their brochures and stopping there even though the beach was officially closed for the works. Boat operators out of Vasiliki and Nydri still advertise and stop at Egremni today.

## What This Means If You're Going

Ten years after the earthquake that first destroyed it, Egremni's staircase has now failed and been informally patched twice — once by a six-year state rebuild, once by an unofficial private initiative with no engineering sign-off. The beach itself has not moved: it is still a pebble shore at the base of a dramatic cliff, still genuinely worth seeing. What has changed is who is responsible for the ground you'd be standing on to get there. As of mid-2026, that answer is: nobody, officially. The safest documented way to see Egremni is still the one the Vasiliki tourism site vasiliki.eu recommends — by water, not by stairs.
