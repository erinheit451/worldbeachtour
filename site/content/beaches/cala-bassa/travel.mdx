# Cala Bassa Travel Guide

Cala Bassa has no on-site accommodation and, outside the summer bus season, no public transport connection — the Sant Josep council's own tourism listing describes access as car, seasonal bus, or bicycle. Visitors base themselves in Sant Antoni de Portmany, the nearest town, and reach the beach by road, by the seasonal municipal bus, or by the Cruceros Portmany boat that sails from Sant Antoni harbor.

## Getting There

<DataCard label="Nearest town" value="Sant Antoni de Portmany" unit="10.9 km by road / 19 min" />

**By air.** Ibiza Airport (IBZ) is the only airport on the island. The beach dataset's own distance figure, 15.6 km, is a straight-line measurement; the road distance is 24.5 km, about 41 minutes by car via the coast road through or around Sant Antoni. There is no direct airport bus to Cala Bassa; the practical routes are a rental car, a taxi, or a transfer to Sant Antoni first.

**By car.** The coast road from Sant Antoni de Portmany, or the inland route via Sant Agustí des Vedrà, both reach Cala Bassa; the Sant Josep council lists both as the standard approach. There is parking at the beach, associated with the restaurants and beach club on-site — the council's own listing doesn't specify a separate paid lot, and capacity is not published.

**By bus.** A seasonal public bus runs to Cala Bassa in summer, with a stop about 50 meters from the sand, per the Sant Josep tourism office. Outside the summer season, this service does not run, and there is no year-round public transit option to the beach.

**By boat.** Cruceros Portmany, based in Sant Antoni harbor, runs a Cala Bassa route as one of its scheduled excursions, alongside its Formentera, Cala Conta, and Es Vedrà routes. Per the operator's own site, the Cala Bassa boat departs at 10:30, 12:00, and 14:00, with returns at 13:00, 16:00, and 18:00, running daily except Sundays from May through October. Fares aren't published on a fixed basis and should be checked against the operator's current listing before booking.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Ibiza's climate, per 30-year averages (1991–2010) recorded at the island's airport, runs from a January high of 15.8°C (60.4°F) to an August high of 30.4°C (86.7°F). Annual rainfall averages 402 mm, concentrated in the winter months, against roughly 2,700–2,800 hours of sunshine a year. Sea temperature averages 19.7°C (67°F) across the year, ranging from about 14.3°C in February to 26.2°C in August, with beach-going conditions generally running May through November.

The beach dataset's own wave model (Open-Meteo Marine, ERA5 reanalysis, 2023–2024) puts Cala Bassa's calmest month as July, with a mean significant wave height of 0.62 m, and its roughest as January, at 1.34 m mean and 2.4 m on its biggest day; the dataset's largest single day of the year, 2.46 m, falls in November — a reminder that "calm cove" is a summer description; the same water is livelier outside the swimming season. Public buses to the beach, and the Cruceros Portmany boat service, both run on a summer schedule; visiting outside roughly May–October means arriving by car with no seasonal transport backup.

## Where to Stay

Cala Bassa has no beachfront accommodation. Sant Antoni de Portmany, 10.9 km away by road, is the nearest town and the practical base — a former fishing village (from the Roman-era harbor of Portus Magnus) that grew into a major package-tourism resort from the late 1950s onward, per Wikipedia's history of the town, with a 2012-recorded population of 22,446. It has its own beach, a working harbor, and the West End and Sunset Strip bar districts (home to Café del Mar and Café Mambo), none of which are at Cala Bassa itself. Specific hotel and rental listings and rates were not verified for this guide and change seasonally; check current listings directly rather than relying on fixed price figures.

## Safety and Access

<DataCard label="Recorded shark incidents" value="0" unit="beach dataset" />

The beach has a lifeguard in summer season only, per the Sant Josep tourism office, which separately confirms the beach is the site of regular lifeguard rescue drills and has an operating rescue team with boats. Outside July–August, or off-season, there is no rescue cover. The beach has restrooms and a wheelchair-accessible ramp to the sand, but no showers, per the same council listing.

The water immediately off the beach carries a protected Posidonia oceanica seagrass meadow; anchoring on it is illegal under a 2018 Balearic decree, with fines up to €450,000 for destroying a meadow, and regional patrols cited nine boats in a single sweep across Sant Antoni Bay and Cala Bassa in August 2025 — the reporting doesn't break down how many were at Cala Bassa itself. Swimmers and snorkelers are not the target of that law — boats are — but it shapes the anchorage close to shore. Full detail is in the [environment deep-dive](/beaches/cala-bassa/environment).

## Local Tips

**The bus only runs in season.** If visiting outside the summer window, plan on a car; there is no year-round public transport link to the beach.

**The snorkel route on the rocky west margin is rated "advanced."** The Sant Josep tourism office's own guidance recommends carrying a marker buoy there, given the boat traffic that also uses the anchorage.

**Distances quoted elsewhere are often straight-line.** The beach dataset lists 5.7 km to Sant Antoni and 15.6 km to Ibiza Airport; those are straight-line figures. The road distances used in this guide, 10.9 km and 24.5 km, come from routing rather than the direct-line dataset value.

**Don't expect a sunset over the water.** The cove faces north, so the classic Ibiza sunset happens elsewhere on the island; late afternoon here lights the limestone slope and pines above the sand instead.
