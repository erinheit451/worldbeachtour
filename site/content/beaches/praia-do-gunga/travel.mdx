# Praia do Gunga Travel Guide

Praia do Gunga is a sand spit in Roteiro, Alagoas, reached by boat across a tidal lagoon or by a road that crosses private land — there is no public route straight to it. That access pattern shapes every practical decision about visiting: whether you go by water or by land, and where you base yourself, matters more here than at a beach with a normal highway frontage.

## Getting There

**By Air**

Zumbi dos Palmares International Airport (MCZ) in Maceió is the practical gateway. The dataset behind this page lists it at 40.7 km from the beach — but that figure is a straight-line distance, not a driving distance. Routed by road, the trip runs about 64 km and 68 minutes, because the coastal route bends inland around the Lagoa do Roteiro system before the final approach. Budget more than an hour, not the 30–40 minutes a straight-line number might suggest.

**By Car**

From central Maceió, the drive is roughly 43 km and about 44 minutes by road. From Marechal Deodoro, the closer town, it's about 29 km and roughly half an hour — again, notably more than the 16.8 km straight-line distance between the two points. The final stretch of the land route crosses private property — the Fazenda Gunga, which the Ministério Público Federal's 2017 recommendation names as controlled by ex-mayor Nivaldo Jatobá and his company, Nivaldo Jatobá – Empreendimentos Agroindustriais LTDA. There is no public through-road to the beach and no roadside parking; the complex's parking lot charges a fee, and under the 2025 TAC at least 20% of that lot's spaces must be kept free for reduced-mobility access.

**By Boat**

Most visitors without a car cross the Lagoa do Roteiro by boat from Barra de São Miguel's marina, a trip of roughly fifteen to twenty minutes. This is the more commonly used route in practice, not a fallback — Barra de São Miguel functions as the real staging town for Gunga, even though the beach itself is administratively part of Roteiro, not Barra.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Alagoas' southern coast runs a dry season roughly September through March and a wetter season April through August, standard for Brazil's Northeast. Beyond season, tide matters more than most beaches: the wave dataset behind this page puts the annual mean wave height at 1.46 m, climbing toward a June peak of 1.8 m (with a recorded peak-day figure of 2.2 m) and easing to about 1.18 m in February. June is the roughest month on the ocean side; February is the calmest. The lagoon side is comparatively sheltered year-round by the geography of the spit itself, not by any seasonal pattern.

## Where to Stay

There is limited lodging directly at the beach itself; the complex documented in 2017, 14 bar-restaurants, 18 food stalls, 32 craft stalls, is a day-use commercial operation, not an accommodation zone. Most visitors base themselves in one of two places:

**Barra de São Miguel** — the marina town across the lagoon, and the practical staging point for boat crossings to Gunga. This is where day-to-day tourist infrastructure concentrates.

**Maceió** — the state capital, roughly 43 km / 44 minutes north by road, with the airport, the widest range of accommodation, and the option to day-trip to Gunga by car or boat tour.

No specific hotels, pousadas, or rental operators at either location are independently verified in the sources used for this page; check current listings directly rather than relying on unverified names.

## Costs

<DataCard label="Beach access" value="Parking fee charged at the complex lot" unit="no roadside parking on the access road" />

Specific prices for boat crossings, food-stall meals, or craft purchases are not independently verified in available sources for this page. What is documented is that the drive-in route funnels through the complex's paid parking lot — with no roadside parking on the access road, paying that fee is in practice the price of arriving by car, though the amount itself isn't verified here; the 2025 TAC requires at least 20% of the lower lot's spaces to be kept free for reduced-mobility access. Local press covering this same TAC (Alagoas 24 Horas, April 2025) described that clause more broadly, as a free-public-access guarantee rather than a reduced-mobility-specific one; the TAC's own wording, as cited here, is narrower. Beyond that, what's documented is the scale of the local economy: a 2017 count of 64 individual bar-restaurant, food-stall and craft-stall operations, drawing an estimated 5,000 visitors monthly at peak season. Expect an informal, cash-oriented market rather than a metered resort strip, and confirm prices on arrival.

## Safety

**Water conditions.** The Atlantic-facing side of the peninsula carries a real rip current, flagged directly in the wave dataset behind this page, alongside a genuine ocean swell (annual mean 1.46 m). The Lagoa do Roteiro side is calm and shallow by comparison, sheltered by the spit's own geography, and is the better choice for children or uncertain swimmers.

**No lifeguards.** There is no lifeguard service documented at Praia do Gunga. When a speedboat lost power and struck rocks near the beach on 11 September 2017, injuring six people including two children, the rescue was carried out by jangadeiros — independent traditional raft fishermen working nearby — not by any official emergency service. On 1 December 2018, a 60-year-old man drowned at the beach; Fire Department, ambulance and helicopter crews responded but could not revive him, and the cause was never established in available reporting.

**Wildlife.** The peninsula sits inside an active ecological corridor. In January 2018, beachgoers found a broad-snouted caiman over two meters long on the sand; the species had been locally extinct in Alagoas for roughly two decades before a breeding facility helped reestablish it, and individuals occasionally move toward the coast during the November–March breeding season. This is not a reason to avoid the beach, but it is a reason not to treat the water as a managed swimming pool.

**Access itself.** Because the only land route crosses private property and the only water route depends on boat availability from Barra de São Miguel, plan the return trip as carefully as the arrival — there is no guaranteed public transport option waiting at the beach after dark.

## Local Tips

**Confirm your access route before you book.** Boat from Barra de São Miguel or road through private land are different trips with different logistics; don't assume either is available on demand.

**Choose your side of the spit deliberately.** Ocean side for surf and open-water photography, lagoon side for calm, shallow water — they are genuinely different conditions on the same beach.

**Bring cash.** The beach complex is a dense but informal market; card acceptance at individual stalls is not documented and shouldn't be assumed.

**Time the tide, not just the light.** The shallow pools and sand flats that make Gunga's lagoon side distinctive are a function of tide level, not time of day — check tide timing separately from sunrise and sunset if that's what you're visiting for.

**Treat the private-land access as exactly that.** It's not a public highway; drive accordingly and don't assume year-round, unrestricted availability.
