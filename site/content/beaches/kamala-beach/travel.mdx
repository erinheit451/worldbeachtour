# Kamala Beach Travel Guide

Kamala sits on Phuket's west coast, a quieter resort base than Patong to its south, built over what was a fishing village into the early 1990s. The practical anchor at its north end, Phuket FantaSea, is worth checking the current schedule for before planning around it — by 2026 its flagship show had been cut to three nights a week rather than nightly, one visible sign of the financial restructuring its operating company, Phuket FantaSea Public Company Limited, has been managing since the pandemic.

**By air.** Phuket International Airport (HKT) is the practical gateway. The beach dataset's 17.8 km figure is straight-line distance; the actual driving route, via Highway 402 (Thepkrasattri Road) and coastal roads, runs closer to 25 km and takes roughly 30 minutes depending on traffic. Taxis, pre-booked private transfers, and rental cars all cover the route; no rail or scheduled ferry service reaches Kamala directly.

**From Patong.** Patong lies about 10 km south by road (6.9 km straight-line). Songthaews — shared open-back trucks that are Phuket's main local transit — run the coastal route, along with metered taxis and ride-hailing apps where available.

## Best Time to Visit

Phuket runs on the same two-season pattern as the rest of Thailand's Andaman coast: a dry season roughly November through April, and a wet, rougher southwest-monsoon season roughly May through October. The beach's own wave data backs this up — annual mean wave height is 0.73 m, but September is the roughest month (1.24 m mean, up to a recorded 2.12 m), while March is calmest (0.40 m mean). The same dataset counts seven tropical cyclone tracks passing within range of Kamala over 50 years, with peak intensity equivalent to Category 3, concentrated in the same wet-season window.

## Where to Stay

Kamala's beachfront is lined with resorts, and the inland village has smaller, more local accommodation options. South of the main beach, a dead-end lane locally nicknamed Millionaire Row runs past Rayee and Hua beaches to a cluster of five-star properties, including Paresa Resort and the Hyatt Regency Phuket, on coral-fringed water that's difficult for casual swimming. Patong, about 10 km south by road, has Phuket's widest range of accommodation and nightlife if Kamala's quieter pace isn't the goal.

## What's There

**Phuket FantaSea**, at Kamala's north end, is the beach's largest built landmark: a 60-acre theme park that opened in 1998, with a 3,000-seat theater for its "Fantasy of a Kingdom" show and a 4,000-seat buffet restaurant. As of a 2026 schedule check, the show runs three nights a week — Tuesday, Friday, and Sunday — down from a historically closer-to-nightly run, alongside a multi-year bond restructuring its operating company, Phuket FantaSea Public Company Limited, has been managing since the pandemic. Confirm the current schedule before building an evening around it.

**Kamala village**, inland from the beach, is organized around the marble-fronted Kamala Mosque on Khok Yang street and hosts an informal evening market selling street food and produce. Sources disagree on exactly which weeknights the market runs — treat any specific schedule as approximate and confirm locally.

## Safety

**Water conditions vary by season.** The wave and cyclone data above point the same direction as general Andaman-coast guidance: conditions are calmer in the November–April dry season and rougher, with a higher tropical-cyclone risk, from roughly May through October. Local advice and any posted flag or lifeguard warnings should take precedence over seasonal generalities on any given day.

**Tsunami history.** Kamala was hit by the 26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami; a surviving video from the beach shows a restaurant's ground floor flooding and sweeping away an elderly couple. Thailand's overall death toll that day was roughly 8,000, concentrated mostly around Khao Lak rather than Phuket's resort beaches. Tsunami-evacuation signage is part of the standard infrastructure along Phuket's west coast beaches generally, Kamala included.

## Local Notes

**Respect the two communities sharing the beach.** Kamala's inland core is a longstanding Muslim fishing village; dress and behavior expectations there differ from the beachfront resort strip. The Kamala Mosque on Khok Yang street is a working place of worship, not a tourist site.

**Millionaire Row is a nickname, not signage.** The lane south of the main beach serving Paresa Resort and the Hyatt Regency Phuket isn't officially marked that way — it's travel-writer shorthand that's stuck.

**Check FantaSea's schedule before you plan around it.** A park built for a near-nightly show now runs three evenings a week; verify current days and hours rather than assuming year-round nightly performances.
