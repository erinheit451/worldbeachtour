Kamala Beach sits on Phuket's west coast, a subdistrict the Kathu district registry counted at 5,459 residents across six villages as of 31 March 2004. The beach itself runs roughly 2 kilometers by one common estimate (Phuket101) to 3 kilometers by another (Wikipedia) — sources disagree and the discrepancy hasn't been resolved in public records. The name comes from Sanskrit and Pali kamalā, "lotus."

Before it was a resort strip, it was a fishing village. Travel accounts from around 1990 describe buffaloes on the sand and fishermen drying small fish on tarps behind the beach, with a scatter of thatched huts, a school, and a temple. The first hotels appeared around 1994. That older village survives inland today, organized around the marble-fronted Kamala Mosque on Khok Yang street, still described in tourism and real-estate sources as a Muslim fishing community distinct from the beachfront development.

## What Anchors the Beach

The single largest structure at Kamala is Phuket FantaSea, a 60-acre nighttime theme park built at the beach's north end in 1998 for more than 3,500 million baht (roughly $100 million). Its "Fantasy of a Kingdom" show seats 3,000 in a reproduction Sukhothai-era elephant theater; its Golden Kinnaree Buffet Restaurant seats up to 4,000. The park survived the 26 December 2004 tsunami — which reached Kamala and, on video, showed a beachfront restaurant's ground floor flooding and sweeping away an elderly couple — closing for just ten days. It has had a harder time recently: its parent company, Safari World Public Company, spent 2024 restructuring six bond series through Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission, and by 2026 the flagship show had been cut back to three nights a week, Tuesday, Friday, and Sunday, instead of a near-nightly run.

South of the main sand, a dead-end lane nicknamed Millionaire Row runs past Rayee and Hua beaches to private villas and five-star resorts — Paresa Resort and the Hyatt Regency Phuket among them — where coral makes casual swimming difficult.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History** — The compressed, well-dated arc from 1990s fishing village to 1998 theme park to a 2024 securities filing is unusual for how thoroughly it's documented in public records rather than tourism marketing.

**Travel** — Kamala functions as a quieter resort base than Patong, with its own beach, hotel row, and FantaSea as the after-dark draw — now on a reduced schedule worth checking before a visit is planned around it.

## Practical Context

Kamala is not a wilderness beach or an untouched fishing village; it's a place where both those things coexist with a financially strained theme park and a resort strip built on reclaimed fishing-village land. None of that makes it less real — it makes the layers worth naming rather than smoothing over.
