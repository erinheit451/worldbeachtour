Sunset Beach is an urban beach park on English Bay, in Vancouver's West End neighbourhood, part of the city's seawall system and bisected by a multi-use path connecting it to the adjacent Vancouver Aquatic Centre and the Burrard Street Bridge. In the Squamish language it carries the name Í7iy̓el̓shn, meaning "soft underfoot."

The park is city-maintained and includes public washrooms, concession stands, an off-leash area for dogs, and a False Creek Ferries dock. Lifeguards are posted between May and September; outside that window, swimmers are on their own. The beach sits at the edge of Vancouver's West End and downtown core, making it a walk-to beach for a large share of the city rather than a destination requiring transit.

**Who goes.** This is a working city beach, not a resort strip. It draws lunch-break swimmers and dog-walkers from the dense high-rise West End, cyclists and skaters using the seawall as a through-route, and, on specific dates, much larger crowds: an estimated 40,000 people turned out for the 2017 edition of the unofficial April 20 cannabis rally the beach has hosted for years, and it is one of the region's best vantage points for Vancouver's summer fireworks over English Bay.

**What's actually here.** Two structures anchor the park beyond the sand itself. The Vancouver Aquatic Centre, next door, opened in 1974 on the site of the old Crystal Pool and permanently closed on June 28, 2026 after 52 years, ahead of a smaller, $175 million replacement. And the Sunset Beach AIDS Memorial, a ribbon of steel panels bearing the names of British Columbians who died of AIDS, was unveiled here in December 2004 after an eight-year process delayed by bureaucracy and, at points, open homophobia. Both are covered in more depth in the [cultural history of Sunset Beach](/beaches/sunset-beach-16/culture).

Sunset Beach also had a stint as an accidental celebrity. A 61-metre barge broke loose in regional floods and washed ashore here on November 15, 2021; it stayed, memed and photographed, for the better part of a year before a delayed, $2.4 million removal wrapped up in late 2022.

Do not confuse this beach with the unrelated Vancouver neighbourhood called "Sunset," with the Summer Village of Sunset Beach elsewhere in British Columbia, or with any of the several other Sunset Beaches in California, Hawaii, North Carolina, or Ontario.
