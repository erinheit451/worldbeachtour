# Cable Beach Travel Guide

Cable Beach is Nassau's main resort strip, fronting West Bay Street on New Providence's north shore. Most visitors base themselves at one of the properties directly on the sand — Baha Mar or Sandals Royal Bahamian — rather than exploring the beach independently, since the frontage is split between resort-controlled sections and public access points with no single formal entrance.

## Getting There

### By Air

Nearly all visitors arrive through **Lynden Pindling International Airport (NAS)**, the Bahamas' main international gateway. In a straight line the airport sits about 7.5 km from Cable Beach, but the actual driving route via West Bay Street covers roughly 9 km (5.5 miles) and takes 12-15 minutes.

### Ground Transportation from NAS

**Taxis** are available at the arrivals terminal; Nassau taxis run on official fixed zone rates rather than meters, and the NAS-to-Cable-Beach fare is $22 for the first two passengers plus $4 per additional passenger. The 5.5-mile route takes 12-15 minutes regardless of whether you take a taxi, drive, or use a rideshare app.

**Rental cars** are available from major counters in the terminal. Driving is straightforward along the airport road and West Bay Street, though the Bahamas drives on the left.

**Resort shuttles** are offered by some properties for guests; confirm availability when booking, since not every Cable Beach hotel runs one.

## Where to Stay

Cable Beach's hotel stock is concentrated in a small number of large properties rather than a spread of independent hotels.

**Baha Mar** is the dominant complex: three hotel brands — Grand Hyatt, SLS, and Rosewood — sharing 1,000 acres, 2,200 rooms total, a 100,000-square-foot casino, and a Jack Nicklaus-designed golf course. The three brands sit at different price and service tiers within the same property; the Grand Hyatt opened first, in April 2017, followed by SLS that November and Rosewood in June 2018. Room rates vary by brand and season — check directly with the resort rather than relying on a fixed range here.

**Sandals Royal Bahamian** is an adults-only, all-inclusive resort on the western end of Cable Beach, part of the Sandals chain's broader Caribbean portfolio.

The all-inclusive **Meliá Nassau Beach**, which occupied the site of the former Sheraton Nassau Beach Resort, closed in 2021 and was demolished by Baha Mar in 2024; it is not currently bookable. The cleared site is slated to become Baha Mar's fourth hotel, planned to open in 2029.

Independent, non-resort accommodation directly on Cable Beach is limited; travelers seeking budget options or a more local atmosphere generally look toward downtown Nassau instead, about 8 km (10-12 min) east by road (6.4 km straight-line) along West Bay Street.

## Best Time to Visit

The Bahamas has two broad seasons. **December through April** is the drier, more temperate stretch and the peak tourist season — expect the fullest resorts and highest demand around the winter holidays. **June through November** is hurricane season: New Providence has recorded 18 tropical cyclone tracks within 50 nautical miles over the past 50 years, including at least one Category 5 storm, per the NOAA IBTrACS archive. Direct hurricane hits on Nassau itself are not annual events, but the season affects flight schedules, resort operations, and travel insurance considerations throughout the June–November window.

## Safety

**Hurricane exposure** is the dominant weather risk; travelers visiting June through November should carry travel insurance that covers storm disruption and monitor National Hurricane Center advisories during their stay.

**Water safety**: the beach's shallow, current-sheltered water on New Providence's lee side is generally calm, but there is no public lifeguard service outside individual resort beach clubs — resort guests are covered by their property's beach staff; visitors at public access points are not.

**General safety**: Cable Beach and its resort district are well-trafficked and generally safe for visitors during the day; standard precautions around valuables and late-night walks away from lit, populated areas apply as they would in any resort district.

## Local Tips

**The beach has no single entrance.** Frontage is split between Baha Mar, Sandals Royal Bahamian, the demolished former Meliá Nassau Beach site (awaiting a planned 2029 Baha Mar hotel), and public access points; non-resort-guests should look for the public access points off West Bay Street rather than trying to walk through a resort property.

**The sand isn't ordinary beach sand.** It's oolitic aragonite — round carbonate grains that form directly in the surrounding warm water — rather than crushed quartz, which is why it stays notably fine and pale.

**Cable Beach faces north, not west.** Unlike waterfronts that face open ocean sunsets, Cable Beach's sunset plays out over the island behind the resorts rather than out over the water — plan golden-hour photography accordingly.

**Arawak Cay's "Fish Fry"** is the closest concentrated local food scene, a cluster of seafood shacks on West Bay Street about a 10-minute drive east toward downtown, known for conch salad and cracked conch — genuinely local food, not resort dining.

**If you're a college basketball fan**, Baha Mar's Convention Center has hosted the annual Bahamas Championship, a preseason NCAA Division I tournament, every year since 2021, typically during Thanksgiving week.

**The Emerald Beach Hotel's original casino**, opened in 1954 and described as the largest in the Bahamas at the time, sat on a site that was later redeveloped as the Crystal Palace Casino, which closed in February 2015; the Wyndham/Crystal Palace towers were demolished in October 2018, and today's Baha Mar Casino is new construction.
