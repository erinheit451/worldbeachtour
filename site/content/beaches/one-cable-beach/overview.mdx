Cable Beach runs about four kilometers along Nassau's north shore on New Providence Island, taking its name from a submarine telegraph cable that came ashore at Goodman's Bay, near the beach's eastern end, in 1892 — the first cable connecting the Bahamas to the United States, landed from Jupiter, Florida. Tourism development began in the 1940s and accelerated through the 1950s, when the strip earned the nickname the Bahamian Riviera; the Emerald Beach Hotel opened here in 1954 as the first air-conditioned hotel in the Bahamas.

The beach's modern character is dominated by Baha Mar, a 1,000-acre, roughly $4 billion resort complex — Grand Hyatt, SLS, and Rosewood hotels, 2,200 rooms, a 100,000-square-foot casino — built between 2011 and 2018 through a construction dispute that ended in a New York court ordering the project's original Chinese state contractor to pay $1.6 billion in fraud damages. That history sits underneath a beach that, on the sand itself, looks like an ordinary developed Caribbean resort strip: pale, fine sand made of oolitic aragonite rather than crushed quartz, calm current-sheltered water on New Providence's lee shore, and a mix of resort-controlled frontage and public access points along West Bay Street.

**Most Relevant Lenses:**

**History** — The 1892 telegraph cable landing named the beach; the 1950s "Bahamian Riviera" hotel boom, its post-independence government ownership, and the decade-long Baha Mar construction saga that ended in a 2024 fraud verdict all sit on the same four kilometers of sand.

**Travel & Recreation** — Cable Beach is a full resort destination: three Baha Mar hotel brands and Sandals Royal Bahamian concentrate restaurants, water sports, and organized activity along the strip, a short drive from Lynden Pindling International Airport. The former Meliá Nassau Beach, demolished in 2024, is slated to become Baha Mar's fourth hotel in 2029.

**Economics** — Baha Mar's $2.45 billion China Exim Bank financing, its 70-percent-Chinese construction workforce, and the 2024 New York fraud ruling against China Construction America make Cable Beach a case study in cross-border resort development going wrong before it went right.

**Family** — Calm, shallow, current-sheltered water and resort-based activities make the beach usable for young children, though there is no public lifeguard service outside individual resort beach clubs.

Cable Beach today functions as Nassau's primary hospitality strip, built and rebuilt across seventy years of hotel booms, government ownership, foreign financing, and — most recently — a fraud verdict that took nine years to arrive.
