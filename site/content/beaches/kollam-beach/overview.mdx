Kollam Beach, also called Mahatma Gandhi Beach, sits at Thamarakulam in Kollam city, on Kerala's Arabian Sea coast, over 2,300 meters long and adjoining Mahatma Gandhi Park. It is a working port city's beach as much as a visitor's one: Kollam Port sits nearby, one of the oldest ports on the Malabar Coast for the international cashew trade, and the Tangasseri breakwater -- extending about 1.8 kilometers south-southeast of Tangasseri Point -- protects the harbor just along the shore.

The beach's identity is layered by history. Kollam, once known as Quilon, was a favoured settlement of the Portuguese, Dutch and English in succession before Indian independence, the Portuguese arriving first in the 16th century. Ruins of Portuguese and Dutch forts and 18th-century churches remain near the port as reminders of that period, and the 144-foot Tangasseri Lighthouse, built in 1902, still marks the harbor entrance. Mahatma Gandhi Park, next to the beach, was inaugurated by then vice president of India Zakir Husain -- 1 January 1961, according to the English and Tamil Wikipedia articles, though the Malayalam-language article instead dates the same inauguration to 1967, a discrepancy no source here resolves.

Who comes here reflects that split identity. Kollam Beach is described as Kerala's first "beach wedding destination," a claim traced to a 2016 ceremony organized by Jo and Company, event managers from Thiruvananthapuram, and sunset visits cluster around the park end of the beach. Local legend also credits Lord Parasurama, the sixth incarnation of Vishnu, with throwing his axe into the sea to reclaim Kerala's coastal land, Kollam included -- a story told about this stretch of coast rather than a documented event. Fishing continues alongside all of this: Kollam Port's cashew-trade history and its working boats are visible from the sand, not tucked away from it.

The beach carries a high hazard rating and is patrolled by lifeguards -- the fuller safety story, including a 2023 redesign plan that followed a documented drowning toll, is covered in the [history deep dive](/beaches/kollam-beach/history). For a first visit, Kollam Beach reads less as a resort and more as a genuine working coastline: a port, a lighthouse, a disputed park anniversary, and a beach that has picked up, almost by accident, a reputation as a wedding backdrop. None of that softens the other fact sitting beside it, which is that this is also a beach the state itself has called dangerous enough to warrant an engineering fix.
