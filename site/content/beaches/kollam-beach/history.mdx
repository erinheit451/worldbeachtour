# The Beach With Three Wikipedia Articles That Disagree

Kollam Beach, in the Indian state of Kerala, is fronted by a park whose own founding year depends on which language edition of Wikipedia you read. The English and Tamil articles say Mahatma Gandhi Park, next to the beach, was inaugurated on 1 January 1961 by then vice president of India Zakir Husain. The Malayalam article -- the language of the region the park sits in -- instead dates the same inauguration, by the same man, to 1967. No source behind this page resolves which year is right, and that unresolved conflict is a useful way into the beach's layered, contested history.

## A port before it was a beach

Kollam -- once known as Quilon -- was a working port long before anyone thought of its shore as a beach. It was a favoured settlement of the Portuguese, Dutch and English in succession before Indian independence, the Portuguese arriving first in the 16th century, followed by the Dutch and then the English. Ruins of Portuguese and Dutch forts and 18th-century churches remain near the port today as mementoes of that colonial period, clustered around Tangasseri.

Kollam Port itself is one of the oldest and most important ports on the Malabar Coast for the international cashew trade. Its current standing is another point of disagreement between sources: an English Wikipedia account ranks it third largest in Kerala as of 2025, behind Trivandrum and Cochin, while a Tamil Wikipedia account instead ranks it second largest, after Kochi, per a 2010 estimate. The two figures are fifteen years and one rank apart, and nothing in the record here reconciles them -- only that the port has mattered to the regional economy for generations either way.

The port is protected by the Tangasseri breakwater, which extends about 1.8 kilometers south-southeast of Tangasseri Point. At that point stands the Tangasseri Lighthouse, a 144-foot (44-meter) structure built in 1902, still a working landmark and the beach's most photographed fixed point after the water itself.

## A park that has been rebuilt at least once

Mahatma Gandhi Park's disputed 1961-or-1967 opening was not its last chapter. At its original inauguration, the park complex included a deer park and an aquarium of its own -- amenities that read as ambitious for a mid-century municipal park. Lack of maintenance let it decline: a 2011 newspaper account describes it as having deteriorated into a haven for anti-socials, a plain-spoken way of saying the park had stopped being a place anyone wanted to be. That year, a five-year maintenance contract refurbished the park, with entry set at Rs. 5 per person and parking at Rs. 5 for two-wheelers and Rs. 10 for cars -- the beach itself remained free. Those prices are now well over a decade old and should not be read as current.

The park's original aquarium is not the only one the record mentions. In June 2014, construction began on a marine aquarium at Kollam Beach's eastern side, built by the Harbour Engineering Department on behalf of Kollam Municipal Corporation and billed as the first of its kind in the state of Kerala. The foundation stone had been laid in March 2014, with completion expected by December of that year: a single-storey building planned to hold 40 large tanks of marine life. No source available here confirms whether that aquarium was ever finished, opened to the public, or is operating today. It is a project that appears in the record with a start date and an expected finish date, and then simply stops appearing.

## What the layered history adds up to

None of this is presented here to flatten Kollam Beach into a single story. It is a port town's beach, sitting beside a park whose own founding year its neighboring language editions of Wikipedia cannot agree on, next to fort ruins from three different colonial powers, below a lighthouse that has outlasted all of them, in front of a port whose modern ranking two of the same encyclopedia's editions also can't agree on. The pattern across all of it is the same: real, sourced, specific claims sitting next to real, sourced, unresolved contradictions. That is not a flaw in the research; it is what an honest look at a place with this much layered history actually turns up. A beach that has been rebuilt, re-ranked, and re-dated by its own sources more than once is still, underneath all of that, the same stretch of Arabian Sea coast it always was.
