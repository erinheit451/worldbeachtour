# L'Espiguette Travel Guide

L'Espiguette is the undeveloped end of the beach system east of Le Grau-du-Roi, in the Gard department of southern France — no train station, no beach clubs, and a single unpaved parking area at the end of the only paved access road. Getting here takes more effort than reaching the resort beaches of the nearby Languedoc coast, and that effort is largely the point: it's what has kept 18 kilometers of dune and open sand undeveloped while towns a few kilometers away built marinas and hotel strips.

## Getting There

**By Air.** Montpellier–Méditerranée Airport (MPL) is the nearest airport, 15.7 km from the beach as the crow flies, per this page's underlying dataset. That straight-line figure understates the actual trip: routing the drive over the real road network (A9 motorway, then the D62B/D979 through Le Grau-du-Roi) comes out to roughly 30 km and about 35 minutes, according to OpenStreetMap-based routing. A smaller regional field, Nîmes–Alès–Camargue Airport, sits further off to the north with more limited schedules; Nîmes itself, reachable by train, is about 55 km from the beach by road (roughly 50 minutes' drive).

**By Car.** From Montpellier, take the A9 east/southeast, then follow signs for Le Grau-du-Roi on the D62B/D979. From Nîmes, the D6113 runs south through Aigues-Mortes before reaching Le Grau-du-Roi. From either direction, the final approach to the beach itself is the RD255, the single paved road serving L'Espiguette's main parking area. Parking is unpaved and paid from 1 April to 30 September (2026; a car runs EUR 7/day, reduced to EUR 4 after 16:30; a motorcycle EUR 3; a camper van EUR 15; pedestrians and cyclists park free), per Le Grau-du-Roi's tourist office. It fills by mid-morning in July and August, though the beach is long enough that space rarely runs out entirely — it's the closest spots that go first.

**By Public Transit.** TER Occitanie runs year-round trains from Nîmes to Le Grau-du-Roi, with more frequent service in summer; travelers from Montpellier normally change trains at Nîmes to reach the line. From the Le Grau-du-Roi station, there is no dedicated shuttle to L'Espiguette itself; visitors without a car typically walk, cycle (the RD255 has a cycle path added in its 2011–2013 renovation), or take a taxi the remaining distance — about 6.8 km by road from central Le Grau-du-Roi to the point, versus the 4.3 km straight-line distance in this page's dataset.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Wave data for this stretch of coast (Open-Meteo Marine, ERA5 reanalysis, 2023–2024) puts the annual mean wave height at 0.89 m, with March the roughest month (1.25 m mean, up to a recorded 2.78 m) and August the calmest (0.59 m mean) — broadly consistent with the region's summer-calm, shoulder-season-livelier Mediterranean pattern. The dominant regional wind is the mistral, which blows down the Rhône valley and can raise chop with little warning; it is a distinct wind from the tramontane further west toward Roussillon, and forecasts are worth checking regardless of season.

July and August bring the warmest water and the largest crowds at the RD255 access point, though the beach's length means crowding is concentrated near parking rather than spread evenly. Spring and autumn bring cooler water, thinner crowds, and more consistent light for photography of the dune system. Winter is when the coast's actual character, wind-driven, empty, and geologically active, is most visible; there is no seasonal closure, but there are also no services.

## Where to Stay

L'Espiguette itself has no accommodation. The closest base is Le Grau-du-Roi, a working fishing town turned resort (8,588 residents in 2023) about 4.3 km away in a straight line, with a mix of hotels and vacation rentals and an active harbor. Port Camargue, the marina adjoining Le Grau-du-Roi and begun in 1968 as part of the state's Mission Racine coastal development program, offers marina-adjacent apartments geared toward sailing visitors. Aigues-Mortes, the 13th-century walled town about 15 km inland, offers smaller hotels and guesthouses inside its ramparts and is a destination in its own right. La Grande-Motte, roughly 20 km west, is a larger, more built-up resort town with more accommodation volume.

## Costs

Beach access at L'Espiguette is free, but the main RD255 parking lot is paid from 1 April to 30 September (2026; EUR 7/day for a car, reduced to EUR 4 after 16:30; EUR 3 for a motorcycle; EUR 15 for a camper van), per Le Grau-du-Roi's tourist office, with pedestrians and cyclists parking free. There are no facilities on the beach to spend money on — no rentals, no food stalls, no paid amenities — so beyond parking, the real cost of a visit is transport and whatever's spent in Le Grau-du-Roi, Port Camargue, or Aigues-Mortes before or after. Restaurant meals in Le Grau-du-Roi's harbor area run to typical French coastal-town prices; the town's fish auction, historically held on the Quai Colbert, has long supplied its harbor restaurants directly.

## Safety

A seasonal lifeguard post covers the main RD255 access in summer (2026: June 27–August 31, 11:00–18:30, tel. 04 66 53 48 48); away from that point, this beach has no supervision. The EU Bathing Water Directive database rates the site's water quality "excellent," its top category (site ID FRJ1230133M030020), but that rating covers water chemistry, not supervision. Swimmers outside the lifeguarded stretch are on their own for currents, swell, and the mistral's tendency to raise chop quickly. The exposed swell that draws surfers and kitesurfers is a real hazard for casual swimmers unfamiliar with open Mediterranean conditions, as distinct from the sheltered coves more common further along the Côte d'Azur.

Away from the water, the main practical risk is the sun and distance: this is 18 kilometers of largely unshaded beach with limited water access points, so carrying enough water for the length of any walk matters more here than at a developed resort beach.

## Local Tips

**Walk to the lighthouse.** The Phare de l'Espiguette, restored and reopened to the public in mid-2023, is reachable on foot along the sand or by a separate access road; standing at its base and looking back roughly 700 meters to the current waterline is the most direct way to grasp how much this coast has grown since 1869.

**The naturist stretch is signed but not fenced.** The clothing-optional section toward the beach's eastern end, historically tied to the Hélio-Marin association of Nîmes, is marked by an official sign giving its authorized period (April 15–September 30) but has no fence separating it from clothed sections; visitors should expect both to coexist without a hard physical boundary.

**Bring everything you need.** With no concessions on the sand, water, sunscreen, and food need to come with you, not be bought on arrival.

**Wind changes conditions fast.** The mistral can turn calm water choppy within an hour; check a wind forecast specifically, not just a general weather forecast, before planning to swim or paddle.
