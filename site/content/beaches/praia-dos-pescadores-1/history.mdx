# The Beach That Outlived Its Name

Praia dos Pescadores means "Fishermen's Beach." For most of the 20th century that was a literal job description, not a heritage label. Before Albufeira built a proper harbor, this 225-meter stretch of sand in the center of the old town was the fishing fleet's working base — boats came in, were hauled up onto the beach between trips, and were launched again for the next catch. The tourist activity that now defines the same sand was, in that era, concentrated next door on Praia do Túnel, reached through a tunnel cut into the cliff to the west. The two beaches have effectively swapped functions since. What covers Praia dos Pescadores today — rows of licensed parasols and sun loungers, an outdoor escalator ferrying visitors down from the Pau da Bandeira clifftop, restaurants backing onto a hard-paved square — sits directly on top of what used to be a boatyard.

## The Earthquake That Reset the Town

Nothing about this beach's later history makes sense without the 1755 Lisbon earthquake. The quake, famous for leveling the Portuguese capital, hit the Algarve coast with a tsunami wave estimated at 10 meters. It destroyed nearly every building along Albufeira's waterfront; only 27 houses in the town proper were left standing. The parish church — itself a former mosque, converted after the Christian reconquest of the Algarve centuries earlier — collapsed onto the residents who had taken shelter inside, killing 227 people. Aftershocks continued along the coast for the better part of a year, until 20 August 1756, delaying reconstruction under Bishop Francisco Gomes de Avelar. The fishing economy that would later define this beach, and the tourism economy that replaced it, were both built from close to zero after this.

## The Industry That Actually Built the Town

The rebuild that mattered for Praia dos Pescadores was industrial, not architectural. By the early 20th century, fish and nut exports dominated Albufeira's economy, and the town ran five canning factories employing 700 to 800 people — mostly the wives of the fishermen who worked the boats moored on this beach. This was a real, exporting industry, distinct from subsistence fishing, and it is the version of Albufeira that has left no visible trace at Praia dos Pescadores today.

The collapse came between 1930 and 1960. Factories closed. The fleet that had once filled this beach with boats shrank. Homes in the old town emptied as residents left. Albufeira's population fell by roughly half over those three decades, and what fishing survived turned to subsistence work, feeding the town rather than an export trade. "Fishermen's Beach" preserves the memory of an economy that had already failed by the time postwar tourism arrived to replace it — a name is not always current information.

## The Trade That Replaced It

Tourism began pulling visitors to Albufeira in the 1960s, filling the economic and demographic hole the canning collapse had opened. It worked at scale: Albufeira's population, about 14,700 in 1960, grew to 40,828 by the 2011 census and 44,158 by 2021, and by 2025 the town ranked as Portugal's second most-visited destination by number of overnight stays — a year-round population near 44,000 swelling toward 300,000 in summer.

Praia dos Pescadores — the old launch point for the fleet — became the physical center of that tourist economy. The sand is now divided into licensed concessions renting parasols and loungers. The old town's restaurants, several specializing in the same fish and seafood the canning factories once processed for export, now sell it a plate at a time to visitors instead of a can at a time to distributors. The outdoor escalator down from Pau da Bandeira moves tourists, not net-carrying fishermen, between the clifftop car park and the sand.

## What's Left of the Original Trade

Little operational survives on the beach itself: the working fleet moved to the marina after 2003, and a few painted boats drawn up on the sand some mornings are what remains. Water quality is monitored, not just marketed — the EU Bathing Water Directive classifies this stretch PTCV7X, rated "1 - excellent," and lifeguards patrol mid-May through mid-October. Beyond that, the clearest surviving gesture toward the maritime past is symbolic rather than functional: every New Year's Eve, a temporary concert stage goes up on the Largo 25 de Abril beside the beach — the reggae band UB40 played a New Year's Eve concert there at the turn of 2009/2010 (accounts differ on whether the date fell in 2009 or 2010) — and the celebration ends with fireworks launched from boats and pontoons moored just offshore. For one night a year, vessels return to the water in front of Praia dos Pescadores. They carry a light show now, not a catch.

## A Name More Honest About the Past Than the Present

Praia dos Pescadores earned its name doing exactly what the name says. It has kept that name for decades after the activity that earned it collapsed — first under an earthquake, then under an industrial failure, and finally under a tourism boom that both replaced the fishing economy and made the name itself worth keeping as a draw. Visitors reading "Fishermen's Beach" off a map or a sign are, mostly without knowing it, standing on the site of a 20th-century economic collapse that the beach's branding quietly commemorates and its present-day use just as quietly contradicts.
