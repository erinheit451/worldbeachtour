# Coast Guard Beach Travel Guide

Coast Guard Beach sits inside Cape Cod National Seashore in Eastham, Massachusetts, on Ocean View Drive at the edge of the Nauset Spit barrier-beach system. Getting there is straightforward by car; getting onto the sand itself, in summer, means routing through a shuttle system built around a parking lot the National Park Service deliberately kept small and inland.

## Getting There

### By Air

**Cape Cod Gateway Airport (HYA)** in Hyannis is the nearest airport to Coast Guard Beach.

<DataCard label="Straight-line distance to HYA (Cape Cod Gateway Airport)" value="33.9" unit="km" />

That figure is a straight-line distance, not a road distance — Cape Cod's road network runs along the peninsula rather than directly across it, so the actual drive will cover more ground than the straight-line number suggests. No official source publishes a specific drive-time figure for this route, so this guide does not estimate one.

For travelers flying into Boston Logan International Airport, the National Park Service's own driving directions give a route rather than a mileage figure: take Route 3 south to the Sagamore Bridge in Bourne, then follow Route 6 east through the Cape to Eastham and the Salt Pond Visitor Center at 50 Nauset Road. Route 6 is the single main highway running the length of Cape Cod, and it is the road nearly every visitor will use regardless of where their trip starts.

### By Car

Route 6, the Mid-Cape Highway, is the primary route to Eastham from anywhere off the Cape. The National Park Service's Salt Pond Visitor Center, at 50 Nauset Road, is the reference destination for directions and the starting point for orienting yourself to Coast Guard Beach and the rest of the National Seashore's beaches in this section of the Cape.

### Reaching the Beach Itself

From mid-June through Labor Day, the parking area at Coast Guard Beach itself is restricted to Eastham residents and vehicles displaying handicap placards. Everyone else parks at **Little Creek**, the primary visitor lot a short distance west of the beach, where a shuttle carries passengers the rest of the way.

Outside that window — from the day after Labor Day through the following June — there are no parking restrictions at the Coast Guard Beach lot, and the shuttle requirement disappears along with the summer crowds. Facilities year-round are limited to seasonal restrooms and a wheelchair-accessible ramp down to the beach; there is no food service on-site in any season.

## Safety

### Lifeguards: seasonal, not absent

Coast Guard Beach is one of six lifeguarded beaches in Cape Cod National Seashore, staffed late June through Labor Day, with a year-round emergency call box available regardless of season. Outside that window, from Labor Day through the third week of the following June, there are no lifeguards on duty at any National Seashore beach, including this one — and the National Park Service is explicit that white sharks remain active into the fall, after the lifeguard season has already ended. Plan accordingly if you are visiting in September or October.

### Sharks

No shark bite has been documented at Coast Guard Beach itself. Cape Cod National Seashore's own shark-safety materials confirm three bites on swimmers since 2012, all off Truro and Wellfleet, one of them fatal in 2018. A purple flag with a white-shark silhouette is standard signage at lifeguarded beaches whenever a lifeguard is on duty — it is not a real-time alert that a shark has just been sighted. NPS guidance for reducing risk: swim, paddle, and surf in groups rather than alone; avoid murky or low-visibility water; and stay away from schools of fish or seals, which attract sharks.

### Rip currents

Official NPS guidance: don't fight the current. Swim parallel to the shoreline until you are out of it, then turn and swim toward shore. If you cannot escape the current, float or tread water calmly rather than exhausting yourself against it. Never turn your back to the ocean, and never swim alone.

### Wildlife distance

The National Seashore asks visitors to respect pedestrian and pet restrictions that protect nesting plovers and terns, and to keep at least 150 feet from any hauled-out seal.

## Food and Drink Nearby

There is no food service at Coast Guard Beach itself. The closest named option is **Arnold's Lobster and Clam Bar**, at 3580 Route 6 in Eastham, known for a trademarked house recipe for stuffed quahog — a Cape Cod specialty made from the hard-shell quahog clam, often prepared Portuguese-style with linguica. Further north, about 12 miles away by road, the town of Wellfleet is the namesake source of the Wellfleet oyster, one of two regional foods (with the Northern Quahog) formally accepted into Slow Food's Ark of Taste catalog of culturally significant foods. Wellfleet also hosts the annual **Wellfleet OysterFest** on Main Street in mid-October; the 2026 edition is the festival's 26th, running October 17-18, with gates open 10am-5pm and general admission $35.

## Local Notes

**Don't confuse the beaches.** A separate, town-run Nauset Beach exists in neighboring Orleans — a different beach entirely. Nauset Light Beach, with its own parking lot and the red-and-white Nauset Light, is a distinct National Park Service beach about one mile north of Coast Guard Beach, inside the same National Seashore.

**The station is not a museum.** The 1936 Coast Guard station on the bluff is a working building — the National Seashore's residential environmental-education center — not a site with public tours or exhibits.

**Off-season has real tradeoffs.** After Labor Day, parking restrictions lift and crowds thin, but lifeguards are gone and sharks are still active through the fall. Weigh that before planning a September or October swim.

**Bring cash-free payment.** The park's fee stations take cards only; arrive prepared rather than counting on cash working at the gate.
