# Visiting Pendine Sands

Pendine rewards planning more than most beaches, because so much of it is not simply open to the public. This guide covers access, timing, safety, and cost from what could actually be confirmed; where the research turned up nothing solid — road distances, drive times, accommodation rates — it says so rather than guessing.

## Getting There

Pendine sits on Carmarthen Bay in Carmarthenshire, south Wales. The nearest airport in this dataset is Swansea (SWS), about 32.8 km away as the crow flies — a straight-line distance, not a road distance, so treat it as a lower bound on any actual drive. The nearest named settlement in the dataset is Llanddowror, 8 km away. No road-distance or drive-time figures to Swansea, Cardiff, Carmarthen, or any other town were confirmed in research, and none are given here; check a map or routing app before travelling rather than relying on this page for mileage or journey time.

## Access: Who Owns What, and When

This is the single most important thing to know before visiting. The beach is split between two owners with different rules:

- **Pendine Community Council** owns and manages the section at the village end and to the west.
- **The Ministry of Defence**, through its contractor QinetiQ, owns the majority of the beach to the east and normally closes it to the public on weekdays, 8am to 4.30pm. It is generally open on weekends. The range covers 20.5 km² of land with 9 km of shoreline, an 18 km² sea danger area, and three test tracks — land the MOD acquired in 1940 and has used since for weapons development, ejector-seat trials, drone testing, and Hercules aircraft touch-and-go landings.

Practical rules on the Community Council side:

- **Dogs** are excluded from the bathing area between the two slipways.
- **Boats and jet skis** cannot be launched without membership of a club registered with Pendine Community Council.
- **Landsailing** is restricted to members of the Carmarthenshire Land Sailing Club (formed 2004), who must carry £5m public liability insurance.

## Driving on the Sand

All vehicle access was banned outright between 9 July 2004 and May 2010 on safety grounds, and cars have been allowed back since. Today, driving on the beach costs £2,000 a day and requires prior permission arranged through Carmarthenshire's local authorities — not a casual, spur-of-the-moment option.

## When to Go: What the Sea Data Actually Shows

Rather than guess at "best season," here is what the available wave record shows for this stretch of coast. Average wave height is calmest in May (0.73 m mean) and roughest in December (1.86 m mean), with an annual mean of 1.34 m and a typical wave period around 8.1 seconds. The largest wave logged in this record is 3.24 m. The overall character logged for Pendine is exposed, with real surf and a rip current risk to respect — this is not sheltered, gentle water at any time of year. No specific air or water temperature figures were confirmed in research for this beach, so none are given here; if you need that detail, check a live forecast before you go.

For lifeguard cover specifically: RNLI lifeguards patrol Pendine daily only from 11 July to 6 September, 10am to 6pm, with the locally posted reminder "no flags, no lifeguards." Outside that window, and outside those hours within it, there is no lifeguard cover. As recently as 15 August 2025, Tenby's RNLI inshore lifeboat was launched to assist people in the water off Pendine — a reminder that the sea here is taken seriously by the people who patrol it.

## Safety Notes Beyond the Water

Natural Resources Wales maintains a tidal flood-warning area at Pendine covering the Beach Hotel, the post office and adjacent property, the Pendine Sands Caravan Park, and the A4066 coast road — a warning was in force there as recently as 5-6 December 2025. Check current flood and tide warnings before staying overnight near the shore, particularly in the caravan park or along that road.

## Costs

Confirmed costs for Pendine are narrow but specific:

- **Driving on the beach:** £2,000/day, with prior permission.
- **Museum of Land Speed admission:** adults £7, concessions £6, children (5-18) £4, with a 10% discount for groups of four or more.
- **Landsailing club insurance requirement:** £5m public liability cover, held by the Carmarthenshire Land Sailing Club on members' behalf.

No parking prices, accommodation rates, or food prices for Pendine or the surrounding villages were confirmed in research. The nearest named food options are the kiosks and first-floor restaurant at the Parry Thomas Centre and the café at the Caban, both on the promenade rather than the beach itself; no menu prices for either are available here.
