# Ocean Beach Travel Guide

Ocean Beach sits along San Francisco's Pacific edge, running from the Cliff House and Sutro Baths area in the north down to Fort Funston in the south, with the Sunset Dunes park corridor — the former Great Highway — paralleling most of its length. That recent change to the road is covered in full on [the main Ocean Beach guide](/beaches/ocean-beach-14); this page covers how to actually get to the beach, what the water and weather are doing by season, what's known and unknown about costs, and how to stay safe once you're there.

## Getting there

The most direct public transit option is the Muni Metro stop at Judah and La Playa, which the park service itself points to as the way in. On-site parking is very limited, and the National Park Service's own guidance is blunt about it: public transportation is the best way to get to Ocean Beach, not a car. Straight-line distance from the beach to San Francisco International Airport (SFO) is 20.2 kilometers. No source behind this page gives the actual road distance or drive time between SFO and Ocean Beach, so treat any specific minutes-in-traffic estimate you see elsewhere as an unverified guess rather than a documented fact — the honest answer here is a straight-line number only.

## Seasons and surf conditions

Ocean Beach doesn't have a conventional beach season built around air temperature and sunbathing; it has a surf season built around swell size. Wave heights here average 1.76 meters across the year, but that average hides a real swing: January is the biggest month, averaging 2.55 meters and reaching as high as 3.96 meters on the biggest recorded days, while August is the calmest month, averaging 1.16 meters. Typical wave periods run around 10.6 seconds. In practice, that means winter delivers the most power and the most serious conditions for experienced surfers, while late summer is the closest this beach gets to calm — which at Ocean Beach still means cold water and real currents, not a swimming-pool ocean. As one live conditions snapshot from September 13, 2026 put it, the surf zone can carry "life-threatening rip currents" even on an ordinary day, with water temperature that day recorded at 59°F. That is a single dated reading, not a seasonal average, but it's representative of how this beach talks about itself even in its calmer months.

## Costs

This is the honest gap in what can be said here: no source behind this page gives current parking prices, transit fares, surf-lesson costs, beach-wheelchair rental fees, or bonfire-permit fees for Ocean Beach, and this page won't invent numbers to fill that space. What is documented is that on-site parking is limited enough that the park service actively discourages driving in, and that public transit via Judah and La Playa is the recommended alternative. If you need current prices for parking, transit, or lessons, check San Francisco Municipal Transportation Agency and National Park Service sources directly before you go, rather than trusting an unsourced number on a travel page.

## Safety

Currents at Ocean Beach vary sharply by location and can be genuinely dangerous regardless of how the surface looks from the sand. The National Park Service has deliberately chosen not to install lifeguard towers here, on the reasoning that a beach that looks and feels dangerous keeps more people out of the water than a staffed one would — the full reasoning behind that policy, and what it has cost, is on the main guide. If you're bringing a dog, the leash requirement in the snowy plover protection zone runs from July 1 through May 15, covering the stretch from stairwell 21 to Sloat Boulevard and extending 1,000 feet offshore. Beach fires are allowed only March 1 through October 31, and only in the fire rings provided between stairwells 15 and 20 — anywhere else, or outside that season, there's no legal fire.

Ocean Beach is a beach to visit prepared, not a beach to wing. Check current surf and rip-current conditions before you go, plan on public transit if you can, and treat the water itself as something to watch rather than something to swim in.
