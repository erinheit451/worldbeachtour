Jaz Beach sits on the Adriatic coast of Budva Municipality, Montenegro, about 2.5 kilometers west of Budva's walled old town, per Wikipedia's unqualified figure, and 2.9 kilometers by straight-line distance from the town center, per this site's dataset; road distance wasn't independently verified. The English Wikipedia entry on the beach describes it as "unique in that it is a pebble beach" — a correction worth stating plainly, since tourism copy about Jaz frequently calls it a rare sand beach on Montenegro's rocky coastline. It isn't. The beach runs in two sections: a main stretch of roughly 850 meters backed by a campground with 2,000 tent and motorhome lots, and a second, formerly nudist, stretch of about 450 meters, for a combined shoreline near 1.3 kilometers.

The name has nothing to do with jazz music, a folk etymology that circulates online. In Serbo-Croatian, *jaz* is an old noun for a gap or abyss, from the Proto-Slavic *ězъ* — fitting for a cove that sits below a break in the coastal hills, behind which lies the small village of Seoca.

## What Jaz Beach Actually Is

Jaz's real distinction is its concert history, and that history is uneven rather than uniformly impressive. Thirteen months after Montenegro's 3 June 2006 declaration of independence, the Rolling Stones played Jaz Beach on 9 July 2007, on the A Bigger Bang Tour, to a crowd Wikipedia estimates at approximately 40,000 (Budva's own Wikipedia article gives a lower figure near 35,000). Madonna followed on 25 September 2008, on the Sticky & Sweet Tour, to a documented 47,524 attendees and a reported $3,463,063 in ticket revenue — her first Montenegro date.

What gets left out of most write-ups: a follow-on Live Music Festival was announced for 2009 with Tina Turner, Britney Spears, and Zucchero billed. None of them performed. The event was cancelled as the 2008 financial crisis reached the Balkans, and Wikipedia states flatly that a second edition "has never been held." The concert-venue identity was revived in 2014, when Serbia's EXIT Foundation launched Sea Dance Festival at Jaz — it ran there through 2017 (peaking at 110,000 attendees in 2015), then relocated to Buljarica Beach (2018–2022), then left Montenegro for Croatia in 2023. It returned to Budva 28–31 August 2026, held at Bečići Beach — not Jaz Beach — with free entry via registration.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History** — The beach's concert record runs from a stadium-scale rock show thirteen months after statehood to a cancelled festival whose headliners never appeared, then a second festival that also eventually left. See the [full history deep-dive](/beaches/jaz-beach/history) for the complete timeline.

**Travel & Logistics** — Jaz functions as a day-use beach with a campground rather than a resort strip; there's no documented on-beach dining in the sources checked for this page, and the closest restaurants are in Budva Old Town, about 2.5 kilometers east.

**Swimming** — Open-Meteo's ERA5 marine reanalysis puts the annual mean wave height at 0.68 meters, with the calmest average seas in June and the biggest in January — figures describing a generally mild, swimmable stretch of the open Adriatic rather than a sheltered lagoon.

## Practical Context

Jaz Beach's appeal is real but specific: it's a pebble beach with a genuine, if inconsistent, record of hosting global-scale music events, next to an ordinary Montenegrin campground and village hinterland. It rewards visitors who come for what's actually there rather than what the marketing promises.
