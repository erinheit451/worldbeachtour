# Freshwater West: A Beach Layered by Institutions That Never Talked to Each Other

Freshwater West sits on the Castlemartin peninsula in southwestern Pembrokeshire, inside the Pembrokeshire Coast National Park, about 3.6 kilometers from the village of Castlemartin. It is almost a mile of open, west-facing sand backed by dunes and red sandstone cliffs that rise to 63 meters inland, with a 6.5-meter tidal range and no offshore islands to blunt the Atlantic swell that reaches it. By its own official designation, it is unsuitable for bathing.

## What Actually Makes This Beach Distinctive

The dunes behind Freshwater West back directly onto Castlemartin Training Area, a roughly 6,000-acre live tank-firing range the British Army established in 1938. From May 1961 to October 1996 — 35 years — West Germany's Bundeswehr trained its tank units on that range under a NATO agreement, because the north of West Germany had no comparable ground of its own. The range still fires roughly 44 weeks a year, with offshore exclusion zones reaching up to 12 nautical miles depending on the ammunition in use, and the Pembrokeshire Coast Path has been permanently rerouted inland to keep walkers clear of it. Three soldiers have died there since 2012.

None of that stopped two film productions from using the beach's isolation for the opposite purpose. In June and July 2009, Ridley Scott's *Robin Hood* filmed a beach battle here depicting the French invasion of southern England, with over 600 extras and 150 horses. That same year, Warner Bros. built a temporary Shell Cottage in the dunes for *Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows – Part 1*, the setting for Dobby the house-elf's death and burial. The cottage was fully removed after filming — nothing of the set remains on site. The grave visitors find in the dunes is a fan-made memorial built at the filming spot, and on 1 November 2022 the National Trust for Wales announced it could remain in the immediate term, asking visitors to take only photographs and leave no socks or painted pebbles.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History** — The Castlemartin range's 35-year lease to West Germany's post-war army, and the three fatal training accidents there since 2012, sit a few hundred meters from a beach most visitors know only for a film set that no longer exists.

**Surf** — A consistent Atlantic beach break that has hosted the Welsh National Surfing Championships; wave data puts the annual mean height at 2.05 meters, with December averaging 2.87 meters and a recorded peak day of 5.18 meters.

**Culture** — Two separate film productions used the beach's emptiness in the same year, 2009: *Robin Hood*'s beach battle depicting the French invasion of southern England, and the Shell Cottage set built for the Harry Potter franchise's Dobby storyline.

**Safety** — No permanent lifeguard covers the beach; a seasonal station has operated only in summer since 2010. Strong offshore swells, strong currents and frequent rips make it unsafe for swimming, and the beach shares a boundary with an active military range.

Freshwater West is not one story. It is several institutions — the Ministry of Defence, NATO, two unrelated film productions, the Welsh surfing federation, and a National Trust conservation team — that each wanted the same isolated stretch of coast for reasons that had nothing to do with each other.
