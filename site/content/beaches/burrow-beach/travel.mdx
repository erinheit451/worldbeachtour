# Burrow Beach Travel Guide

Burrow Beach is a neighborhood strand in Sutton, on Dublin's northern coast, reached easily from the city by rail. It has no beachfront hotels, no rental kiosks, and no lifeguard for most of the year — this is a guide to a place Dubliners use for a walk, not a resort itinerary.

## Getting There

<DataCard label="Dublin Airport (DUB)" value="11.3" unit="km straight-line" />
<DataCard label="Sutton DART Station" value="~800" unit="m walk" />

**By Air.** Dublin Airport (DUB) sits 11.3 kilometers from Burrow Beach in a straight line; by road, via the airport approach roads and the coastal route through Sutton, the distance is closer to 12 kilometers. Go-Ahead Ireland's route 102 runs directly between Dublin Airport and Sutton DART station along the Fingal coastal corridor, making a taxi/rideshare the only faster door-to-door option; check current timetables before relying on it, since routing on Dublin-area bus services has been revised under the ongoing BusConnects network changes.

**By DART.** Sutton railway station, on the Howth branch line that opened in 1846, is roughly an 800-meter walk from the beach. DART trains run this line from Dublin city center north through Sutton toward Howth; from the station, follow the coastal road toward Burrow Road to reach the sand.

**By Bus.** Dublin Bus routes H3 and 6 serve Sutton Cross, and route 102 terminates at Sutton DART station, according to Wikipedia's entry for the beach.

**By Car.** Sutton sits off the coastal approach north of Dublin city center; road distance from the city center runs close to 14.6 kilometers by typical routing, against an 11-kilometer straight-line distance. Roadside parking exists along the beach access road at Burrow Road.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Wave data drawn from marine reanalysis (Open-Meteo Marine, ERA5 ocean reanalysis, 2023-2024) shows this stretch running calmest in May (mean wave height 0.5 m) and roughest in March (mean 1.03 m, with big-day heights up to 1.62 m), with a short wave period around 4.3 seconds year-round — this is fetch-limited Irish Sea chop, not a seasonal surf swell. The site dataset records zero tropical cyclones tracked near this coast in the past 50 years. Lifeguard cover, where it exists, is weekends-only in June and full-time in July and August; outside that window the beach is unsupervised regardless of weather.

## Where to Stay

Burrow Beach itself has no accommodation on the sand. The closest option is the **Marine Hotel** at Sutton Cross, built in 1897 and renamed the Marine in 1913, roughly a 10-15 minute walk from the beach, with an in-house bar and restaurant. Beyond that, Howth village, a few kilometers east along the coast, and Dublin city center, reachable directly by DART, both carry substantially more accommodation density than Sutton itself, which is primarily residential.

## Costs

<DataCard label="Beach Access" value="Free" unit="No entrance fee" />

Burrow Beach itself is free, with no entrance fee and free roadside parking along the access road. Food and transport costs at Sutton and in Dublin generally follow the city's standard price level; there is no beach-specific pricing to report because there is no commercial activity on the beach itself.

## Safety

**Water conditions.** This is Irish Sea water off a north-facing tombolo beach — cold for swimming outside high summer, with no on-site verified water-temperature monitoring in the source data for this page. Dublin Bay's tides are semidiurnal with a spring range close to 4 meters, enough to expose or submerge a wide stretch of the tombolo's outer sand within a few hours; check tide times before walking out at low water.

**Lifeguard cover.** Fingal County Council posts a lifeguard at Burrow Beach on weekends in June and daily through July and August. Outside that window, and outside those hours within it, the beach is unsupervised.

**General safety.** Sutton and the surrounding Fingal area are ordinary Dublin suburbs with no particular crime profile tied to the beach; standard precautions with unattended belongings on the sand or in parked cars apply, as anywhere.

## Local Tips

**Combine it with the Howth cliff path.** Burrow Beach sits at the western, tombolo end of the Howth peninsula; the more dramatic cliff walking is further east, around Howth Head itself.

**Time it to the tide.** Given the roughly 4-meter spring tidal range, the beach looks and behaves very differently at low water, when a much wider stretch of sand is exposed, than at high water.

**Look east for Ireland's Eye.** The uninhabited island offshore, with its early Christian monastic site and 1803-04 Martello tower, is visible from the beach's eastern end without needing a boat trip to appreciate.

**Bring your own food.** There is no kiosk, café, or vendor on the beach; the closest options are the Marine Hotel at Sutton Cross or Sutton village near the DART station.

**Expect a working links course next door.** Sutton Golf Club's nine-hole course runs along part of the tombolo behind the dunes; it is a private club, not a beach amenity, but it explains some of the land use immediately inland of the sand.
