Olympic Beach is the English name for Olympiaki Akti, a settlement in the Katerini municipality of Pieria, Central Macedonia, on Greece's Thermaic Gulf coast. The place is younger than it looks on a map: nobody lived here permanently until 1980, and the name Olympic Beach itself only dates to 1986. Before that it was Parathalassia Katerini, "Seaside Katerini," a functional label for the stretch of coast serving the inland city of Katerini, 7.5 kilometers away. Some locals still use an older name for the same coastline — Katerinoskala, "Katerini's landing" — and the city's 3rd Port Authority Department of Skala Katerini is still headquartered here, a working office rather than a tourist attraction.

The mountain the beach is named for is not on it. Mount Olympus's highest point, Mytikas, rises 2,917.7 meters above sea level about 26 kilometers to the southwest, at the boundary of Thessaly and Macedonia — visible from the shore on a clear day, not a backdrop you can walk to. In Greek myth it was the dwelling of the twelve Olympian gods; its first confirmed ascent came on 2 August 1913, when the local guide Christos Kakkalos led Swiss climbers Frédéric Boissonnas and Daniel Baud-Bovy to the summit, sixty-seven years before the beach that now carries the mountain's name had a single resident.

What the beach delivers, independent of its name, is measurable. The EU rates its bathing water "1 – excellent" at monitoring site ELBW099047006101, and it has held the Blue Flag every year except 1998, according to the Greek-language Wikipedia article — no source consulted for this page explains that one gap year. Open-Meteo's ERA5 marine reanalysis for 2023–2024 puts the annual mean wave height at 0.35 meters, with the calmest month, June, averaging 0.2 meters and the roughest, January, averaging 0.57 meters; the largest single modeled day reached 1.16 meters. This is a sheltered gulf beach, not a surf coast.

The basic facts about the settlement itself are not fully settled. The 2021 census population is reported as 236 residents by the English Wikipedia infobox and as 420 by the body of the Greek-language article — the two disagree, and neither links a primary source that resolves the gap. What both agree on is that hotels, rental rooms, bungalows, and a campground line the waterfront and fill well beyond either figure through July and August, then empty by autumn. Three kilometers north sits Paralia, the larger neighboring resort with about 928 year-round residents, founded after 1922 by refugees from Cius in Asia Minor and itself renamed from an earlier, unflattering name, Vromeroskala ("Foul Landing") — the same rebranding pattern that produced Olympic Beach's own name, playing out on this coast twice.

**Most relevant lenses on this page:**

**Travel** — Getting here means reconciling two different distances to the nearest airport: 45 kilometers in a straight line from Thessaloniki (SKG), but roughly 107 kilometers by road around the gulf via the A1/E75 motorway. Katerini's railway station, 7.1 kilometers away, connects to Thessaloniki and Athens.

**History** — The deep-dive spoke on this page covers the 1980–1986 renaming in detail: what the settlement was called before it was "Olympic," what a "skala" is on this coast, and why Wikipedia has to disambiguate this village from actual Olympic Games beach venues.

Olympic Beach is what it is without the name: a calm, EU-rated-excellent stretch of the Thermaic Gulf, built up for tourism since 1980, formally renamed for a mountain 26 kilometers away in 1986. The Olympian branding sits on top of a coastal landing that did its actual job — moving people and goods between Katerini and the sea — long before anyone thought to call it Olympic anything.
