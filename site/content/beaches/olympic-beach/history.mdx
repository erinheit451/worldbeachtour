# The Beach That Renamed Itself for a Mountain

Olympic Beach is younger than the parents of most people who visit it. The settlement Wikipedia lists as Olympiaki Akti, in the Katerini municipality of Pieria, was not inhabited until 1980. For six years after that it carried a plain, functional Greek name, Parathalassia Katerini — "Seaside Katerini." Only in 1986 did the municipality rename it Olympiaki Akti, trading a description of what the place was (Katerini's stretch of coast) for the name of a mountain it does not actually sit on.

## Katerini's Landing, Before It Was a Beach

Before either name, this stretch of the Thermaic Gulf had a working identity: Katerinoskala, "Katerini's landing." Wikipedia's English-language article notes the village is "still domestically known by its initial name Katerinoskala," and the function behind that name persists today — the 3rd Port Authority Department of Skala Katerini is based here, a small administrative office serving the inland city of Katerini, 7.5 kilometers away. The beach identity is a layer added on top of a coastal access point that predates the tourist economy.

## A Rebrand With a Precedent Next Door

The 1986 rename was not an isolated choice. Three kilometers north, the larger neighboring resort of Paralia — 928 year-round residents at the 2021 census, more than double even the higher of the two population figures reported for Olympic Beach — went through the same arc decades earlier. Paralia was founded after 1922 by refugees from Cius, in Asia Minor, part of the population exchange that followed the Greco-Turkish War, and it was originally called Vromeroskala: "Foul Landing." At some point the town dropped "vromero" (foul, stinking) for the generic, pleasant "Paralia" — Greek for "beach." Olympic Beach's own transition from a working skala to a marketed name followed the same template roughly sixty years later.

## The Mountain in the Name

Mount Olympus, the source of the 1986 branding, is not close in any way a swimmer would register. Mytikas, its highest point, stands 2,917.7 meters above sea level, about 26 kilometers to the southwest as the crow flies, straddling the border of Thessaly and Macedonia. In Greek mythology it was the seat of the twelve Olympian gods — Zeus, Hera, and the rest of the pantheon. In documented history, its first confirmed ascent came on 2 August 1913, when the local guide Christos Kakkalos led the Swiss climbers Frédéric Boissonnas and Daniel Baud-Bovy to the summit, sixty-seven years before anyone lived permanently on the beach later named for the mountain. What the beach and the mountain share is a sightline on clear days, not a location.

## The Name Collides With Itself

The genericness of the choice shows up inside Wikipedia's own architecture. The English-language article for Olympiaki Akti carries a disambiguation notice: readers searching "Olympic Beach" are redirected toward a separate article, the list of beach venues built for actual editions of the Olympic Games — the sand courts and seaside sport facilities constructed for Games such as Athens 2004. A search engine cannot immediately distinguish a small Greek resort village from Olympic Games infrastructure, because both are described, accurately, by the same two words. That collision is a small but telling measure of how replaceable "Olympic" branding becomes once it is detached from the Games themselves and applied to real estate.

## What the Sources Actually Say — and Where They Disagree

The 1980 settlement date and 1986 rename both trace to specific, quotable sentences in the Greek-language Wikipedia article: "Η Ολυμπιακή Ακτή κατοικήθηκε για πρώτη φορά το 1980 και μέχρι το 1986 ονομαζόταν Παραθαλάσσια Κατερίνη" — "Olympiaki Akti was first inhabited in 1980 and until 1986 was named Parathalassia Katerini." That's a sourced claim inside an encyclopedia entry, not a documented municipal record; the underlying government act renaming the settlement isn't linked from the article.

The population figure is where the sources openly conflict. The infobox on the English Wikipedia article gives 236 residents for the 2021 census. The body text of the Greek-language article gives 420 for the same census. Both state "the 2021 census" as their basis; neither links a resolvable primary document, and this article cannot adjudicate which number is correct. What both versions agree on is that the permanent population, whichever figure is right, is a small fraction of what the settlement's hotels, rental rooms, bungalows, and campground are built to hold during July and August.

## The Honest Version

None of this is a scandal on the scale of a polluted bay or a cratered reef. It's a smaller, more common kind of thinness: a functional coastal landing, formally settled only in 1980, renamed in 1986 for a mountain it doesn't touch, with even the most basic fact about it — how many people live there — unresolved between the two language editions of the encyclopedia that documents it. The beach itself performs fine by the metrics that are actually measured: EU bathing water rated "1 – excellent," a Blue Flag held every year but 1998, gentle Thermaic Gulf swells averaging 0.35 meters annually. None of that required Mount Olympus. The mountain's name was added afterward, in an administrative decision thirty-nine years old, to a coastline that had already existed under its own, less glamorous name for as long as Katerini needed a way down to the water.
