Pipa Beach is a former fishing village in Tibau do Sul, Rio Grande do Norte, on Brazil's northeast coast. It was still a working fishing settlement when surfers and backpackers began arriving in the 1970s, drawn by clear water, fine white sand, and a run of red-ochre sandstone cliffs that wall the beach off from the forest above. The name comes from a barrel-shaped rock ("pipa" is European Portuguese for barrel) that Portuguese sailors used as a navigation landmark.

Those cliffs are the reason to come, and the reason to be careful. They are cut from the Barreiras Formation, a partially cemented sedimentary sequence common along this coast, and a 2022 geomorphology study by researchers at the Federal University of Ceará and the Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte found them structurally unstable by nature: wave action notches the base at high tide while rainwater leaches the cement out of fracture sets higher in the rock. On 17 November 2020, a section collapsed on a family resting in its shade, killing Hugo Pereira, 32, his wife Stella Souza, and their seven-month-old son. On 17 January 2024, a tourist on a guided clifftop quadbike tour, Ana Carla Silva de Oliveira, 31, died falling roughly 30 meters when the vehicle went over an unmarked edge. In May 2025, after a state civil-defense warning, the municipality of Tibau do Sul declared a state of emergency and fenced the four most dangerous sections with eucalyptus wood and rope.

## What Pipa Actually Offers

Beyond the cliffs, Pipa is a working beach town: Avenida Baia dos Golfinhos, the commercial spine that grew out of the 1970s surfer settlement, is lined with restaurants, pousadas, and shops, some run by the international residents the village has attracted since the 1980s. A short walk south leads to Praia do Amor, a cove whose sandbar forms a heart shape at low tide. In the other direction, roughly 4 km northwest toward Tibau do Sul, Praia do Madeiro borders the roughly 90-hectare Santuario Ecologico de Pipa, a private Atlantic Forest reserve established from 1980.

Baia dos Golfinhos, the bay that lies between the village and Praia do Madeiro, hosts Guiana dolphins (boto-cinza) most mornings. The bay is reachable on foot at low tide (best entry from Praia do Centro), and dolphins are also seen from boat tours out of Pipa and from the clifftop miradouro. From roughly December to May, Fundacao Projeto TAMAR technicians patrol the beaches at dawn tracking hawksbill turtle nests, a program that has run since the early 2000s.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**Environment** — The cliffs' geology (Barreiras Formation, wave-cut notching, rain-leached fracture sets) is documented in a 2022 academic study and is the direct cause of a five-year run of deaths and injuries, addressed most fully in the environment deep-dive.

**Travel** — Getting to Pipa, when to go around the December-to-May turtle season versus quieter months, and what the 2025 clifftop fencing means for visitors are covered in the travel guide.

**History** — The shift from 1970s fishing village to backpacker destination to the 2025 municipal emergency decree is a fast, documented arc for a place this size.

## Practical Context

Pipa's economy runs on the same red cliffs that a 2022 study calls structurally unstable and that have caused a documented sequence of deaths and injuries from 2020 through 2025. The 900 meters of clifftop fencing installed in 2025, and a September 2025 federal-prosecutor order for a carrying-capacity study and demolition schedule, are the current, unresolved state of that tension.
