# The Cliffs That Won't Hold Still

Pipa's postcard image, red-ochre sandstone dropping straight to white sand, is also the site of a documented, dated pattern of collapse and injury. Between November 2020 and September 2025, the cliffs and the clifftop plateau above them were linked to at least four deaths and at least six additional injuries across separate incidents in 2020, 2024, and 2025. This is the geology behind that pattern, and the government response it eventually forced.

## What the Rock Actually Is

The bluffs walling off Pipa's beaches are cut into the Barreiras Formation, a sequence of partially consolidated Cenozoic sediments that runs along much of Brazil's northeast coast. A 2022 study in the Revista Brasileira de Geomorfologia, by Rubson Pinheiro Maia (Federal University of Ceará) and Rodrigo de Freitas Amorim (Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte), used high-resolution aerophotogrammetry and fracture-set mapping to identify the failure mechanism in detail.

Two processes compound each other. Wave impact at the cliff base during high tide carves an erosive notch that strips structural support from the rock above it. Separately, strata-bound vertical fracture sets, confined to specific layers of the formation, raise the rock's permeability; rainwater percolates into those fractures and leaches out the material that cements the sand grains together. The cliffs reach roughly 40 meters in places. The study's central finding is blunt: this formation is not becoming unstable because of visitor traffic. It is unstable on its own, and tourism places people directly beneath and on top of the part that is actively failing.

## The Afternoon It Became Unignorable

On 17 November 2020, around midday, a section of cliff collapsed onto a family resting in its shade on the sand below. Hugo Pereira, 32, a hotel reception manager at the Sunbay hotel whose family had recently moved to Pipa from Jundiaí, São Paulo, his wife Stella Souza, and their seven-month-old son were killed; the family dog died with them. Three rescue teams responded, along with forensic investigators from Rio Grande do Norte's Technical-Scientific Institute. No single trigger was ever publicly identified — consistent with how these formations actually fail, where the wave notch and the leached fracture planes do slow, invisible work for months before a given afternoon happens to be the one where a block finally lets go.

## A Second Hazard, Riding on the First

The cliff face is not the only danger. The clifftop plateau above it, known as the Chapadão, is a working route for guided quadbike tours, and the unmarked edge has claimed lives independent of the rock below. A tourist fell from a quadbike here in September 2020, weeks before the fatal collapse. On 17 January 2024, Ana Carla Silva de Oliveira, 31, a Legislative Assembly employee from Roraima, died after the quadbike she was riding in went over the edge; she fell roughly 30 meters. Her sister-in-law survived and was airlifted to a hospital in Natal. Local reporting noted there was no warning signage at the fall site. In January 2025, two more quadbike accidents on the Chapadão, four days apart, injured four additional people.

The pattern repeats because the feature that makes the clifftop marketable as a tour route, an uninterrupted drop straight to the beach, is the same feature that kills when a vehicle or a person crosses the edge.

## The State Finally Moves

Rio Grande do Norte's State Civil Defense issued a technical report in 2025 warning of imminent collapse risk in specific Chapadão sections. Tibau do Sul's municipality responded with Municipal Decree No. 008/2025, declaring a state of emergency under Article 8 of Brazil's federal Civil Protection Law (12.608/2012), and began emergency works: 900 meters of fencing, built from eucalyptus wood and nautical rope, strung across the four most dangerous points, paired with new warning signage. The material was a deliberate choice — a barrier rustic and low-impact enough not to wreck the clifftop view it exists to keep people a safe distance from.

The fencing did not close the file. On 7 September 2025, a landslide at Praia do Madeiro, roughly 4-5 km northwest of the main Chapadão on the opposite side of the village, injured two people and buried four beach bars operating at the cliff base, following an earlier collapse at the same beach in June 2024 that had already destroyed structures there. Brazil's federal public prosecutors (the MPF) responded by giving Tibau do Sul 15 days to produce a management plan defining permitted activities, an environmental carrying-capacity study, and a demolition schedule for structures built too close to the edge. Separately, Justiça Federal had already ordered, in August 2024, demolition of restaurante Barravento's irregular constructions between Cacimbinhas and Ponta do Madeiro, and the MPF separately obtained a conviction against Madeiro Beach Hotel Bar e Restaurantes, unlicensed for more than 20 years; removal of its structures has begun, and the owners must present a PRAD recovery plan.

## The Law That Predates All of This

Brazil's federal environmental council, Conama, designated a 100-meter buffer from cliff edges as a Permanent Preservation Area under Resolution 303/2002 — over two decades before the most recent landslide. A 2020 attempt to weaken that protection was overturned by Brazil's Supreme Court in December 2021, restoring the buffer nationwide. The rule was in force through the 2020 deaths, the 2024 death, and the 2025 incidents. The recurring failure at Pipa has not been an absence of law; it has been the gap between the law on paper and what was actually permitted to be built, and walked on, at the edge.

## Living Alongside It

Not every part of Pipa's ecology cuts against the village. From roughly December through May, Fundacao Projeto TAMAR technicians patrol the beaches at dawn, tracking overnight turtle tracks to locate and protect hawksbill (Eretmochelys imbricata) nests, a monitoring program running since the early 2000s. Past Praia do Madeiro, the roughly 90-hectare Santuario Ecologico de Pipa, a private Atlantic Forest reserve established from 1980 by engineer Charles Capelle, has kept part of the coastline, including access to Baia dos Golfinhos, out of development entirely. Guiana dolphins (boto-cinza) gather in that bay most mornings, seen from a low-tide walk along the beach, from boat tours out of Pipa, and from the clifftop overlook — a reminder that the same cliffs responsible for the fatalities are also what has kept this stretch of coast from being built out to the water's edge.

## What This Means If You're Standing on the Sand

Pipa's cliffs were not made dangerous by overtourism layered onto an otherwise stable formation. The 2022 geomorphology study is explicit that the fracture sets and wave notching would erode this coastline with or without a single visitor. What tourism did was put hundreds to thousands of people a day directly beneath and on top of a formation that was already failing on its own schedule — and it took a family's death, a tourist's death, four more people injured in a single week, and a second beach's bars getting buried before fencing went up. The 900 meters of rope-and-eucalyptus barrier and the September 2025 prosecutorial deadline are the current state of a five-year-old, still-unresolved argument about how much of this landscape a coastal village can sell before the landscape sends back a bill.
