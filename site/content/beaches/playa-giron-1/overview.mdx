Playa Girón is a village and beach on the east bank of the Bahía de Cochinos, the Bay of Pigs, in the municipality of Ciénaga de Zapata in Cuba's Matanzas Province. It is named after Gilberto Girón, a French pirate active on this coast around 1604, and about 1,973 people live here on the edge of the Caribbean's largest wetland. The shore is a low coral and limestone iron-shore, sharp fossil rock rather than sand, edged by clear water over reef.

## Why this beach matters

The village lends its name to a Cold War battle. What the English-speaking world calls the Bay of Pigs invasion, Cubans call Girón, after this beach, where the main seaborne landing came ashore on 17 April 1961. Roughly 1,400 CIA-trained Cuban exiles of Brigade 2506 landed here at Blue Beach; within hours a Cuban Sea Fury sank the transport Río Escondido, carrying their reserve ammunition and fuel. Over about 72 hours the fighting closed on Girón, the last ground the brigade held, and by 19 April the invasion had collapsed. About 114 brigade members were killed and roughly 1,189 captured, later ransomed for some $53 million in food and medicine.

The victory is kept deliberately visible. The Museo Girón, founded in 1976 and declared a National Monument in 1978, stands in the village with a Hawker Sea Fury and an M41 tank parked outside, and concrete markers name the Cuban dead along the coast road.

The same coral shelf that made this a hard beach to land on makes it a diving coast. Cueva de los Peces, a flooded sinkhole about 70 metres deep, sits on the inland side of the road; Punta Perdiz and Caleta Buena, a cove about 8 km southeast, are open, clear shore-diving and snorkelling sites. There is no wide sand and no lifeguard, and you enter over sharp diente de perro rock or from fixed ladders, but the visibility is long and the reef starts close in.

## Most relevant lenses

**History** — The 1961 battle is the beach's defining story: the main landing at Blue Beach, the loss of the Río Escondido, and the collapse of the invasion in 72 hours, all preserved in the Museo Girón and the roadside memorials. This is the reason the village is known.

**Diving** — The reef-fringed coast and the cenote at Cueva de los Peces make Girón a shore-diving and snorkelling destination, with long visibility and a drop-off close to shore.

**Nature** — The Ciénaga de Zapata, the largest wetland in the Caribbean and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, wraps the village and is major bird habitat, with flamingos, herons, and Cuban endemics.
