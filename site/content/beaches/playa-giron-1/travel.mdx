# Playa Girón Travel Guide

Playa Girón is a small village on the east bank of the Bahía de Cochinos, the Bay of Pigs, in the Ciénaga de Zapata of Matanzas Province, on Cuba's southern coast. It is best known as the main landing site of the April 1961 invasion that Cubans call Girón, and the two reasons to make the trip are the battle history, centred on the Museo Girón, and the diving along the clear, reef-fringed coast. This is not a resort beach: the shore is coral and limestone rock, not sand, and there is no lifeguard.

## Getting There

### By air

International flights to Cuba mostly arrive at **Havana's José Martí International Airport (HAV)**, roughly a 3 to 3.5-hour drive north of Playa Girón. **Varadero's Juan Gualberto Gómez Airport (VRA)** handles charter and seasonal traffic and is a comparable drive. The **nearest airport** is **Cienfuegos (Jaime González, CFG)**, about 64 km away, but it serves limited domestic and seasonal flights rather than most international arrivals.

### By car

Most visitors drive or hire a private taxi. From Havana, the route runs southeast on the Autopista Nacional to the **Jagüey Grande** exit, then south on the peninsula road through the Zapata wetland to the coast. Fuel is scarce once you leave the highway, so fill up at Jagüey Grande; the last stretch is a two-lane road across the swamp, and road surfaces are uneven. Renting a car in Havana or Varadero and hiring a driver for the round trip are both common; agree on the price before leaving.

### By bus

**Víazul**, the intercity coach line that serves foreign travellers, runs the Zapata coast on limited schedules, with services connecting Havana, Playa Larga, Playa Girón, Cienfuegos, and Trinidad. Schedules are thin and change seasonally, so confirm current times directly. There is no rail service to the village.

## Best Time to Visit

The **dry season, roughly November to April**, is the better window: less rain, calmer conditions, and clearer water for diving. The **wet season, May to October**, is hotter and more humid and overlaps the Atlantic hurricane season, which runs June through November; this coast has recorded numerous cyclone passages, some at the highest intensity, so watch forecasts if you travel then.

Sea conditions inside the bay are usually mild. Wave heights average about 0.79 metres over the year, running calmest around May and a little livelier around November. Water visibility is generally long, which is the point for divers.

## Where to Stay

Lodging in Playa Girón is overwhelmingly **casas particulares**, licensed rooms in private homes, which are the backbone of Cuban travel outside the big resorts. They are modest and family-run, and hosts usually cook meals for an added charge. There is also a dated state-run beach hotel in the area, but the casas are the more reliable and more common choice; book ahead in the dry season, when the small supply fills up.

**Playa Larga**, about 35 km northwest at the head of the bay, is a slightly larger settlement with more casas and its own 1961 history, and works as an alternative base. **Cienfuegos**, a colonial city about 85 km east, has conventional hotels and full services and can be used for day trips if you prefer a city base and don't mind the drive.

## Costs

Cuba unified its currency on 1 January 2021, retiring the old convertible peso (CUC); the national currency is now the Cuban peso (CUP). In practice, **cash in US dollars or euros is the workable medium** for foreign visitors, and card acceptance and ATMs are unreliable in a village this small. Bring enough cash from Havana or Varadero for your whole stay: lodging, meals, the museum, and dive fees.

- **Museo Girón**: a modest entrance fee, usually with an extra charge for cameras.
- **Casas particulares**: room plus optional home-cooked meals, paid in cash.
- **Diving and snorkelling**: guided dives and gear rental at Caleta Buena and Punta Perdiz are the main paid activities; agree prices with the operator.

Exact prices shift with Cuba's volatile exchange rate, so treat any quoted figure as approximate and carry a cash buffer.

## Safety

- **The shore is sharp rock.** The coast is diente de perro, fossil-coral limestone that cuts bare feet. Wear water shoes, and expect to enter the water off rock or from fixed ladders at the dive sites rather than a sand beach.
- **No lifeguards.** There is no patrolled swimming beach. Snorkel and dive with a local operator who knows the reef and the water; do not dive unguided.
- **Currents and open water.** Conditions inside the bay are usually calm, but the water is deep close in and the reef drops off; stay within your limits and your guide's briefing.
- **Marine life.** Nurse sharks and moray eels are present but not aggressive; sea urchins and stinging coral are the real hazards, so footwear and reef distance matter. There are no recorded shark incidents here.
- **Medical and insurance.** Cuba requires visitors to carry travel medical insurance. Local medical services are limited; the nearest full hospitals are in Cienfuegos and Havana, so bring any medicines you need.
- **General.** Playa Girón is a quiet, low-crime fishing village; ordinary care with valuables is enough.

## Nearby

- **Cueva de los Peces**, a flooded sinkhole about 70 metres deep on the coast road toward Playa Larga, with a small shore and reef across the road for swimming and snorkelling.
- **Punta Perdiz** and **Caleta Buena** (about 8 km southeast), the main open-water dive and snorkel sites, both with simple day-use cafeterias.
- **Playa Larga** and the **coast-road memorial markers** naming the Cuban dead, covered in the [full Playa Girón guide](/beaches/playa-giron-1).
- The **Ciénaga de Zapata**, the largest wetland in the Caribbean and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, is major bird habitat, with flamingos, herons, and Cuban endemics best seen in the dry season.
