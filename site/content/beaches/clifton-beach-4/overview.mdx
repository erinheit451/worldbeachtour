Clifton Beach, known locally as Sea View, is Karachi's largest public beach, a stretch of the Arabian Sea shoreline about 14.6 kilometers from the city center and 18.6 kilometers from Jinnah International Airport. It serves a metropolitan area of more than 20.3 million people, recorded in Pakistan's 2023 census, and functions as the city's default civic beach rather than a resort destination.

## What's Actually Here

The sand itself is unremarkable — brown, urban, and crowded on weekends with families, camel and horse handlers, kite flyers, and food vendors. What sets Clifton apart is what sits above it: on a low sandy hill about 25 meters over the water stands the shrine of Abdullah Shah Ghazi, an 8th-century Sufi mystic Karachi has long credited with turning cyclones away from the city. That belief has hardened with each near-miss — Cyclone Phet in 2010, Nilofar in 2014, Tauktae in 2021, Biparjoy in 2023, all forecast toward Karachi before swerving off course late — an outcome meteorologists attribute to wind shear and sea-surface temperature patterns, not the shrine, though the story persists. In 2010 the shrine itself was bombed, a reminder that the belief is contested as fiercely as it is held.

## The Colonial Layer

West of the main sand, the Jehangir Kothari Parade — a stone promenade and pier funded by Parsi philanthropist Jehangir Hormasji Kothari, opened in stages between 1919 and 1921 — anchors the 130-acre Bagh Ibn-i-Qasim park, rebuilt in 2005–2007. Funland, an amusement park the Karachi Metropolitan Corporation opened next to the shrine in 1965 with rides that included dodgem cars, lost its aquarium (Machli Ghar), bowling alley, and most of its footprint to that same park redevelopment.

## The Honest Problems

Clifton is not a clean or fully safe beach. Karachi treats only a fraction of the roughly 472 million gallons of wastewater it produces daily before the rest reaches this sea, and water quality worsens in winter when currents push polluted Gizri Creek runoff onto the sand. On 30 July 2014, the second day of Eid, at least 21 people drowned at Clifton alone despite an official bathing ban the crowds ignored; there is no consistent lifeguard presence. The camel and horse rides that are one of the beach's signature activities were briefly banned by the local Cantonment Board in 2022 after an overcharging dispute, displacing roughly 100 working camels before rides gradually resumed.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History and culture** carry the most weight here — the shrine's 8th-century origins, its cyclone-steering folklore, and the 2010 bombing are the beach's defining story, deeper than any single stretch of sand.

**Travel and access** matter for the same reason most urban beaches do: Clifton is genuinely easy to reach from a major international airport and offers a real, unpolished look at how Karachi's residents actually use their coastline, camel rides, seafood at Do Darya, sunset crowds, and all.

Clifton Beach is not a swimming destination or a natural landmark in the usual sense. It is a working civic beach for one of the world's largest cities, presided over by a grave its residents still credit with keeping the water out.
