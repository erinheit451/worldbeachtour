Pandawa Beach is what the mapping data behind this page calls "Padang Beach," but every reference source — Wikipedia, Wikidata, Bali's provincial government — uses its real name: Pandawa Beach, Pantai Pandawa in Indonesian. It is a roughly 1-kilometer cove in Kutuh village, South Kuta district, on the southern tip of Bali's Bukit Peninsula, enclosed by two limestone headlands. Until 2012, there was no public road into it. The cove sat behind a limestone ridge, and the small number of outsiders who found their way in called it Secret Beach.

The road that opened the cove followed the path of an existing rock quarry cut into the hillside — English-language sources describe the access road itself as a former quarry. On the northern headland, six figures are carved directly into the cliff face: the goddess Kunti and her five sons, Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadewa, the Pandava brothers of the Mahabharata the beach is named for.

## What changed after 2012

Growth was fast and documented. A Bali tourism outlet profiled the beach as a newly rising attraction in May 2013, and Indonesian media separately credited location shoots for FTV television drama serials with helping make Pandawa a recognizable name that same year. Bali's provincial tourism office then logged 906,001 visits in 2016 and a recorded peak of 1,356,321 in 2017 — a beach almost nobody outside Kutuh had heard of a decade earlier drawing more than a million people a year. Visits held above one million through 2019, then fell 44% to 585,531 in 2020 as COVID-19 travel restrictions cut off the island.

Underneath that tourist infrastructure, the cove's shallow, sheltered water has supported seaweed algaculture for longer than it has supported paragliding concessions. Locals still work rows of cultivation line across the tidal flats at low tide, on the same water that tourists now swim in.

## The two sides of the cove

The access road ends at a parking area, and from there the beach splits. The right turn leads to the developed side: paragliding landings from Bukit Timbis above, jet ski rentals, food stalls, toilets, showers, and the bulk of the tour-bus traffic. The left turn leads to a quieter, locally run stretch near a surf break that Wikipedia describes as "not ideal" for surfing. Entry costs Rp15,000, plus Rp2,000 to Rp5,000 for parking.
