# Cable Beach Travel Guide

## Getting There

Broome International Airport (BME) is about 3.2 km from Cable Beach in a straight line. Current sourcing does not include a road distance or drive time for that route, so treat the 3.2 km figure as a straight-line measurement only, not a driving estimate. The beach itself is about 4 km west of the Broome central business district.

Vehicle access onto the beach is currently disrupted: Cable Beach's vehicle ramp has been closed since 3 June 2025 for Stage 2 of the Walmanyjun Cable Beach foreshore redevelopment, and it is not scheduled to reopen until mid/late 2026. Stage 1 of the redevelopment is complete; Stage 2 is adding a community plaza, improved event spaces, and accessibility upgrades to the shoreline. Until it reopens, there is no vehicle access point onto the beach heading toward north of the rocks — pedestrian access to that section, and to the camels, runs through the surf club beach access point only. Where vehicles are permitted (near Gantheaume Point and north of the rocks), the speed limit is 15 km/h. A gate at the Cable Beach amphitheatre is locked whenever vehicle access is not permitted, and also during turtle nesting season.

## Seasons and Timing

Broome's beaches, Cable Beach included, run on two seasons that matter for planning a visit: dry season and tropical season. During the dry season, the Broome Surf Life Saving Club and Shire of Broome run daily lifeguard patrols of the swimming area, marked by red and yellow flags. Patrols are scheduled to resume for the 2026 dry season on Good Friday, 3 April 2026. During the tropical season, usually late October to early April, the beach is not patrolled at all.

That tropical-season gap overlaps with box jellyfish (stinger) season, which the same sourcing places between November and April. Caution is specifically advised for swimmers during that window, and the lack of lifeguard patrols through most of it is worth weighing before deciding to swim rather than simply admire the water. If a visit is timed around the Staircase to the Moon — the full-moon-over-mudflats phenomenon visible from the Roebuck Bay side — that window runs March to October, with sourcing giving either two to three days a month or three nights a month, depending on which tourism source is consulted.

## Safety

Beyond the seasonal stinger risk, crocodiles have occasionally been sighted off Cable Beach's shore, which is why the Shire of Broome treats preventative measures as standard practice rather than a one-off response. Tides here can reach over nine metres, one of the larger ranges on the Australian coast, which means sandbanks and the beach's usable width change meaningfully over the course of a single day — a useful thing to know before assuming any part of the beach will look the same at the end of a visit as it did at the start.

## Camel Rides and Costs

Cable Beach's two camel tour operators, Red Sun Camels (red blankets) and Broome Camel Safaris (blue blankets), run rides around sunrise and sunset. As of 2026, both enforce a 90 kg individual weight limit; an older, undated source instead cites a 120 kg limit and a camel-train capacity of up to 36 riders at once, so treat the current 90 kg figure as the one to plan around. Current sourcing does not include prices for camel rides, resort or hotel rooms, or any other Cable Beach activity, so no cost figures are given here — check directly with the operators or the Broome Visitor Centre before budgeting a trip. A shore walk from Cable Beach to Gantheaume Point, at the beach's southern end, covers about 5 km.
