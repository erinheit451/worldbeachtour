# Strandbad Wannsee Travel Guide

Strandbad Wannsee is a lake lido on the east shore of the Großer Wannsee, a bay of the Havel river in the Nikolassee locality of southwestern Berlin. It is one of the largest inland lidos in Europe: a 1,275-metre strip of Baltic sand, about 80 metres deep, built for some 12,000 bathers and fronted by yellow clinker-brick halls that Martin Wagner and Richard Ermisch designed in 1929-1930. The water is a still, tideless freshwater lake, not the sea, and the beach is a gated public bath with a small daily admission.

## Getting There

### By Air

The nearest airport is **Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER)**, about 24 kilometres southeast of the beach. From BER, the simplest public route is rail into the Berlin network and then the S-Bahn out to Nikolassee; by car the trip is usually 35-50 minutes depending on traffic. Berlin's older airport, Tegel, closed in 2020, and all scheduled flights now use BER.

### By S-Bahn

The S-Bahn is the right way to arrive, as it has been since the 1920s. The nearest station is **Nikolassee**, served by the **S1** (Wannsee–Oranienburg) and **S7** (Potsdam–Ahrensfelde) lines, and the entrance is a walk of roughly 10 to 15 minutes from there. The neighbouring **Wannsee** station (S1, S7, and regional trains) is a short bus or longer walk away. Nikolassee sits in Berlin's fare zone B, inside the standard AB ticket area; a trip from BER crosses into zone C, so buy an ABC ticket for the airport leg.

### By Car

Wannsee is in Berlin's far southwest, reached from the city via the **A115 (AVUS)** motorway and the Kronprinzessinnenweg through the Grunewald forest. Parking near the beach is limited and fills on hot summer weekends, so the S-Bahn is usually faster and easier than driving. If you do drive, arrive early in the day in July and August.

## Best Time to Visit

The lido is an **open-air, seasonal** bath, open roughly **May to September**, with hours and dates set each year by the Berliner Bäder-Betriebe. **July and August** are the peak, when Berlin's summer highs sit in the low-to-mid 20s Celsius and the shallow lake water is at its warmest; these are also the most crowded days, when the beach can push toward its 12,000-bather capacity.

**Late May, June and September** are quieter. The lake is cooler early in the season and holds some warmth into early autumn, and the sunbathing lawn behind the sand is far less packed outside the school holidays. Outside the open-air season the beach is closed, so confirm current opening dates before planning a trip.

## Where to Stay

There are no hotels on the beach itself; Wannsee is a leafy, low-density corner of Berlin, and most visitors come for the day. Rather than name specific properties, here is the honest lay of the land.

- **Central Berlin (Mitte, Charlottenburg, City West).** Most travellers base themselves in central Berlin for the widest choice of hotels, dining and transport, and reach the beach in roughly 40 minutes to an hour by S-Bahn. This is the practical default.
- **The southwest (Zehlendorf, Wannsee, Nikolassee).** The districts around the lake are mostly residential, with a thin scatter of guesthouses and short-term rentals rather than large hotels. Staying out here puts you near the water and the Grunewald forest but far from the city centre, and supply is limited.

Availability and rates are tightest in high summer; book well ahead for July and August.

## Costs

Entry is cheap by design — this was built as a public bath for ordinary Berliners.

- **Admission:** a 2025 day ticket runs about **€6.50**, with a reduced rate of about **€3.80**; family and multi-entry summer tickets are also sold. Children under five enter free with an adult. Prices are set by the Berliner Bäder-Betriebe and change year to year, so confirm current rates.
- **Strandkorb rental:** the roofed wicker beach chairs on the sand are rentable for an extra seasonal fee.
- **Transport:** a single Berlin AB public-transport ticket is roughly **€3.80** (2025); add the C zone for the airport leg. Confirm current fares with BVG/VBB.
- **Food:** on-site kiosks sell standard open-air-pool fare (Bratwurst, Pommes, ice cream); expect a few euros per item.

A day at the beach itself is inexpensive; the main cost of a visit is usually the transport and food around it.

## Safety

- **It is a lake, so there is no tide and no surf.** The Großer Wannsee is a calm bay of the Havel; the swimming is still freshwater bathing rather than sea swimming.
- **Water quality is good.** The lake is monitored under the EU Bathing Water framework and rates as excellent quality. Water temperatures are cool in spring and warm through the summer.
- **Lifeguards are on duty during the open-air season.** The lido is a supervised public bath while it is open; there is no supervision outside operating hours, and swimming is confined to the marked bathing area.
- **A marked FKK (nudist) section** occupies one end of the beach, signposted and separated from the main family beach, as at many German lakes.
- The postwar hazard that once closed the beach — stray bullets from the adjacent American Keerans Range between 1951 and 1955 — is long gone; the range gave way to ordinary Grunewald forest decades ago.

## Nearby

- **Liebermann-Villa** — the summer villa the Impressionist painter Max Liebermann built on the same lakeshore in 1909-1910, whose garden he painted for years; it opened as a museum in 2006.
- **Pfaueninsel (Peacock Island)** — a UNESCO-listed park island in the Havel just south of the lido, reached by a short passenger ferry, with an 18th-century folly palace and free-roaming peacocks.
- **Haus der Wannsee-Konferenz** — a **separate** site on the west side of the Großer Wannsee, the villa where senior Nazi officials met in January 1942 to coordinate the Holocaust, now a memorial and education centre. It is not part of the beach and should not be confused with it; it is simply nearby on the same lake.
- **Grunewald forest and the Havel shore** — the wooded southwest of Berlin around the lido, with lakeside trails and the Havel's chain of bathing spots.
