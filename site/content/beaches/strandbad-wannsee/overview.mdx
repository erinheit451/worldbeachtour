Strandbad Wannsee is a lake lido on the east shore of the Großer Wannsee, a bay of the Havel river in the Nikolassee locality of southwestern Berlin. It is one of the largest inland lidos in Europe: a 1,275-metre strip of sand about 80 metres deep, on a site of roughly 355,000 square metres, built to hold some 12,000 bathers. The water is a still freshwater lake, not the sea, and the sand under it was imported — hauled in wagonload by wagonload from the Baltic coast at Timmendorfer Strand.

## Why this beach matters

The lido is a piece of Weimar-era social democracy built in brick and imported sand. Bathing here began in 1907 on a 200-metre shore, but the beach that survives was built in 1929-1930 by Berlin's city building councillor **Martin Wagner**, who laid out the whole ensemble as a Weltstadtbad — a bathing works for the masses — and the architect **Richard Ermisch**, who designed the long yellow clinker-brick halls in the plain New Objectivity style. The Great Depression then halved a 5-million-Reichsmark budget, so only the northern axis was built: four two-storey buildings instead of the planned ring of halls, restaurant, pier, marina and theatre. Even half-built, it drew about 1.3 million visitors in 1930.

Its history darkened with the country. Wagner, a Social Democrat, left Germany in 1935; under the Nazis, Jews were barred from public baths, a ban made explicit in 1938, and the antisemitic signs were removed only to sanitise the beach for the 1936 Olympic Games. In 1951 the lido became a national pop song, "Pack die Badehose ein," sung by eight-year-old Cornelia Froboess.

Restored in the mid-2000s and protected as a listed monument, Strandbad Wannsee runs today as a public bath under the Berliner Bäder-Betriebe, open through the warm-weather season for a few euros a day. The lake water is monitored under the EU Bathing Water framework and rates as excellent quality; there is no tide and no surf, so the swimming is calm freshwater bathing. Rows of rentable wicker Strandkörbe line the sand, a marked FKK section sits at one end, and the easiest way in is the S-Bahn to Nikolassee, as it has been since the 1920s.
