Karon Beach runs about 3.3 kilometres down the west coast of Phuket, one of the longest beaches in the province, facing the Andaman Sea between Patong to the north and Kata to the south. The town behind it is Ban Karon, and the airport is about 43 kilometres away by road (roughly 30 kilometres direct). Karon developed later and more loosely than its neighbours, and kept a wide beach road and a deep, open back-beach as a result.

## Why this beach matters

Karon's singular trait is a sound. On a dry, hot afternoon the pale sand above the waterline squeaks and barks underfoot, a real acoustic property that puts Karon among the small number of "singing sand" beaches in the world. The effect comes from the grains: global sand modelling puts Karon's sand at roughly 80 percent quartz, worn into rounded, closely sorted grains that slip in phase when a foot shears them and radiate a short, high tone. It only works dry, clean, and above the last high tide.

Karon is quieter than Patong and suits families and couples, with a long-standing Scandinavian following along the beach road. But the sea is more dangerous than the dry-season calm suggests. Karon has a strong shore break and powerful rip currents, worst on the central and southern beach, that turn lethal in the May-to-October southwest monsoon. Lifeguards fly red flags on the bad days; they are routinely ignored, and Karon recurs in Phuket's annual drowning toll, including a cluster of deaths in mid-2025.

The most sudden event in the beach's modern history was the 26 December 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami, which heavily damaged Karon's southern end. The beach was rebuilt and by 2008 showed little visible sign of it.
