# Cherry Beach

Cherry Beach is a lakeside public beach and park at the foot of Cherry Street, just south of Unwin Avenue, on the north shore of Toronto's Outer Harbour and Port Lands, east of the Eastern Gap. It was created in the early 1930s by city alderman Harry Clarke and named Clarke Beach Park after him; the City of Toronto officially renamed it Cherry Beach in 2003, adopting the name locals had already been using.

## What It Is

The beach sits on land with an unusual geological history: it was once part of a peninsula connected to the Toronto Islands, separated into its own sandbar around 1852, then further isolated when an 1858 storm carved out the harbour's Eastern Gap. That sandbar, later called Fisherman's Island, held a small community — homes, a commercial fishery, even a church — for roughly another half-century before disappearing. Waterfront Toronto's Port Lands flood-protection excavation later unearthed the 1882 breakwater that had once connected the island to the mainland, along with everyday artifacts from the lost settlement, buried for more than a century.

Today the park holds one of the only major stands of cottonwood trees in the Toronto area outside the Toronto Islands, and its water — sheltered from Lake Ontario's open surges by the Leslie Street Spit — runs calmer and slightly warmer in summer than the city's other lakeshore beaches. Cherry Beach typically earns Blue Flag certification, the international eco-label for water quality and beach management, on an annual basis.

## Who Goes

Cherry Beach draws swimmers to its Blue Flag-rated water, kiteboarders to its west end, dog walkers to its designated off-leash area, and picnickers to its barbecue sites and change rooms. The Martin Goodman Trail, a multi-use waterfront path, runs through the park between the beach and its wooded sections, connecting Cherry Beach to Toronto's broader lakeshore trail network. For most of the twentieth century the land served a different population: it was a marshy bog that was land-filled and given over to heavy industry before being handed back for public use, and the adjacent 275 Unwin Avenue site hosted FIFA-standard soccer fields — including practice sessions for Toronto FC — after its own environmental assessment found heavy contamination there.

## A Second Name

Alongside its official identity, Cherry Beach carries an unofficial one — the "Cherry Beach Express," a euphemism for alleged 1940s-1990s police brutality reported at a deserted stretch of the beach, a story significant enough that Toronto Police once tried to have a song about it banned — covered fully in the [Cherry Beach history deep dive](/beaches/cherry-beach-1/history).
