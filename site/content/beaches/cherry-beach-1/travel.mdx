# Getting to Cherry Beach

Cherry Beach sits on the north shore of Toronto's Outer Harbour, at the foot of Cherry Street just south of Unwin Avenue, east of the Eastern Gap. The straight-line distance to Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport (YTZ) is 4.3 kilometres; that figure is a straight-line measurement, not a road or transit distance, and no sourced road-distance or driving-time figure exists for this route. The beach is roughly 9.1 kilometres from downtown Toronto by the same straight-line measure.

## By Transit and Road

TTC bus service reaches Cherry Beach during the summer season, with parking available near the Martin Goodman Trail, the multi-use path that runs through the park between the beach and its wooded sections. Beyond that seasonal bus service and the trail connection, no sourced information here covers specific route numbers, driving directions, or road mileage to Cherry Beach from the airport or downtown — only the straight-line distances above are documented.

## Facilities on Site

The official facility listing for Cherry Beach (recorded under its former name, Clarke Beach Park) counts one beach, seven bike-trail segments, one off-leash dog area, two parking lots, two picnic sites, and one washroom facility. Change rooms are available for bathers and barbecue areas for picnickers. There are no named restaurants, food vendors, or concessions documented at or near the beach itself; visitors bring their own food to the picnic and barbecue areas.

## Water and Seasons

Cherry Beach's water is sheltered by the Leslie Street Spit from Lake Ontario's open-lake surges, which keeps it calmer and slightly warmer in summer than the city's other lakeshore beaches. Wave data for the site puts the annual mean wave height at 0.51 metres, with January the roughest month (0.71 m average, up to 1.46 m on the biggest days recorded) and July the calmest (0.24 m average) — in short, a lake beach whose most swimmable water lines up with its warmest months, not a location with meaningful surf in any season.

The City of Toronto's own beach-water-quality page, as fetched in 2026, states that daily water-quality results will not resume until the summer of 2027; anyone checking conditions before a visit should account for that gap rather than assume current daily postings are running. Separately, the city advises against swimming at Cherry Beach, along with its other beaches, for 24 to 48 hours after heavy rain, because of contaminant runoff.

## Safety and What's Not Confirmed Here

No sourced information here covers lifeguard staffing or supervised hours at Cherry Beach, current parking or facility-rental prices, or recent (post-2020) renovation work to the entrance, washrooms, or change rooms, even though some renovation appears to have been underway according to older references. Anyone planning a visit around those specifics should check current City of Toronto sources directly rather than rely on this page.

What is documented: a 2012 environmental assessment found lead and zinc above guidelines in the beach's soil, tied to the site's industrial past — worth knowing even though it hasn't stopped the beach from typically earning Blue Flag certification most years. That contamination history, and the beach's separate, unofficial nickname tied to alleged police brutality, are covered in the [full Cherry Beach history](/beaches/cherry-beach-1/history).
