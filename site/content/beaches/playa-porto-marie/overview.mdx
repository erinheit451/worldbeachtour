Playa Porto Marie is a private, fee-gated beach on Curaçao's southwest coast, in the Bandabou district near the village of Sint Willibrordus. It occupies a plantation bay that changed hands by force twice in its documented history — once in 1795, when rebels in the largest slave revolt in Curaçao's history routed the Dutch militia sent to retake it, and once in 1805, when English forces destroyed the fort the Dutch had built there. Neither event is mentioned at the gate today, where entry costs Nafl. 5.00 (about $3) per person plus $3.50 for a beach chair.

## What's actually here

The beach itself is a narrow, developed strip: rental chairs, a viewing tower, and an on-site restaurant open 9:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m. daily (closed 31 December and Carnival Sunday), ranked #8 of 211 restaurants in Curaçao on TripAdvisor. The draw for most visitors is offshore — a shore-accessible double-reef system locally called "the Valley," with two coral walls at roughly 15 and 18 meters split by a sandy channel, and visibility that regularly exceeds 30 meters. Hundreds of artificial reef blocks have been sunk in recent years to rebuild coral cover that tourism and runoff had degraded.

The dataset behind this page models the sand as 93.6% quartz by predicted composition (GloPrSM v1.0.0), a regime consistent with a beach fed by weathered coral limestone and terrigenous sediment rather than the biogenic white sand common on Curaçao's more sheltered coves. Wave data from Open-Meteo's ERA5 marine reanalysis (2023–2024) puts the annual mean wave height at 1.12 m, with July the roughest month (1.25 m mean) and October the calmest (0.91 m) — a mixed, usually swimmable profile typical of this stretch of coast.

## The history underneath it

Before it was a beach club, Porto Marie was a working 593-hectare dairy plantation, established by 1704 on land where cattle farming had been permitted since 1684, with roughly seventy dwellings for enslaved and free laborers. The Dutch fortified the bay in 1742. On 19 August 1795 — two days into the revolt Tula led off Knip plantation — a 67-man Dutch militia sailed here to crush the rebel camp and was routed instead, driven off with no surviving record of its own losses — the sharpest tactical defeat the colonial government suffered in the six-week uprising. English forces destroyed the fort ten years later, in 1805. That history, and what it has to do with a modern pay-to-enter beach, is covered in full in the [history deep dive](/beaches/playa-porto-marie/history).

## Most relevant lenses

**History** — The 1795 Battle of Porto Marie and the plantation that preceded it are the strongest reason this beach is more than a dive stop; see the dedicated history page.

**Diving** — The Valley's double-reef structure is shore-accessible and consistently cited among Curaçao's better dive sites for visibility and ease of entry.

**Travel** — As a private, fee-gated beach with no resort development on-site, Porto Marie requires planning around opening hours, cash for the gate, and a car or taxi to reach it; see the [travel guide](/beaches/playa-porto-marie/travel).
