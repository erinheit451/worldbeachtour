# Playa Porto Marie Travel Guide

Playa Porto Marie is a private, fee-gated beach in Curaçao's Bandabou district, on the island's southwest coast near Sint Willibrordus. There's no resort on-site and no public transit to the gate; getting here means a car or a taxi, and getting in means Nafl. 5.00 (about $3) in cash or card at the entrance, plus $3.50 for a chair if you want one.

**By air.** Hato International Airport (CUR) is Curaçao's only commercial airport. The straight-line distance to Porto Marie is 14.2 km, but the coast road adds distance the straight line doesn't show: the actual drive is roughly 19 km and about 23 minutes, curving around Bullen Bay rather than cutting straight across the interior.

**By car.** From Willemstad, the drive is about 25 km and takes 30–45 minutes depending on traffic, heading northwest along the island's west-coast road toward Sint Willibrordus. A rental car is the practical option — there's no bus route to the beach itself, and the nearest scheduled public transport stop is Sint Willibrordus village, about 2 miles (3 km) inland. Specific rental-car or taxi rate figures for this route weren't independently verified for this guide; check current listings from agencies at Hato before budgeting.

**By boat.** The bay is a working dive and snorkel entry point, not a marina; there's no ferry or scheduled water-taxi service to Porto Marie.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Curaçao's climate doesn't follow the wider Caribbean's June–November hurricane-season pattern in the way many visitors expect. The island lies outside the tropical cyclone Main Development Region, and its wettest months are October through December, not summer — annual rainfall averages only about 450 mm (18 in), making Curaçao genuinely arid rather than tropically wet. Average temperatures run about 25.7°C (78°F) year-round, from a coolest-month average near 26.6°C (80°F) in January to a hottest-month average near 29.1°C (84°F) in September; day-to-day swings are small.

The wave data behind this page (Open-Meteo ERA5 marine reanalysis, 2023–2024) shows Porto Marie's own conditions are mixed but usually swimmable: an annual mean wave height of 1.12 m, with July running roughest (1.25 m mean, up to 1.68 m on big days) and October calmest (0.91 m mean). There's no separate seasonal crowd or pricing data specific to this beach; the general Curaçao tourism pattern of higher demand around December–January and Easter week is the closest available proxy, not a verified figure for Porto Marie itself.

## Where to Stay

There's no accommodation on the beach itself. The nearest village, Sint Willibrordus, is a small settlement (588 residents as of the 2011 census) with limited lodging; most visitors base in Willemstad, about 25 km / 30–45 minutes away by road, where the bulk of the island's hotels, guesthouses, and vacation rentals are concentrated. No verified nightly-rate figures for either area are included here — check current listings rather than relying on general Caribbean price assumptions, which vary significantly by season and property type.

Beyond the gate fee and chair rental, Porto Marie has one on-site restaurant (first-come, first-served, no reservations) and dive/snorkel rental facilities; no further verified pricing for meals, equipment rental, or guided dives at this specific location was found for this guide. Curaçao's legal position is that beaches must remain publicly accessible, and Porto Marie's fee is part of a live, ongoing political argument on the island about whether private operators should be allowed to charge admission to the shoreline at all.

## Safety

The dataset behind this page records zero recorded shark incidents at Playa Porto Marie. Curaçao sits outside the Atlantic's main hurricane development region, so direct storm strikes are rare — Hurricane Ivan passed north of the island in September 2004, still causing roof damage from high winds and prompting roughly 300 residents to evacuate, the most-cited recent near-miss. The dataset's own 50-year tropical-cyclone track count logs 11 storm passages near the island, up to Category 5 strength, a methodology based on tracks passing within range of the coordinates rather than confirmed direct hits.

Reef etiquette matters here specifically: the Valley's double-reef system and its artificial reef-restoration blocks are the reason most visitors come, and standing on or handling coral damages both the natural reef and the restoration effort. There's no lifeguard service documented for this private beach; swimming and diving are at the visitor's own responsibility, as is standard for Curaçao's smaller beaches.

## Local Tips

**It's not a swim-up bar beach.** Porto Marie is built around diving and snorkeling access, not nightlife; hours end at 6:30 p.m. and there's no late-night infrastructure.

**Bring cash or a card that works locally.** The gate fee is quoted in Antillean guilders (Nafl.) with a dollar equivalent; don't assume every small beach operator on this coast takes every card type.

**Westpunt is close enough for a meal detour.** Further along the same Bandabou coast road sits Jaanchie's, a roughly 60-year-old, three-generation family restaurant and the only restaurant on Curaçao licensed to serve iguana stew — a real, verifiable stop if you're exploring the west coast beyond the beach itself.

**The history isn't posted anywhere on-site.** If the plantation and the 1795 battle interest you, read the [history deep dive](/beaches/playa-porto-marie/history) before you go — nothing at the gate will tell you about it.
