Woodbine Beach is a 15.2-hectare (38-acre) city-run sand beach and park on Toronto's Lake Ontario waterfront, at the foot of Woodbine Avenue, south of Lake Shore Boulevard, in the Beaches neighbourhood. It is the largest beach in Toronto. Ashbridge's Bay sits immediately to its west; Kew-Balmy Beach continues the sand to its east. Together the three once formed what residents still call the eastern shoreline of old Toronto.

The beach is younger than its neighbourhood's history. Before Lake Shore Boulevard was extended to Woodbine Avenue in the 1950s, this stretch was a wooded area nicknamed "the cut," not a bathing beach. The park itself was created in 1950. Everything a visitor sees today, the sand, the courts, the boardwalk, dates to decisions made in the decades since, not to some older natural shoreline.

What people actually come for is volleyball. Woodbine has roughly 95 sand courts, and the courts are the main reason the beach reads as a destination rather than a quiet local strip of sand. The Donald D. Summerville Outdoor Olympic Pool sits just behind the beach, built starting in 1961 with scaffolding designed for temporary spectator seating in case Toronto ever hosted the Olympics; it never did, but the pool remained. The Martin Goodman Trail runs along the boardwalk parallel to the shoreline, connecting Woodbine on foot or by bike to the rest of Toronto's waterfront path network.

The beach has held Blue Flag certification, the international mark for water quality and swimming safety, since 2005. The city says water and sand quality improved dramatically starting in 1990, with daily water testing and daily machine-grooming of the sand since. Lifeguards work the beach throughout the summer. None of that is glamorous, but it is the ordinary maintenance record of a heavily used public beach, and it is a real one.

Who actually goes there is mostly Toronto itself. Local coverage describes Woodbine as rowdier and busier than calmer city beaches like Sunnyside or Cherry Beach, a reputation earned by the volleyball crowd and the boardwalk traffic rather than by any single event. The beach and neighbouring Ashbridge's Bay are also a popular spot to watch the Victoria Day fireworks each May, one of the few dates when the crowd has a specific reason to be there beyond the water and the sand.

The beach's day-to-day management is not entirely a public affair, though: the only food-and-alcohol concession on Woodbine has been held by one family for more than two decades, and most of the volleyball courts require a paid membership rather than open public use. That story, and the 2008 waterfront master plan that stalled alongside it, is told in full in [the beach's history](/beaches/woodbine-beach/history) rather than here.
