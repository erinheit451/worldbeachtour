Woodbine Beach sits at the foot of Woodbine Avenue on Lake Shore Blvd. East, in Toronto's Beaches neighbourhood, about 9.8 km east of downtown Toronto and 8.1 km from Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport (YTZ), both straight-line distances rather than road or transit distances. No source used for this page gives a road distance or transit time to either downtown or an airport, so plan travel time around transit schedules and traffic rather than the straight-line figures above.

## Getting there

The 501 Queen streetcar and the 92 Woodbine bus both reach the beach directly, and on busy summer weekends both run packed. The Ashbridges Bay Park parking lots, the beach's main lot area, fill up by 10 a.m. on warm weekend mornings. There is one public parking lot at the beach itself, on Lake Shore Blvd. East, with free or low-rate parking depending on the time and day; it is genuinely hard to find a spot in during peak hours. On foot or by bike, the Martin Goodman Trail runs along the boardwalk and connects Woodbine to the rest of Toronto's waterfront path network, useful if you're coming from elsewhere on the lakefront rather than by transit.

## Seasons and water

Lake Ontario's wave conditions at Woodbine run calm nearly year-round, with an annual mean wave height around 0.57 m; the calmest month is July, averaging about 0.27 m, and the roughest is January, averaging about 0.8 m with bigger days reaching up to 1.56 m. Swimming season is the summer months, when lifeguards are present at the beach throughout. Water temperature is not simply a function of air temperature here: strong westerly winds can trigger upwelling, pulling cold deep-lake water to the surface and dropping the water into the lower teens Celsius for days even during a heat wave. A 2010 newspaper profile put the average July water temperature at 15°C, cooler than the mid-20s Celsius sometimes cited for late summer, so pack a plan for a colder swim than the calendar suggests.

## Safety

Woodbine and neighbouring Kew-Balmy Beach have held Blue Flag certification, the international standard for water quality and swimming safety, since 2005. The city has run daily water testing and daily machine-grooming of the sand since a marked improvement in conditions that it dates to 1990. Lifeguards work the beach throughout summer. There are no recorded shark incidents at this beach. The one recurring complaint in local coverage is a periodic bad smell from a nearby sewage treatment plant when the wind blows the wrong way; it is unpleasant, not a health closure, but worth knowing about before you plan a picnic close to the water on a still, onshore-wind day.

## Costs

There is no entrance fee to Woodbine Beach itself. Parking is the main cost variable: the single public lot on Lake Shore Blvd. East charges free or low rates depending on time and day, though no source gives an exact figure. Sand volleyball is the beach's signature activity, and it is not entirely free: only 10 of the roughly 95 courts are open to the public without charge, while the rest are run by the Ontario Volleyball Association and require an adult league membership starting at $45 for the summer. The only restaurant confirmed at the beach in the research for this page, the Boardwalk Pub, was charging $11 for gourmet burgers and fries as of a 2010 profile; no current pricing or confirmation that it still operates is available, so budget for Queen Street East's restaurants a couple of blocks north instead if you want a confirmed, current option.
