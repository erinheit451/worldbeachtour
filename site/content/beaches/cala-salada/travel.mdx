# Cala Salada Travel Guide

Cala Salada sits on Ibiza's west coast in the municipality of Sant Antoni de Portmany. Distances to it vary by source: Wikipedia gives 4.7 km (2.9 mi) north of Sant Antoni town; the Spain Film Commission gives 4 km. Both are straight-line figures, not driving distances — treat them accordingly unless a source specifically says "driving."

## Getting There

### By Air

Most visitors fly into Ibiza Airport (IBZ). No verified straight-line or road distance, and no drive time, from the airport to Cala Salada turned up in our research — treat any specific "X km" or "X minutes from the airport" claim you see elsewhere with caution; it isn't sourced here.

### By Car

Barceló, a hotel operator's visitor guide, gives a route from Ibiza Town (the island capital, not the airport): about 20 km via the C-731 and PM-812 roads, roughly 25 minutes' driving. The road down to the beach itself ends at a small, shaded car park with 65 spaces, with stairs down to the sand.

### By Public Transport (it does exist)

One widely read visitor guide, Barceló, states flatly that there is no public transport to Cala Salada and recommends a taxi instead. That claim is contradicted by Sant Antoni's own municipal program, which has run continuously since 2016: a bus every 15 minutes in both directions, with a first departure from Sant Antoni at 9:30 and a last departure from the beach at 20:30, plus a port boat costing 9 euros round trip for adults and 4.5 euros for children aged 7 to 12 (those are the earliest fares on record, from the 2016-17 rollout; no updated 2026-season price list was found). A later minibus line, P7, runs every 30 minutes from 9:30 to 20:30 with a park-and-ride stop at the Can Coix sports centre. The full contradiction, and what it implies about how these guides are researched, is covered in the [access-control history deep dive](/beaches/cala-salada/history).

## Vehicle Access Restrictions

Two separate control measures apply here, and they are not the same program.

From 1 June to 30 September, under the original "Playa Conectada" mobility scheme, private cars are restricted on the access road between 10:00 and 18:00 — the bus and boat above exist specifically to substitute for that.

Since 2023, a second and separate paid checkpoint has also gated the road, at a cost of 113,176.02 euros a year (VAT included) under a contract that, as reported in 2024, runs two years with an option to extend for two more. In the 2026 season it operates daily, without interruption, from 9:30 to 17:00. The stated reason is not crowd control for its own sake: the town hall classifies the Cala Salada/Cala Saladeta zone as an area of extremely high wildfire risk, and the checkpoint keeps the road passable for emergency vehicles. Motorcycles and bicycles, vehicles for people with reduced mobility, taxis, and local residents or workers are exempt from the restriction; every other private car is turned back during checkpoint hours.

In 2024 the town hall also built a signposted pedestrian path, about 500 metres long, connecting the first car park to the beach, so people arriving on foot are not forced onto the road itself.

## Costs

Cala Salada's own facilities carry no fee mentioned in any source used for this page. Beyond that, the only verified prices on record are the 2016-17 boat fares above; nothing more current for parking, the bus, or the boat was found, so treat any specific 2026 price you see elsewhere as unverified.

## Food

There is one restaurant, at a corner of Cala Salada, popular with locals for seafood paella; a separate description of the same restaurant mentions an Ibizan-style menu with rice dishes, lobster stew, and grilled fish and meat, run as a family business. Its actual name, full menu, and current prices were not retrievable — both its dedicated listing pages returned access errors during research. Cala Saladeta, the sibling cove next door, has no food service of its own.

## Safety

Facilities at Cala Salada include sunbeds, parasols, a restaurant, lifeguards, toilets and disabled access; showers are installed but turned off to save water. Our research did not turn up exact lifeguard staffing numbers or a precise lifeguard season calendar — sources confirm lifeguards operate in season but give no headcount or dates.

The extreme wildfire-risk classification behind the vehicle checkpoint is the clearest safety fact on record for this specific stretch of coast, and it is the reason the access road is actively controlled rather than left open. On water quality: our geodata lists this stretch as "1 - excellent," and the EU's 2025 bathing-water report rated 85% of roughly 22,000 monitored coastal sites across the bloc "excellent" — but a station-specific classification for Cala Salada's own monitoring point could not be retrieved from the public EU map, so treat the "excellent" label as a general regional signal rather than a site-specific lab result.

## Local Tips

Distinguish Cala Salada from Cala Saladeta before you go: they're divided by the Casa Rosada building, and only Cala Salada has the restaurant, lifeguards, toilets and parking. If you're relying on public transport, don't take one guidebook's word that none exists — Sant Antoni's own bus and boat program has run every summer since 2016. And if you're driving in season, expect the road itself to be actively controlled, not just busy: between the 1 June-30 September car restriction and the separate checkpoint contract running since 2023, this is one of the more actively managed access roads on the island.
