Treasure Beach is not one beach but five coves strung along Jamaica's south coast in St. Elizabeth Parish: Fort Charles Bay, Billy's Bay, Frenchman's Bay, Calabash Bay, and Great (Pedro) Bay. It takes its name from the Treasure Beach Hotel, built in the early 1900s by Canadian Ernest George Hamilton Dicker at the invitation of the local Senior family. Density restrictions have kept large hotels out ever since; there are no all-inclusive resorts here, hotels stay small, and the beaches are all public.

## A fishing village that became a cultural anchor

The area's modern identity runs through one family. Sally Henzell bought the Calabash Bay property in 1991 and opened Jake's as a restaurant in 1993; her husband, Perry Henzell, directed The Harder They Come (1972), Jamaica's first feature film and the movie most credited with introducing reggae to an international audience. Henzell died at a relative's home in Treasure Beach in November 2006. Their daughter Justine Henzell co-founded the Calabash International Literary Festival with Colin Channer and Kwame Dawes in 2001; the festival has since hosted Salman Rushdie, Zadie Smith, and Marlon James, among others, on a stage a short walk from the fishing boats.

That same year, 2001, fisherman Floyd Forbes built a driftwood-and-plank bar on a sandbar a mile off Parrottee Point, naming it for the pelicans that gather there. Hurricane Ivan flattened it in 2004; Forbes rebuilt it. Locals with pale skin, light eyes, or red hair are called "red" or "browning," a trait travel writers attribute to a Scottish shipwreck — genealogists who have studied the parish records say the actual ancestry is more complicated, running through Taino, Spanish, and slavery-era British and African lines.

## Hurricane Melissa, and what nine months of recovery looks like

On 28 October 2025, Hurricane Melissa came ashore near New Hope in the neighboring parish of Westmoreland with sustained winds of 185 mph and a landfall pressure of 897 millibars — the strongest hurricane on record to strike Jamaica, surpassing 1988's Hurricane Gilbert. St. Elizabeth Parish recorded 18 of the storm's 95 regional deaths. Aerial surveys between Treasure Beach and Black River found 80 to 90 percent of roofs destroyed, and at Calabash Bay Fishing Beach the storm surge stripped the sand down to bare stone and wrecked the boats, ice houses, and processing sheds the local fishing economy runs on. Floyd's Pelican Bar was destroyed a second time.

The response was fast at the community level. BREDS – Treasure Beach Foundation, the nonprofit Jason Henzell co-founded in 1998, became a hub for relief distribution; on 23 December 2025, about 200 farmers and fisherfolk received more than J$7 million in recovery supplies and equipment at the BREDS Foundation Complex. By late April 2026, the Jamaica Gleaner reported the town roughly 98 percent restored — sand naturally returning to Calabash Bay, seawalls rebuilt piece by piece, fishermen slowly working their way back out to sea. Floyd's Pelican Bar reopened in February 2026, rebuilt again by former staff and volunteers.

## What's actually here now

Treasure Beach remains what it has been for decades: a working fishing community with no resort strip, a literary festival that punches well above the town's size, and — as of this writing — visible, ongoing recovery from the worst storm in Jamaica's recorded history. The sand has come back faster than some of the infrastructure has.
