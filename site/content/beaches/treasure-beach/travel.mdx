# Treasure Beach Travel Guide

Treasure Beach is a working fishing community on Jamaica's south coast in St. Elizabeth Parish, kept deliberately undeveloped by local density restrictions that keep out all-inclusive resorts. As of this writing, it is also nine months into recovering from Hurricane Melissa, the strongest hurricane on record to strike Jamaica, which made landfall in the neighboring parish on 28 October 2025. Most of the visible damage has been rebuilt, but this is a working recovery, not a finished one — expect a genuine fishing village rather than a resort town, and check current conditions with your accommodation before you go.

## Getting There

**By Air**

There is no airport in Treasure Beach. Sangster International Airport (MBJ) in Montego Bay is the usual gateway; it sits roughly 72 km from Treasure Beach in a straight line, but the drive along the South Coast Highway runs closer to 130 km and takes 2 to 2.5 hours depending on traffic and road conditions. Norman Manley International Airport (KIN) in Kingston is a comparable drive from the other side of the island. Neither route is direct — expect winding two-lane road for most of it.

**By Car**

Rental cars are available at both airports. From Montego Bay, the South Coast Highway runs south and east through Whitehouse and Black River before reaching Treasure Beach; from Kingston, it runs west through Mandeville and Santa Cruz. Driving is on the left. Roads in St. Elizabeth were significantly damaged by Hurricane Melissa in October 2025; while most primary routes have been repaired as part of the broader south-coast recovery, some secondary roads may still show storm damage. Driving after dark is not recommended on unfamiliar rural roads.

**By Bus**

Knutsford Express runs a South Coast route with stops in Mandeville, Santa Cruz, Black River, Savanna-la-Mar, and Negril, plus a dedicated stop in Black River on the Luana–Sandy Ground Road. From any of these stops, a local taxi covers the final leg into Treasure Beach.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

St. Elizabeth's south coast is drier than Jamaica's north side. The dry season, roughly November through April, is the most reliable window for beach weather and also brings the Atlantic hurricane season to a close by the end of November — worth noting given Hurricane Melissa's late-October 2025 landfall. The wet season runs May through October, with the heaviest rain typically August through October, overlapping the peak of hurricane season (June to November). The Calabash International Literary Festival, when it runs, is held in May; the 2023 and 2025 editions both went ahead, though the schedule for the next edition had not been announced as of this writing.

## Where to Stay

Treasure Beach has no large resort hotels by design — density restrictions rule them out. Accommodation is family guesthouses, small independent inns, and vacation rentals, concentrated around Calabash Bay and Frenchman's Bay. Jake's Hotel, built up from Sally Henzell's 1993 restaurant and still run by the Henzell family, is the closest thing the area has to a landmark property, and remains small and colorfully built by design rather than scale. Southfield, the nearest inland town, and Black River, the parish capital about 15 km west, both offer additional basic lodging if Treasure Beach proper is full.

## Costs

<DataCard label="Beach Access" value="Free" unit="all five coves are public" />

Treasure Beach is not a budget backpacker circuit and not a luxury enclave either — expect mid-range Jamaican pricing. Guesthouse rooms run from the low tens of thousands of Jamaican dollars per night up to boutique rates at Jake's; meals built around the local catch at places like Jake's Restaurant or the fish stalls at Calabash Bay Fishing Beach cost less than equivalent meals on the resort-heavy north coast. Boat trips out to Floyd's Pelican Bar are arranged locally and priced per boat rather than per person, so costs drop with a bigger group.

## Safety

<DataCard label="Shark Incidents" value="0" unit="recorded" />

There are no lifeguards on any of Treasure Beach's five coves; ask locally about currents before swimming, particularly outside the more sheltered Frenchman's Bay. The bigger practical safety issue as of this writing is storm recovery: this coast has logged 19 tropical cyclones within 50 nautical miles over the past 50 years, and Hurricane Melissa's October 2025 landfall was the first to reach Category 5. Rebuilding has reached roughly 98 percent by the Jamaica Gleaner's April 2026 accounting, but road conditions, power reliability, and business hours can still be inconsistent in ways a resort destination wouldn't have. Confirm specifics with your accommodation rather than assuming pre-2025 conditions.

## Local Tips

**Go to Calabash Bay Fishing Beach at dawn.** This is when the boats that survived Hurricane Melissa, and the newer ones that have replaced what didn't, launch for the day — the clearest daily evidence of a fishing economy still working its way back to normal.

**Book a boat to Floyd's Pelican Bar in advance.** The bar, rebuilt in February 2026 after Melissa destroyed it, is only reachable by boat from Treasure Beach; arrange transport through your accommodation rather than trying to find one on the beach.

**If the Calabash Festival is running, plan around it.** It is a three-day event that fills the area's limited guesthouse rooms; book well ahead if your visit falls in a festival year.

**Ask before assuming a business is open.** Nine months after a Category 5 storm, some smaller operations are still rebuilding capacity even where the beach itself looks fully recovered.
