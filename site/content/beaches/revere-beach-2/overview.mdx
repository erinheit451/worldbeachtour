Revere Beach opened to the public on July 12, 1896, with the Metropolitan Park Commission taking over management that October 1. Landscape architect Charles Eliot's redesign moved the Boston, Revere Beach & Lynn Railroad off the sand and cleared hundreds of private bathhouses that had crowded the shore since the railroad first opened it to Boston crowds in 1875. The result, 84 acres running just over three miles along Massachusetts Bay, is generally credited as the first public beach in the United States, and it became a National Historic Landmark on May 27, 2003.

## What Is Actually There Now

Almost none of the beach's amusement-park reputation survives on the ground. Wonderland Amusement Park, which opened at the north end in 1906, went bankrupt in the Panic of 1907 and closed for good after 1910. The Cyclone roller coaster, built in 1925 and the world's tallest coaster until 1964, burned twice and was demolished in 1974. A February 1978 blizzard destroyed the seawall and what structures remained. When the beach reopened in its current, revitalized form in May 1992, it was backed by condominium towers rather than dance halls and coaster scaffolding.

The north end kept getting reused. Wonderland Greyhound Park's own history says its ground briefly became a bicycle track after the amusement park failed, then in 1935 the greyhound racetrack itself; the amusement park's own Wikipedia record disputes that continuity, placing the amusement park across Route 1A instead. What's undisputed is that Wonderland Greyhound Park ran live dog races for 75 years until Massachusetts voters banned the sport statewide and the track closed in 2010. The City of Revere took the vacant parcel by eminent domain in November 2022 to build a new Revere High School; the former owner is contesting the city's valuation in court, and construction broke ground in August 2025. Today's visitor sees a beach flanked by an active school construction site at one end and residential condominiums along most of the rest of the boulevard.

## What's Reliable Here

**Transit access.** Three MBTA Blue Line stations, Wonderland, Revere Beach, and Beachmont, sit directly on the beach, making it one of the more transit-accessible ocean beaches near a major American city.

**Food.** Kelly's Roast Beef has operated from the same address on the boulevard, 410 Revere Beach Boulevard, since 1951; it is the beach's one long-standing, verifiable food landmark rather than a boardwalk food scene, which no longer exists.

**Water testing.** Massachusetts checks four points along the shore, Oak Island, the bathhouse/state police station, Beach Street, and Point of Pines, for Enterococcus bacteria weekly through the summer.

**One big annual draw.** The International Sand Sculpting Festival, organized by the Revere Beach Partnership since 2004, brings international sculptors and an estimated one million visitors to the beach over a single summer weekend.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History** — The Wonderland district's three lives (amusement park, greyhound track, contested eminent-domain school site) and the beach's own 1896 founding, itself a government land-clearing action, are the strongest, most specific story here.

**Travel** — Direct MBTA Blue Line access from Boston, a straightforward Route 1A drive from Logan Airport, and a beach that is genuinely free and low-cost compared with most East Coast ocean destinations.
