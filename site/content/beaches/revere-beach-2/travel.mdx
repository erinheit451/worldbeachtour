# Revere Beach Travel Guide

Revere Beach opened to the public on July 12, 1896, and is generally credited as the first public beach in the United States. It sits roughly five kilometers north of Boston Logan International Airport as the crow flies and is served directly by three MBTA Blue Line stations, which makes it one of the more transit-accessible ocean beaches near a major U.S. city. The amusement-park boardwalk that once defined the beach is gone; what's left is a residential boulevard, a construction site where a new high school is going up on the former Wonderland Greyhound Park land, and a beach that is free to use year-round.

## Getting There

<DataCard label="Nearest City" value="Revere" unit="1.7 km away, straight-line" />

**By air.** Boston Logan International Airport is the nearest major airport. The dataset's 5.3 km figure is a straight-line distance; the actual road route runs via Route 1A North and covers closer to 7-8 km, typically 15-20 minutes outside rush hour.

**By transit.** The MBTA Blue Line runs directly from Boston through Beachmont and Revere Beach stations to its Wonderland terminus, all three on the beach itself. From the airport, Blue Line service connects via the Airport station with a transfer; total travel time is commonly 20-30 minutes depending on connections. Blue Line service ends around 1 a.m.

**By car.** Revere Beach Boulevard (Route 1A) runs the length of the beach and is the primary access road. Street parking is metered and fills on summer weekends; the beach's own website and city parking signage should be checked for current rates, since these change and are not part of any dataset here.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Wave data from Open-Meteo's marine reanalysis (2023-2024 period) characterizes conditions here as "mixed — usually swimmable, with a livelier season": the annual mean wave height is 0.61 m, with March the biggest month at a 0.89 m mean (peak recorded day 1.56 m) and July the calmest at 0.34 m. That means the roughest water tends to arrive outside the summer swimming season, and the calmest water coincides with peak beach crowds, a favorable pairing for casual swimming in July and August.

Revere Beach runs roughly north-south with open water to the east, so it is a sunrise beach rather than a sunset one; early morning light comes straight off Massachusetts Bay, and the boulevard's condominium towers are what catches the last light at day's end, not the water.

The beach's single largest scheduled draw is the International Sand Sculpting Festival, organized by the Revere Beach Partnership since 2004, which draws an estimated one million visitors over a summer weekend; exact dates vary by year and are set closer to the season. Revere Beach PRIDE has run every June since 2022, following the city's first Pride flag raising over City Hall on June 12, 2020.

## Where to Stay

Direct beachfront lodging along Revere Beach Boulevard is limited compared with resort destinations; the boulevard itself is mostly residential and condominium housing rather than a hotel strip. Travelers commonly base themselves in downtown Boston or in East Boston, both a single Blue Line ride from the beach's three stations, and commute in rather than staying beachside. Given the transit access, proximity to a hotel is less important here than proximity to a Blue Line station.

Revere Beach itself costs nothing to visit. Kelly's Roast Beef, the beach's one long-standing food landmark, has sold roast beef sandwiches and lobster rolls from 410 Revere Beach Boulevard since 1951; beyond it, dining along the boulevard is limited to a small number of fixed addresses rather than a boardwalk vendor strip, since the amusement-era food stalls closed with the rest of the district decades ago.

## Safety

**Swimming and water quality.** Massachusetts tests four points along the shore, Oak Island, the bathhouse/state police station, Beach Street, and Point of Pines, for Enterococcus bacteria weekly through summer; advisories can follow heavy rain. The dataset used for this guide records zero shark incidents historically at this beach.

**Storms.** This coastline has real storm exposure. A February 1978 blizzard destroyed Revere Beach's seawall and sidewalks outright, and on July 28, 2014, an EF2 tornado tore through Chelsea and into Revere, damaging City Hall, the first tornado recorded in Suffolk County since the National Weather Service began keeping records in 1950. Check forecasts before a visit during hurricane season (roughly August-November) or winter nor'easter season.

**Construction.** The former Wonderland Greyhound Park site at the beach's north end is an active construction zone for the new Revere High School, with groundbreaking in August 2025; expect fencing and possible access changes near Wonderland station for the duration of the build.

## Local Notes

**It is not a resort beach.** The boardwalk, the rides, and the dance halls that once made Revere Beach a destination in its own right are gone; what remains is a public beach embedded in a working residential city of about 62,000 people (2020 census), one of the fastest-growing in Massachusetts.

**The MBTA is the practical choice.** With three stations directly on the beach and metered, limited street parking, transit is usually faster and cheaper than driving on a summer weekend.

**Sunrise, not sunset.** Because the beach faces east, plan golden-hour visits for early morning rather than evening if watching the light over the water is the goal.
