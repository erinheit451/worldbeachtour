# Bang Saen Beach Travel Guide

Bang Saen is Chonburi Province's Thai-domestic weekend beach on the Gulf of Thailand, about 108 km southeast of Bangkok and roughly 30 km north of Pattaya. It draws Bangkok weekenders rather than international package tourists, and the sourced material for this guide is narrower than a typical resort write-up: several figures common in beach-travel content -- specific driving times, taxi fares, hotel rates, and food prices -- could not be independently verified for Bang Saen and are left out rather than estimated.

## Getting There

**By air.** Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) is 50.3 km from Bang Saen in a straight line. That figure is a straight-line distance, not a road distance, and no sourced road driving time or taxi fare exists for the airport-to-beach route -- treat any specific minutes-or-baht figure you see elsewhere as an estimate, not a sourced fact.

**By road.** The corridor most visitors use is Motorway 7, Thailand's first intercity motorway: 149.3 km total, with a 125.865 km Bangkok-to-Pattaya section, a 120 km/h speed limit, and nine tollgates. It originates at the Sri Nagarindra Interchange in Bangkok's Suan Luang district and runs southeast through Samut Prakan, Chachoengsao, and Chonburi toward Rayong; a Pong-to-Map Ta Phut extension opened in May 2020. Toll examples by vehicle class: at the Lat Krabang gate, 25 THB (4-wheel), 45 THB (6-wheel), or 60 THB (over 6-wheel); at the Pattaya gate, those rates rise to 105, 170, and 245 THB respectively.

## When to Go

Locals report that the sea runs its clearest from October through February, a clarity some compare to the Maldives -- a claim carried in local sourcing, not backed by an independent measurement. ## Safety

The Gulf of Thailand's wave data at Bang Saen is calm by any standard -- well under half a meter on average, all year -- The site's own dataset records no shark incident at Bang Saen and two tropical cyclones affecting the area in the last 50 years, both at tropical-storm intensity.

Beyond that, the record thins out fast. No official source or dated press report confirms lifeguard staffing, rip-current risk, or formal beach-closure protocols specific to Bang Saen. A search-engine summary claimed Bang Saen has posted jellyfish warning signs, but no Bang Saen-specific source corroborates it -- only generic Thailand-wide box-jellyfish guidance exists, and that claim is not repeated here as fact.

The water itself is not always calm to look at, even when it is calm to swim in. On November 7, 2025, the shoreline turned visibly black after storm runoff from the Bang Pakong River mixed with the seawater; vendors reported no dead fish or bad smell and expected it to clear within days, but no water-quality data was cited at the time. Anyone concerned about conditions after heavy rain should ask locally before swimming, since no public water-quality monitoring for Bang Saen specifically was found in the research for this page.

## What's Nearby

**Khao Sam Muk**, the headland at the beach's northern end, is a sandstone hill (54 m per Wikipedia's infobox, 45 m per the article text -- the two disagree) topped with a Chinese shrine to the sea goddess Mazu, with a second Thai shrine at its base tied to the local drowning legend that also named the beach.

**Wang Saen Suk**, a Buddhist temple a few hundred meters from the sand, houses the largest hell garden in Thailand -- twenty-one tortured souls built to warn visitors away from sin. It is a distinct site from the beach and from Khao Sam Muk, despite sharing the same municipality.

**Burapha University**, adjoining Bang Saen, enrolls 47,865 students and runs an Institute of Marine Science whose aquarium is a major attraction in its own right -- and whose researchers are the ones who treat stranded marine animals and study the whale pods that pass through these waters.

**Bangsaen Street Circuit** closes the coastal roads every July for racing: the Bangsaen Grand Prix 2026 ran July 1-5 with over 100 cars entered and free public entry, marking the circuit's 20th anniversary since it opened in 2007.

## Local Notes

Wang Saen Suk's hell garden is graphic by design -- animal-headed torture figures and donation boxes beside each scene -- and worth knowing about before visiting with young children. Khao Sam Muk's summit and Wang Saen Suk's grounds are both separate destinations from the beach itself and from each other; don't conflate the three when planning a day.
