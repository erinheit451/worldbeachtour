# Vodarevu Beach

Vodarevu Beach is a fishing-village beach on the Bay of Bengal, in Chirala mandal on India's east coast. It was once a minor port that flourished into the first quarter of the twentieth century and wound down by the time India became independent; today it is primarily a working fishing town, and secondarily a weekend beach destination for visitors from Hyderabad and Telangana.

## Where it actually is

Vodarevu sits about 6 km from Chirala by Wikipedia's account, corroborated independently by Outlook Traveller — though the Bapatla district government's own tourism page gives 8 km for the same stretch. Wikipedia's infobox and that district portal both place the beach in Bapatla district; Wikidata and a 2025 press article on a resort-corridor proposal still label it Prakasam district, a mismatch that likely dates to Bapatla being carved out of Prakasam and Guntur. Wikipedia records the coordinates as 15°47′44″N 80°24′51″E; Wikidata's structured record gives a slightly different pair, 15°48′0″N 80°24′59″E, a short distance to the north.

The place goes by more than one name. A village profile lists "Vadarevu" as an alternate spelling, and this beach's own identity record also carries "Odarevu" (ఓడరేవు) as a variant — none of which have a dedicated Telugu-language source beyond that village page.

## What's here now

The Bapatla district government's tourism page for Vodarevu, last updated 19 August 2026, describes it as one of the district's most-visited beaches, offering water sports, fishing, and boat rides — a working, still-current description rather than a historical one. A separate business listing gives the beach's hours as daily, 11:00 to 01:00. Coconut trees line the shore, and fishing boats and drying nets are the beach's actual scenery; there is no built promenade or resort strip at Vodarevu itself.

## Who goes, and why

Vodarevu is described in its own Wikipedia entry as the shortest-distance beach from Hyderabad and Telangana, and it draws large numbers of weekend tourists from those areas specifically because of that proximity, rather than because of any singular attraction on the sand. That same weekend draw is also the backdrop to Vodarevu's least comfortable fact: a beach popular enough to bring large weekend crowds into water that has, on more than one October, turned dangerous with little warning — covered in full in [the currents deep dive](/beaches/vodarevu-beach/surf).

Inland, at nearby Vadarevu, the Lalithananda Ashram — founded roughly 70 years ago — anchors a small parallel identity for the area: a religious and social-service compound with its own 11th-century temple, reported under reconstruction as of a 2004 press account, entirely separate from the beach crowd a few kilometers away.

## What it isn't (yet)

Vodarevu has no resort infrastructure of its own — no hotels, no built beachfront, no organized water-sports operator confirmed in current sources. That is the beach's defining condition today, and it is actively contested: an Andhra Pradesh business federation has proposed turning this stretch of coast into a resort corridor, a plan covered honestly in the sections below rather than glossed over here.
