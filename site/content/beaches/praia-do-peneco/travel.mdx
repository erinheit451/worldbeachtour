Praia do Peneco sits in central Albufeira, on Portugal's Algarve coast, about 38.5 km (23.9 miles) by road west of Faro — Faro's Gago Coutinho International Airport (FAO) sits on the near side of that same town, so plan driving time on the road figure.

## Getting There

Three routes lead down from Albufeira's old town to the sand: a roughly 20-meter pedestrian tunnel cut through the cliff beside the old town's tourist office, a stairway to the west, or the free public **Elevador do Peneco**, a 28-meter elevator carrying up to 13 people, located in the Rossio area. The elevator normally runs daily from 08:00 to 21:00. For drivers, the beach's street address is Esplanada Dr. Frutuoso da Silva 9, Albufeira; street parking is available around the old town, but spots there can be limited, so arriving early or walking down from further out is worth planning for.

## Season

The beach's official EU Blue Flag window runs 1 July to 30 September 2026, inside a wider bathing season of 15 May to 15 October 2026. That is when lifeguards, loungers, parasols, and pedalos for hire are reliably present. Portugal's tourism board lists the beach's Blue Flag facilities as security and surveillance, sunshade rental, small-craft hire, showers, outdoor parking, a bar and restaurant, windsurfing, and full accessibility for visitors with disabilities.

## Costs

The beach itself is free, and so is the elevator. The record does not confirm current parking, food, or rental prices near the beach beyond the fact that a beach bar, Roca Beach Bar, built in 2018, sells lunch and ice cream on the sand.

## Safety

The water gets deep quickly close to shore, and English Wikipedia notes the beach has good access for disabled visitors alongside seasonal lifeguard coverage. As of February 2026, a safety perimeter has been marked off around the fissured Peneco boulder itself — stay outside it; officials, including Albufeira's mayor and Portugal's environment minister, have called the underlying cliff instability here a serious and urgent problem, and the rock's own fate is still undecided. Mayor Rui Cristina has called the rock "something very symbolic, an emblematic rock that represents Albufeira," while conceding the technical call rests with specialists, and the news outlet ECO has described it as a "natural monument" facing possible demolition. The environment minister has said she will ask Portugal's National Laboratory for Civil Engineering (LNEC) to help decide on any intervention. Albufeira logged 23 storm-damage occurrences between October and early March, 18 of them cliff-instability cases including at this beach, and the national coastal agency's pre-summer 2026 priority plan for the town covers repairing beach access and marking off the risk zone here. The full account of the crack, the erosion survey behind it, and the repair timeline is in the [environment deep dive](/beaches/praia-do-peneco/environment).
