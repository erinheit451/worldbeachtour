# Sunnyside Beach (Sunnyside North Beach)

Sunnyside North Beach is a clothes-optional beach on Port Phillip Bay, about 12 kilometres from Frankston and south of Mount Eliza on Victoria's Mornington Peninsula. It is the only clothes-optional beach on the east side of Port Phillip Bay and one of just three legally designated clothing-optional beaches in the entire state.

Here's the part that trips people up: the beach directly beside the Sunnyside Road carpark is not the legal clothing-optional section. The designated stretch begins about 500 metres further along, reached by a winding dirt path through scrub or by walking the beach itself over a rocky section. From there it runs south of Moondah Beach and north of Bidwell Beach, a stretch dominated by sandstone rock, reef and bluff, with a rocky north end and a shortish sandy stretch. There is no lifesaver patrol, and the general hazard rating here is 3 out of 10 — among the least hazardous ratings going.

The carpark itself has toilets and showers, plus free electric barbecues at its far end. Beyond that there isn't much infrastructure: no food vendors, no kiosk, nothing resembling the amenities of a mainstream metropolitan beach.

The clothing-optional status itself rests on the Nudity (Prescribed Areas) Act 1983, which lets the state planning minister prescribe or revoke such an area after first consulting the local council — the same clause a 2021-2022 council review threatened to invoke. Victoria has only three beaches carrying this designation: Sunnyside North, Point Impossible near Torquay, and Southside near Point Addis. Point Impossible sits on the eastern outskirts of Torquay, about 30 minutes' drive from Geelong, and is itself a separate clothing-optional beach under the same Act, not to be confused with Sunnyside North. Southside is the more cautionary comparison: it exists today because a previously legal nude beach nearby had its status revoked outright, the closest thing on record to what a "no" from the minister actually looks like.

Next door to the beach sits the Morning Star Estate, a mansion built around 1867-1870 for London merchant Francis Alfred Gillett, who bought the surrounding land in 1865. The Catholic Church purchased the estate in 1932 and ran it as Morning Star Boys' Home, a training centre for delinquent boys, until the home closed in 1975. Today the 63-hectare property is heritage-listed, home to what may be Victoria's largest rose garden with around 75,000 bushes, and was relisted for sale at $40 million in 2020 after an earlier $30 million asking price in 2017 — a genteel, unrelated identity sharing the same stretch of coast as the beach's own decades-long political fight.

Mount Eliza, the seaside suburb the beach adjoins, sits 42 kilometres south-east of Melbourne's CBD and recorded a population of 18,734 at the 2021 census.

Who actually goes: a mix of naturists and clothing-optional regulars drawn by the scarcity of the designation itself — this is one of only three places in Victoria where that's legal — plus a longstanding LGBTIQ+ community that has used the beach as a gathering space for years. It's also, less romantically, a beach whose carpark and surrounding bushland drew a 2022 council report alleging drug use and syringes, a controversy that nearly cost the beach its legal status before a decisive community consultation kept it in place — the full story is in [the culture deep-dive](/beaches/sunnyside-beach-3/culture).
