Cala Vedella is a small hooked cove and low-rise beach resort on Ibiza's western coast, in the municipality of Sant Josep de Sa Talaia. Most English-language sources spell it "Cala Vadella"; the official Sant Josep municipal site uses the Catalan "Cala Vedella," which matches this page's name. It is the same beach either way.

The cove's shape is what defines it. Its north side juts out into a headland called Es' Torraci, which has its own small satellite beach, Sa Torrasa, tucked on the far side. A small beach on the inlet's southern side once held an illegal bar-nightclub; it closed long ago, but the concrete slab of its dance floor still sits near the water. Sources disagree on the main beach's exact footprint: Wikidata gives 275 m long by 30 m wide; Ibiza Spotlight gives 275 m long by 30-70 m wide; Spain's official tourism portal lists it at 200 m long and 45 m wide.

The sand is white and the substrate is genuine sand. Spain's official tourism site rates the swimming conditions calm, and the WBT dataset's own water-quality rating is "good." The rocky sides of the cove make for reasonable snorkelling, though in some years the swimming and snorkelling experience is marred by infestations of stinging jellyfish. As of 2012, Cala Vedella was one of 12 Blue Flag beaches on Ibiza, and its bathing water is registered under EU ID ES531M0481209.

During the summer season the main beach is patrolled by lifeguards; outside that window there is no coverage. Behind the beach, 250 parking spaces include a small allocation for disabled drivers with a European blue badge. The cove's shape also frames a clear view out to the islet of s'Espartar, one of the reasons the official Sant Josep tourism page singles it out.

Cala Vedella's calm, family-beach reputation sits directly on top of a residential development that has never been fully regularized since a 1973 plan — a story with its own room here: [the fifty-two-year file behind the beach](/beaches/platja-de-cala-vedella/history).

On the sand itself, two restaurants — Vadella Beach Bar and Ca Na Sofía — both carry the island's Sabors d'Eivissa local-produce seal. A short drive inland, Sant Josep village runs a Saturday ecological food and craft market in the summer months.
