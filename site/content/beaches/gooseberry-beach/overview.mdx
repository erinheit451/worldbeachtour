Gooseberry Beach is a private beach in Newport, Rhode Island, on Ocean Drive between Bailey's Beach and Hazard's Beach — privately owned, but open to the public for day use. It sits inside a small cove, sheltered by a spit of land and a line of large boulders roughly 75 meters offshore at high tide and as close as 50 meters at low tide, which keeps the water calmer here than at Newport's open-ocean beaches.

The land under Gooseberry has a specific, documented history. In the mid-19th century it was farmland called Rocky Farm, owned by John Alfred Hazard, a descendant of one of Newport's founders. Hazard died in 1880, and the contested settlement of his will led to Rocky Farm being gifted to Newport Hospital — an organization, by one historical account, "entirely unprepared for its management." Two separate beaches eventually grew out of that land: Hazard's Beach and Gooseberry Beach, each run by a corporation formed by local citizens. Gooseberry's shareholders have operated the club since 1952, a founding the club's own president cites when he says the beach gives the public more access than its 1952 deed with the hospital actually requires.

That framing has been tested. In June 2021, a part-time Newport resident retained an attorney to argue the beach should function, in practice, as a public beach for Newport residents — a dispute this fact sheet found no documented resolution for. What is documented is more concrete: no walk-ons or drop-offs are permitted at Gooseberry under any circumstance, entry requires a paid vehicle, bike, or scooter pass, and rising rideshare traffic on narrow, shoulderless Ocean Drive got dangerous enough that the club now pays a police officer to direct it. The full argument over what "open to the public" means at a shareholder-owned beach is covered in the [history deep dive](/beaches/gooseberry-beach/history).

Gooseberry is a working cove, not a resort. Lifeguards and the gate operate 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., weather permitting; the only food on site is a cash-only "snack shack" (a Rhode Island tourism listing calls the same thing a "cafe") open 11 a.m. to 5 p.m.; and alcohol, pets, grills, motorized watercraft, open fires, spearfishing, and all but small infant tents are banned. The cove's calmer water makes it more family-friendly than Newport's open beaches, but it isn't immune to the state's broader coastal problems: Rhode Island Department of Health testing closed Gooseberry to swimming for high bacteria counts in June 2022 and again in August 2025, both times reopening within days once counts fell back under the state's 60 CFU/100 mL threshold.

Two addresses are attached to Gooseberry Beach, and neither source resolves the conflict: the club's own site lists 130 Ocean Avenue, Newport, RI, while a state tourism listing gives 123 Ocean Drive, Newport, RI 02840.
