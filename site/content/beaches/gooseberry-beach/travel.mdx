# Gooseberry Beach Travel Guide

Gooseberry Beach is privately owned and shareholder-run, but it takes day visitors — with real limits on how you get in and what it costs. This page covers access, timing, safety, and cost; for the dispute over what "open to the public" should mean here, see the [history deep dive](/beaches/gooseberry-beach/history).

## Getting there

<MapEmbed lat={41.4598534} lng={-71.3220415} name="Gooseberry Beach" />

Gooseberry sits on Ocean Drive in Newport, Rhode Island, between Bailey's Beach and Hazard's Beach. The beach's own site gives its address as 130 Ocean Avenue; a Rhode Island tourism listing instead lists 123 Ocean Drive, Newport, RI 02840 — the two conflict, and no source in this fact sheet resolves which is correct. Straight-line, Gooseberry is about 3.4 km from central Newport and roughly 8.7 km from Newport State Airport (NPT); no source describes an actual driving route from either point, so treat these as as-the-crow-flies distances, not directions.

There is no walk-on or drop-off entry of any kind — not on foot, not by rideshare. Entry is strictly by paid vehicle, bike, or scooter pass, capped at five people per car. Rideshare traffic on Ocean Drive got dangerous enough, with cars stopping on a narrow, shoulderless road, that the club now pays a police officer just to direct traffic there.

## When to go

The beach and its lifeguards operate on a fixed daily schedule — 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., weather permitting — with no exceptions documented for early or late access. Water-quality testing for Tier II beaches like Gooseberry happens only once a month, roughly from May 26 through September 1 each season, which is the effective window for supervised, tested swimming.

## Costs

Parking is the entire admission cost, and it isn't cheap for a day pass: $30 per car on weekdays and $40 on weekends and holidays, both plus 7% Rhode Island sales tax, capped at five occupants. Bikes, e-bikes, and scooters pay $10 a day, or $100 for a season pass. As of June 29, 2026, the parking lot stopped accepting cash — credit or debit only. The only food on site, a cash-only "snack shack" (11 a.m.-5 p.m., weather permitting), runs its own separate payment rule despite the lot going cashless.

## Safety

Gooseberry's cove, protected by a boulder spit roughly 50-75 meters offshore, produces calmer water than Newport's open beaches, and no shark incidents are on record here. But "calm" hasn't meant "clean": Rhode Island's Department of Health closed Gooseberry to swimming for high bacteria on June 8, 2022 — the first beach closure notice of that year statewide — and again on August 14, 2025, alongside Bailey's Beach/Spouting Rock. The 2025 closure lifted within two days once bacteria counts fell back under the state's 60 CFU/100 mL threshold; a 2023 water-quality report found Gooseberry had run high on bacteria on nearly half the days it was sampled in 2022, one of 14 Rhode Island beaches that topped the threshold on a quarter or more of their tested days. Check the state's current advisories before you swim rather than assuming the water is clear because the cove looks calm.

Alcohol, pets, grilling, motorized watercraft, open fires, spearfishing, and tents (except small infant tents) are all banned on the beach.
