# Goat Rock Beach

Goat Rock Beach is a crescent-shaped sand beach in northwestern Sonoma County, California, a sub-unit of Sonoma Coast State Beach at the point where the Russian River meets the Pacific in Jenner, on Highway 1 at Goat Rock Road. At its southern end, the massive Goat Rock outcrop -- barely attached to the mainland by a narrow isthmus -- gives the beach its name; the Russian River's mouth marks the northern end. The beach lies within Rancho Bodega, a Mexican-era land grant, and the name "Goat Rock" itself does not appear on maps before World War I.

## What's Actually Here

The sea stacks are Franciscan Complex rock, formed roughly 200 to 30 million years ago in the same plate-collision zone that put the active San Andreas Fault close to this coastline. Erosion strips 1 to 3 feet of land here every year, even as uplift has raised the marine terrace above the beach to 30-150 feet above sea level. Harbor seals have hauled out at the river mouth since at least 1974, sharing the sand and surrounding rocks with western, California and ring-billed gulls, river otters, elephant seals and sea lions; more than 50 fish species, including juvenile steelhead and Chinook salmon, live in the estuary behind the beach.

## Who Actually Goes

Seal-watchers are the largest audience, viewing the rookery from a required 50-yard distance, especially during pupping season. Climbers work the Sunset Boulders cluster near the entrance road, on routes from 5.2 to V10. A smaller group of hang-gliders launch from a 150-foot terrace point at the beach's south end, provided they carry a USHGA card, a Sonoma Wings card and a signed waiver. Photographers come for Goat Rock and Arched Rock themselves -- the same view that stood in for "Cauldron Point" in the 1985 film The Goonies -- and Metroactive readers once voted this the second-most-popular beach in Sonoma County. Almost nobody comes to swim: it is prohibited, for reasons this page's Honest Reckoning section covers in full, including why the beach went so long without a lifeguard.

## What This Beach Is Not

Goat Rock Beach is not a swimming beach, not a place with any confirmed on-site food or lodging, and not a place where dogs are allowed (leashed dogs are permitted on the adjacent Blind Beach instead, off the south parking lot). Fishing is banned along part of the shoreline inside the Russian River State Marine Conservation Area, and drones are banned across the whole state park district. What it is: a working piece of coastline -- geologically active, ecologically protected, and visited far more for what can be watched from the sand than for anything done in the water.
