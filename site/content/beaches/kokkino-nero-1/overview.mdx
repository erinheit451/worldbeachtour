# Kokkino Nero

Kokkino Nero — "red water" — is a village and beach in the community of Karitsa, municipal unit of Evrymenes, municipality of Agia, in the Larissa regional unit of Thessaly, Greece. It sits at the foot of Mount Ossa, known locally as Kissavos, on the eastern shore of the Thermaikos gulf. The name comes from an iron-tinted mineral spring that colours the shallows near the shore rust-red — the same spring that, as the history lens on this page covers in full, three separate commercial ventures tried and failed to bottle across more than half a century.

## Two Villages, One Name

Anyone researching Kokkino Nero should know there are two of them. This village — officially Kokkino Nero Evrymenon, part of the community of Karitsa — sits about two kilometres from a separate, smaller settlement of the exact same name that belongs to the neighbouring municipal unit of Meliboea. The two have separate Wikipedia and Wikidata entries and separate histories. Nothing on this page describes the other village, and nothing about the other village belongs here.

## What It Is Today

Kokkino Nero was first officially registered as its own settlement in 1928, under what was then the community of Karytsis. Until 2020 it belonged to the separate former municipality of Evrymenon; it now falls within the municipality of Agia. The 2021 census recorded 116 permanent residents — a small place by any measure, and one whose own reference sources don't always agree on the details, from its elevation to its population a generation ago (the honest-reckoning section on the main page goes through the specific discrepancies).

The village's identity is built almost entirely around its spring, an iron-rich mineral source with a documented history and a still-active state medical classification — recounted in full in the history lens linked below, not repeated here.

## Who This Suits

This is not a resort. There is no confirmed lifeguard presence, no named hotel or restaurant a source here can vouch for, and no ferry service. What there is: a genuine mineral spring with a documented history, a beach in two distinct textures, and a village small enough that its own paperwork doesn't always agree with itself.
