# Fistral Beach Travel Guide

Fistral Beach sits half a mile west of Newquay town center on Cornwall's north coast, 8.0 km from Cornwall Airport Newquay (NQY) and about 1.9 km from Newquay's train station. It is the most developed surf beach in the UK for visitor infrastructure, with surf schools, hotels, and restaurants clustered around the International Surfing Centre, and it is also, as of 2026, the beach a national sewage lawsuit against South West Water is built around. Both facts should factor into how you plan a visit.

## Getting There

**By air.** Cornwall Airport Newquay (NQY) is the region's gateway, with year-round domestic UK connections and seasonal European routes. It sits about 8 km from Fistral, roughly a 15-20 minute drive.

**By train.** Great Western Railway's Atlantic Coast Line runs 20¾ miles from Par, the mainline interchange station in Cornwall, to Newquay Station, a scenic branch route. From Newquay Station it is a short walk through town to Fistral Beach.

**By car.** From the A30, the main road through Cornwall, follow signage into Newquay and then toward the seafront and Fistral Beach car park. Traffic on approach roads is heaviest on summer weekends.

**By bus.** Local routes including 21A, 85, 87, 88, 92A, and 93 stop near Fistral Beach; from Newquay's bus station it is roughly a 15-minute walk to the beach.

Newquay has a temperate maritime climate (Köppen Cfb) with a mean annual air temperature around 11.3°C and roughly 985 mm of rainfall a year. February is the coldest month, with daytime highs around 10°C and sea temperature around 9-10°C; August is the warmest, with daytime highs around 20°C and sea temperature around 16-17°C. Wetsuits are effectively required for surfing year-round; a full or thicker wetsuit is standard outside summer.

Wave energy runs opposite to air temperature: the site's wave data shows an annual mean significant wave height of 2.06 m, biggest in December (2.96 m average, with a peak recorded day of 5.26 m) and calmest in June (1.2 m average). Winter (roughly October-April) is when the Cribbar reef can break at serious size and when experienced surfers come specifically for the swell; summer brings smaller, more consistent, beginner-friendly waves and by far the largest crowds, peaking around the Boardmasters surf contest each August.

## Where to Stay

Fistral itself has limited beachfront lodging. The Headland Hotel, a Grade II-listed Victorian building open since 1900, sits directly on the headland above the beach. Most other accommodation is a short walk or drive away in Newquay town, which has a large seasonal tourism base ranging from hostels to hotels, concentrated around Towan Head and the town center. Book well ahead for the week of Boardmasters in early August, when the town's accommodation fills fastest.

## Costs

Fistral Beach itself is free to access. Parking near the International Surfing Centre is a private, camera-controlled car park charging during the day in season (roughly 8am-6:30pm in summer) and free overnight; third-party booking platforms list Fistral parking from around £1.50. Council-run alternatives sit nearby at Belmont, Dane Road, Towan Headland, and Tower Road.

Surf lessons vary by operator and format. Surf Sanctuary, based at the Headland Hotel, lists private lessons from £120 for one person; Escape Surf School lists lessons from £36 each when booked as a block of six or more. Most beginner lessons run about two hours and include equipment.

## Safety

**Rip currents** are Fistral's primary physical hazard. RNLI lifeguards patrol the main flagged section seasonally, June through September. Rip currents can form suddenly: on Saturday 26 July 2025, a powerful flash rip current outside the flagged swimming area required the rescue of 17 people within a short window, and similar multi-person rescues have recurred in past summers. Swim only between the red-and-yellow flags during patrol hours, and never attempt to swim directly against a rip; swim parallel to shore to escape it.

**Water quality** is a separate issue from rip currents and needs a separate check. South West Water discharged sewage in the Newquay area 221 times in 2024 (376 hours), and Surfers Against Sewage tracked 162.8 hours of discharge specifically at Fistral in 2025, ranking it sixth worst among the UK surf spots it monitors. Discharges cluster after heavy rain. Both South West Water and Surfers Against Sewage publish live discharge alerts; check them before swimming, particularly after rain, and be aware that events at Fistral have been cancelled with a day's notice because of pollution warnings.

**The Cribbar reef** off Towan Head is an expert-only big-wave break that only breaks at serious size during major winter groundswells. It is not a beginner or intermediate wave under any conditions.

## Local Tips

**Check two separate forecasts before getting in the water:** the surf and tide forecast, and the current sewage-discharge alert. They are unrelated to each other and both matter.

**Take a lesson if you're new to surfing.** Fistral has one of the UK's densest concentrations of certified surf schools, several operating directly from the International Surfing Centre.

**Time a visit around Boardmasters deliberately, not accidentally.** The surf contest that anchors the wider Boardmasters festival still runs at Fistral each August (music and camping moved to nearby Watergate Bay in 2005); it draws large crowds and fills accommodation across Newquay.

**Eat at Rick Stein's Fistral outpost** if you want a sit-down meal without leaving the beach; it has run at the International Surfing Centre since 2015 and does not take bookings, using a virtual queue when busy.

**Respect the Cribbar as a spectator sport unless you are an expert.** Watching from Towan Head is how most people, including most locals, experience it.
