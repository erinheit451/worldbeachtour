# Alona Beach Travel Guide

Alona Beach is on Panglao Island's southern coast, in barangay Tawala, Bohol Province. It functions as two things at once: a dive-industry base with operators running boats to Balicasag and Pamilacan Islands, and a dense resort strip that draws day-trippers off the Bohol-Panglao airport road. Both identities trace back to the same 1973 film shoot that gave the beach its name — see the [history spoke](/beaches/alona-beach/history) for that story. This guide covers what it actually costs and takes to get there, stay there, and dive there, sourced to operators' own pricing and current booking listings rather than round guesses.

## Getting There

### By Air

Bohol-Panglao International Airport (TAG) opened commercially on 27-28 November 2018, replacing the old Tagbilaran city airstrip. It sits on Panglao's north shore, about 2.8 kilometers from Alona Beach — roughly a 10-minute drive. There is no direct long-haul international service; the typical routing from North America or Europe is a gateway city to Manila or Cebu, then a short domestic or regional hop into TAG.

### By Ferry

OceanJet and other fastcraft operators run Cebu City–Tagbilaran crossings several times daily, a trip of about 2 hours. As of 2026, passenger fares run roughly ₱1,000–1,560 depending on seat class; fares fluctuate with fuel costs, so treat this as a ballpark, not a quote. Tagbilaran's port is about 20 kilometers northeast of Alona Beach, a 30–40 minute drive.

### From Airport or Port to Alona

Grab (ride-hailing) began operating GrabCar and GrabFood in Tagbilaran, Dauis, and Panglao in December 2024 — before that, taxis and tricycles were the only options, and outside those three areas tricycles still are. Tricycle fares are negotiated, not metered; agree on a price before getting in. Many dive resorts and mid-range hotels include airport or port transfers in package bookings, which is usually simpler than arranging it separately.

## Diving: What It Actually Costs

Two operators with long track records on the beach publish course pricing directly. Alona Divers, established 1996, lists its 3-day PADI Open Water course — classroom instruction plus confined-water training and open-water dives — at 21,000 PHP. Seaquest Dive Center, on Alona Beach since 1990, offers a PADI Discover Scuba Diving introductory session (no certification, one supervised shallow dive); the operator's site doesn't post a price, so treat it as quote-on-request. Both operators run boats to the Balicasag Island Marine Sanctuary, a no-take reserve since 1985, and to Pamilacan Island. Prices at other operators along the strip vary; get a written quote before booking, since package inclusions (boat fees, gear rental, marine-sanctuary entrance fees) aren't standardized across shops.

## Where to Stay

Alona's built-up frontage is compact enough that almost anywhere on the strip is walking distance to dive shops and restaurants. As of 2026 booking-aggregator listings: Henann Resort Alona Beach — the strip's largest property, 6.5 hectares and roughly 400 rooms, open since June 2015 — lists a sister budget property, Henann Tawala Resort, from around $75/night. Alona Vida Beach Resort lists from around $40/night, and Panglao Regents Park Resort from around $23/night. Aggregator sites put the area's blended average around $60/night on weekdays, higher on weekends. These are snapshot prices from booking platforms, not fixed rates — they move with season and occupancy, so check current listings rather than treating any figure here as a quote.

Beyond Alona's central strip, Doljo Beach and Dumaluan Beach on other parts of Panglao offer lower density; Tagbilaran city, 20 km away, has conventional hotels but adds the port-to-beach drive to every day's logistics.

## Sea Conditions

Wave data for this stretch of coast (Open-Meteo Marine / ERA5 reanalysis, 2023–2024) shows a calm annual pattern: mean wave height 0.46 m, with February the roughest month at a 0.61 m average and June the calmest at 0.27 m. The single biggest recorded day reached 1.08 m. That's gentle swimming water most of the year by open-ocean standards — the operative hazard here isn't surf, it's the habagat-season (southwest monsoon) erosion that has driven the seawall-and-riprap dispute covered in the [history spoke](/beaches/alona-beach/history).

Typhoon exposure is real: the IBTrACS storm-track archive records 36 tropical cyclone passages near this coast over the past 50 years, with a maximum recorded category of 3. Storm season in the Philippines runs roughly July through November; check forecasts before booking dive trips in that window, since boats to Balicasag don't launch in rough seas.

## Water Quality

Don't assume the water is clean by default. DENR testing in October–November 2018 recorded fecal coliform at Alona Beach at 16,000 MPN per 100 mL against a swimming standard of 100 MPN, triggering a brief inter-agency swimming ban; a follow-up reading weeks later came back at 33 MPN and the ban lifted. There is no continuously published, current water-quality feed for the beach that this guide could verify. A February 2026 opinion column in the Manila Times described the nearshore water as turbid and algae-clogged on a recent visit, with the section fronting Henann Resort comparatively better kept. Both data points are real; neither tells you what the water looks like on the day you arrive. If it looks or smells off, it probably is — ask your dive operator or hotel, who monitor conditions more closely than any published source.

## Local Notes

- **Dive early.** Morning boats to Balicasag typically load 6–7 a.m., before wind and afternoon plankton blooms reduce underwater visibility, according to operators on the strip.
- **Balicasag is a protected sanctuary, not open-access reef.** It's been a no-take marine protected area since 1985; touching or standing on coral is both damaging and, in a managed sanctuary, against the rules operators are expected to enforce.
- **Reef-safe sunscreen** is standard operator guidance for boat trips to Balicasag and Pamilacan.
- **Cash still matters.** ATMs exist on Alona Beach and in Tagbilaran, but smaller tricycle drivers, market stalls, and some independent dive shops are cash-only.
- **The seawalls you see (or don't) are part of an ongoing dispute.** Some sections of the beach have DENR-approved riprap installed since a 2022 policy reversal; others don't, after 2018-19 demolitions. Erosion is visibly uneven along the strip as a result.
