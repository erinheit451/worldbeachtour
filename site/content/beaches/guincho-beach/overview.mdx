Praia do Guincho is an exposed Atlantic beach on the Estoril coast, about 5 km northwest of Cascais and on the western edge of the Sintra-Cascais Natural Park, in the District of Lisbon. It is roughly 800 metres of open sand between rocky headlands, facing the ocean with no bay or island to shelter it. Its defining feature is the wind: the summer nortada, a north-to-northwest wind funnelled onto the beach by the Serra de Sintra and the coastal turn at Cabo da Roca. It carries a Blue Flag.

## Why this beach matters

The reliable summer wind is why Guincho became the centre of Portuguese wave-sailing. The beach hosted the country's first major international windsurfing event in 1986, with riders including Robby Naish, and through the 1990s and 2000s it was a stop on the PWA World Cup wave tour; the PWA World Cup Guincho Wave Contest of 15-19 June 2005 ran for €55,000 in prize money. The same wind and swell that draw sailors make the water hazardous: strong currents and a heavy shore break mean inexperienced swimmers and families with young children are warned to stay out.

The wind shapes the land as well as the water. Behind the sand, the Guincho-Cresmina-Oitavos dune system covers 66 hectares of mobile sand that the northwest wind drives inland about 10 metres a year, and a 2011 dune park protects it with close to 2 km of raised boardwalk.

The south end is closed by the Fortaleza do Guincho, a 17th-century coastal fort now run as a Relais & Chateaux hotel whose restaurant has held a Michelin star since 2001. The opening sequence of the 1969 James Bond film On Her Majesty's Secret Service, in which George Lazenby's Bond pulls Diana Rigg's Contessa Tracy from the surf, was filmed on this sand. Cabo da Roca, the westernmost point of mainland Europe, is a short drive north.
