# Praia do Guincho Travel Guide

Praia do Guincho is an exposed Atlantic beach about 5 km northwest of Cascais, on the western edge of the Sintra-Cascais Natural Park in the District of Lisbon. It is roughly 800 metres of open sand between rocky headlands, known above all for wind: the summer nortada that made it Portugal's home of windsurfing and kitesurfing. It is a Blue Flag beach, but not a calm-water swimming beach.

## Getting There

### By Air

The practical arrival airport is **Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport (LIS)**, about 40 km east of the beach; the drive takes roughly 40-55 minutes outside peak traffic, west through Cascais and out along the coast road. The much nearer Cascais aerodrome (Aerodromo de Tires) handles only general aviation and business flights, not scheduled commercial service, so most visitors land at LIS.

From the airport, the common routes are a rental car or taxi/ride-share straight to Guincho, or the train-and-bus combination described below. A taxi or ride-share from LIS to the Guincho area typically runs on the order of €40-60, depending on traffic and time of day; confirm the fare before relying on it.

### By Train and Bus

There is **no train to Guincho itself**. The Cascais railway line runs along the coast from Lisbon's Cais do Sodre station to Cascais (about 40 minutes, frequent service), and from Cascais you transfer to a bus for the final leg to the beach. Buy a rechargeable Navegante/Viva Viagem card at any station for the train, and check current fares, which change year to year.

### By Bus

From central Cascais, **local bus route 15** runs out to Praia do Guincho along the coast road. It is the standard car-free way to reach the beach from the town. Service is more frequent in summer than in winter; check the current Cascais bus timetable before setting out, and allow extra time on busy summer weekends.

### By Car

From Cascais, a single coast road (the Estrada do Guincho / Avenida Nossa Senhora do Cabo) leads directly to the beach in about 10-15 minutes. From central Lisbon, take the A5 motorway west toward Cascais, then follow the coast out to Guincho; budget 40-55 minutes in light traffic. Guincho also sits on the scenic loop toward **Cabo da Roca** and **Sintra**, so many visitors combine it with those on a single drive.

## Best Time to Visit

**Summer (June-August)** is the high season and the reliable-wind window. The nortada blows most days, building through the afternoon, which is what draws windsurfers and kitesurfers; it is the busiest and windiest time. Air temperatures are warm but the exposed beach can feel cool and gusty, and blown sand is common on strong days.

**Spring and autumn (April-May, September-October)** are quieter, with a mix of conditions and fewer crowds. Autumn brings the first bigger Atlantic swells for surfing.

**Winter (November-March)** brings the largest swells and easterly offshore winds that groom the waves for surfers, with wave heights commonly in the 1.5-3 metre range and occasionally over 4 metres. It is cold, wet, and windswept, and the beach is quiet; some of the paid parking becomes free in this season.

## Where to Stay

There is little lodging on the beach itself. Realistically you choose between three areas, described honestly rather than by inventing property names.

### On the beach

The **Fortaleza do Guincho**, the 17th-century fort at the south end, operates as a small Relais & Chateaux hotel with a Michelin-starred restaurant. It is the one place to stay directly on the beach and is priced accordingly. A small number of other hotels sit on the cliffs and dunes immediately behind Guincho.

### Cascais

**Cascais**, about 5 km away, has by far the widest range of hotels, guesthouses, and short-term rentals across price points, plus restaurants, the train to Lisbon, and the bus to the beach. It is the practical base for most visitors.

### Sintra or Lisbon

**Sintra**, inland, and **Lisbon**, to the east, are both within an easy day-trip drive and offer their own accommodation if you want to fold Guincho into a wider trip rather than base yourself on the coast.

## Costs

Access to the beach is **free**. Budget mainly for transport, parking, and any lessons or rentals.

- **Parking**: paid parking (around 500 spaces) sits at the north end; free parking is available a short 2-5 minute walk away at the south end, and some paid spaces become free in winter.
- **Bus**: local bus 15 from Cascais is inexpensive; carry a Navegante/Viva Viagem card and check the current fare.
- **Lessons and rentals**: surf, windsurf, and kitesurf schools operate along the access road; a group lesson or a half-day gear rental is the typical spend for anyone getting on the water. Prices vary by school and season, so confirm directly.
- **Food**: seafood restaurants along the Guincho road are mid-to-higher priced; the Fortaleza do Guincho restaurant is fine dining.

## Safety

Guincho is a **wind-sports beach, and its water is genuinely hazardous for casual swimmers**.

- **Currents and shore break**: the beach's own references list strong currents, a powerful shore break, and rapidly changing wind. Inexperienced swimmers and families with young children are warned to stay out of the water.
- **Lifeguards**: Portuguese beaches are typically staffed by lifeguards during the official bathing season (broadly summer) and flagged for conditions; obey the flags and swim only when and where lifeguards indicate. Outside the season there is no guarantee of cover.
- **Wind and cold**: the exposure that makes the beach great for sailing makes it cold and gusty for everyone else. Bring layers and wind protection even on warm days.
- **The dunes**: stay on the boardwalk in the Guincho-Cresmina dune park; the dune is active and protected.

## Nearby

- **Cabo da Roca**, the westernmost point of mainland Europe, is a short drive north, marked by a lighthouse and a cliff-top monument.
- **Cascais**, 5 km away, is the nearest town, with the marina, old centre, and train to Lisbon.
- **Sintra**, inland, holds the palaces and gardens of its UNESCO-listed cultural landscape, an easy add-on to a Guincho day.
- The **Guincho-Cresmina dune park**, at the beach's inland edge, has close to 2 km of boardwalk across the moving sand, covered in the [surf and wind deep dive](/beaches/guincho-beach/surf).
