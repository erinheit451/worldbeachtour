# Getting to Shelly Beach

Shelly Beach is at Manly, about 0.9 km from central Manly, connected to Manly Beach by a walking oceanway that also passes Fairy Bower. Sydney Kingsford Smith International Airport (SYD) is 19.7 km away in a straight line — that figure is "as the crow flies," not a road distance or a transit time.

## Getting there on foot

The most direct route most visitors use is the oceanway from Manly Beach, past Fairy Bower, to Shelly Beach — within walking distance of Manly's shops and restaurants. Of the three legs of the separate Eastern Hill Heritage Walk, only the South Steyne-to-Shelly Beach section is fully wheelchair accessible; the rest of that walk is not.

## Parking and facilities

Parking at Shelly Beach's own facilities is charged at $8.00 an hour. On site: two free electric barbecues, showers, toilets, and a kiosk selling meals, drinks and coffee. Public toilets on this stretch of coast are also at Little Manly Beach and Fairy Bower. A restaurant, The Boathouse Shelly Beach, operates on the sand itself according to Wikipedia, though nothing in the record confirms it's still trading today — treat that as background, not a booking recommendation.

## Rules that actually get enforced

Cabbage Tree Bay is a no-take aquatic reserve: no fishing, spearing or collecting inside the boundary. The one exception is Blueys, a fishing spot at the southeast end of the Shelly Beach headland car park, which sits just outside the reserve in a separate intertidal protected area where line fishing is allowed (collecting invertebrates still isn't). Dogs are prohibited from the reserve and beach area at all times, with no exceptions. Weddings are permitted only between May and October.

## Safety

Shelly Beach is not patrolled by lifeguards, at any time of year. Nearby Manly Beach runs a different roster entirely: Northern Beaches Council lifeguards cover South Steyne year-round, and North Steyne and Queenscliff seasonally from October to April — none of that covers Shelly Beach itself. Conditions here run genuinely exposed for a spot tucked inside a bay: modelled wave heights average around 1.2 m annually, peaking near 1.4 m in April and dropping to about 0.95 m in calmer August, with a rip current worth respecting even on an apparently calm day. Shelly Beach and the water between it and Manly have also seen real, dated shark incidents — that history is addressed honestly in the reckoning section of this guide rather than glossed over here.

## Diving and snorkelling

This is the reason most people come. Depths across most of the beach run 2 to 6 metres, shallow enough for confident snorkelling, and the site is popular enough with scuba schools that many bring beginner students here specifically because conditions suit learning to dive.
