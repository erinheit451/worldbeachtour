# Shelly Beach

Shelly Beach is a small, west-facing beach at Manly, on Sydney's northern beaches, wedged between North Head and Fairy Bower. Locals and older signage sometimes call it Shelley Beach. It is one of several places in Australia sharing that name — this page is specifically about the one at Manly, inside Cabbage Tree Bay.

## What it actually is

The name is a leftover from richer times. One account traces "Shelly" to a shell midden once recorded near the Fairy Bower car park, even though Wikipedia notes the beach itself isn't especially shelly today. An older account, from a 2001 Manly Council heritage-walk guide, says the beach used to have huge quantities of shells that the tide deposited regularly in the 1920s, until so many were taken by visitors that by 1933 few remained. Both stories point at the same thing: whatever gave the beach its name has mostly been carried off or built over.

What's left is a reflective, sheltered cove backed by a bush track around the headland, with the western point holding a surf break called Bower — one of the shallowest-breaking waves in Sydney. The whole area sits inside the Cabbage Tree Bay Aquatic Reserve, a no-take marine reserve, which is the actual reason people come.

## Who it's for

Snorkellers and beginner divers are the core audience. Depths across most of the beach run 2 to 6 metres, shallow enough to see the bottom clearly and calm enough that scuba centres bring their newest students here specifically because conditions suit learning to dive. It is not a surf beach in the conventional sense and not really a lounging beach either — facilities are limited to two free electric barbecues, showers, toilets and a kiosk.

## What almost happened instead

In the 1920s this stretch of Manly foreshore was nearly redeveloped into an amusement park. It wasn't, and the reason the beach today is a quiet marine reserve rather than a fairground traces back to that decision — the full story, and what came before and after it, is in the [history deep dive](/beaches/shelly-beach-8/history).

Dogs are prohibited at all times, and there is no lifeguard patrol — this is a self-managed, no-take reserve, not a patrolled swimming beach.
