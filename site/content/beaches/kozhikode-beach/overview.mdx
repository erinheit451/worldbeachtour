# Overview

Kozhikode Beach is the public city beach of Kozhikode (formerly Calicut), promoted by Kerala's tourism board as "Calicut Beach." It is a working, historic urban waterfront rather than a resort beach, running roughly from Beypore in the south to Kappad in the north through the South Beach, Vellayil, and Kallayi sections of the city's coastline. The beach is reached from the city via four separate road overbridges.

## The Borrowed Landing

Kerala Tourism's own page for this beach credits "this coastline" with Vasco da Gama's 1498 arrival. The documented landing was at Kappad Beach, 16 km north of Kozhikode town — the beach that, along with Kannur's Chal Beach, received Blue Flag certification from the Denmark-based Foundation for Environmental Education in February 2025. Kozhikode Beach itself holds no Blue Flag certification and is not the documented landing site.

## A Real Political History

The beachfront road was renamed Gandhi Road after Mahatma Gandhi's 1934 visit, and the sand became a rallying ground for the independence movement: Gandhi, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, Indira Gandhi, and V.K. Krishna Menon all spoke here. In April 1950, land on the beach was acquired to build the Government Headquarters hospital. To the north, the Iron Screw-pile pier, built in 1871 and running 400 feet (120 metres) out to sea, once carried cranes loading spices onto trading ships; the nearby lighthouse dates to 1847 and was converted to a solar-powered LED light in 2008.

## What's There Now

Paved-stone walkways, evening illumination, a children's play area called Lions Park, and an adjacent aquarium make up the beach's built amenities. Vendors along the promenade sell kallummakaya (fried mussels), banana chips, quail eggs, and ice cream rather than sit-down restaurant fare. The beach hosts the Malabar Mahotsavam, an annual January cultural festival with Theyyam, Kathakali, and Kalaripayattu performances, run by Kerala's Department of Tourism.

## What the Brochure Skips

On the night of October 15-16, 2025, the sea receded roughly 200 metres along a 1.5 km stretch of South Beach in what officials called a rare tidal phenomenon — not the first such event in the district; the sea receded about 50 metres at Kothi Beach in October 2022. District-wide, a 2022 survey found only about 10.5 km of Kozhikode district's 53 km of seawalls still intact, with 14.4 km collapsed or in poor condition. No official source publishes lifeguard staffing or designated swimming zones specific to this beach.
