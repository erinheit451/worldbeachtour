# Overview

Kozhikode Beach is the public city beach of Kozhikode (formerly Calicut), promoted by Kerala's tourism board as "Calicut Beach." It is a working, historic urban waterfront rather than a resort beach, on a coastal drive that runs from Beypore in the south to Kappad in the north; the city beach itself takes in the South Beach, Vellayil, and Kallayi sections of Kozhikode's shore. The beach is reached from the city via four separate road overbridges.

## The Borrowed Landing

Kerala Tourism's page for this beach says Vasco da Gama arrived "near this coastline" in 1498 — true only because Kappad, 16 km north, is on the same coast. The commemorated landing — a monument at Kappad dates it to 1498, though historian M.G.S. Narayanan argues the actual landfall was further north near Panthalayini — is at Kappad Beach, not here. Kappad is also the beach that, along with Kannur's Chal Beach, holds a Blue Flag from the Denmark-based Foundation for Environmental Education: Kappad's since October 2020, Chal's since January 2025. Kozhikode Beach itself holds no Blue Flag certification and is not the commemorated landing site.

## A Real Political History

The beachfront road was renamed Gandhi Road after Mahatma Gandhi's 1934 visit, and the sand became the city's main political rally ground over the following decades: Gandhi, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, Indira Gandhi, and V.K. Krishna Menon all spoke here. In April 1950, land on the beach was acquired to build the Government Headquarters hospital. To the north, the Iron Screw-pile pier, built in 1871 and running 400 feet (120 metres) out to sea, once carried cranes loading spices onto trading ships; the nearby lighthouse dates to 1847 and was converted to a solar-powered LED light in 2008.

## What's There Now

Paved-stone walkways, evening illumination, a children's play area called Lions Park, and an adjacent aquarium make up the beach's built amenities. Vendors along the promenade sell kallummakaya (fried mussels), banana chips, quail eggs, and ice cream rather than sit-down restaurant fare. The beach hosts the Malabar Mahotsavam, traditionally held in January with Theyyam, Kathakali, and Kalaripayattu performances and organized by the Kozhikode District Tourism Promotion Council rather than the state tourism department; check whether a current edition is scheduled.

## What the Brochure Skips

On the night of October 15-16, 2025, the sea receded roughly 200 metres along a 1.5 km stretch of South Beach in what officials called a rare tidal phenomenon — not the first such event in the district; the sea receded about 50 metres at Kothi Beach in October 2022. District-wide, a 2022 survey found only about 10.5 km of Kozhikode district's 53 km of seawalls still intact, with 14.4 km collapsed or in poor condition. No official source publishes lifeguard staffing or designated swimming zones specific to this beach.
