# The Vanishing Sand: Kozhikode Beach's Coastal Erosion Problem

Kozhikode Beach looks stable: paved promenade, evening lighting, a lighthouse that's stood in some form since 1847. Underneath that infrastructure, the coastline it sits on is only partly holding.

## The Night the Sea Went Missing

On the night of October 15-16, 2025, the sea retreated roughly 200 metres along a 1.5 km stretch of South Beach. By the next morning, the same effect had shifted to the stretch opposite the Corporation office. CMFRI scientists and the local taluk tahsildar inspected the site the same day; a CMFRI scientist put it down to a morning high tide that returned to normal by 8 am. Ward councillor SK Aboobacker described the phenomenon as starting around midnight and continuing till morning at South Beach, after which the water returned and the same effect shifted to the stretch opposite the Corporation office.

Whatever it was, it had happened before. In October 2022, the sea similarly receded about 50 metres at Kothi Beach near Nainamvalappu, elsewhere in Kozhikode district, with a tsunami officially ruled out — Kerala's disaster management authority called it "most likely a local occurrence." Around the same period as the 2025 event, India's ocean-information agency INCOIS had issued rough-sea and "black sea phenomenon" alerts — but for the coast between Thiruvananthapuram and Kollam, a different stretch of Kerala entirely. Kozhikode's own event fell outside that advisory. No source describes any permanent monitoring installed at Kozhikode Beach after either the 2022 or the 2025 event.

## What's Actually Holding the Coast Up

Kozhikode district's 78 km of coastline is backed by 53 km of engineered seawalls and 16 groins. An August 2022 field survey found the defenses in worse shape than the postcard image suggests. Only about 10.5 km of seawall were still intact. Another 28.1 km were partly damaged, and 14.4 km were collapsed or in poor condition outright. That is a district where a large share of the engineered seawall is not doing its job at full strength.

The same survey found actual beach width holding up reasonably well in some places — 10 to 25 metres of sand still standing near Kozhikode Beach itself, Vellayil, Puthiyappa, and Kappad — while it had vanished entirely at other points along the district, including Kadduka bazar, Vakkadavu, Bhatt Road, and Thuvvapara. Kozhikode Beach is, by that measure, one of the district's better-off stretches. It is also sitting behind infrastructure that is, district-wide, more broken than not.

## The Storm History Behind the Defenses

The seawalls exist because the coast needs them. Kozhikode district was among Kerala's worst-hit by Cyclone Ockhi in late November-December 2017, the most intense Arabian Sea cyclone since Cyclone Megh in 2015 — a reminder of what the 53 km of seawall along this 78 km coast is there to absorb.

## A District Safety Record, Not Just a Beach One

None of the reporting found for this page describes an official water-recession or erosion advisory issued specifically for Kozhikode Beach, as distinct from the district-wide coastal-defense survey. The beach's own safety record is otherwise not well documented: no official source publishes lifeguard staffing, designated swimming zones, or beach-closure protocols for Kozhikode Beach specifically. That gap sits alongside a real district safety record elsewhere on the coast — four people, including two women, drowned off Thikkodi beach, about 36 km north, on January 27, 2025, after being swept away by strong waves; local people and fishermen carried out the rescue rather than official lifeguards.

## What This Means for a Visitor

None of this means Kozhikode Beach is dangerous in any specific, documented way — there is no recorded drowning at this beach itself in the research behind this page, and the October 2025 event caused no reported casualties. What it means is that the postcard version of the beach — paved walkway, lighthouse, evening crowds — sits on top of a coastline that is actively losing ground in places, defended by infrastructure that a 2022 survey found more damaged than not, and subject to two water-recession events in the district in three years, each waved through with a one-line official explanation (a high tide; "a local occurrence") and no account of what, if anything, has changed since.
