# Lido Beach Travel Guide

Lido Beach is a real, actively used public beach — and also a documented target, attacked by Al-Shabab at least five times since 2016 (see the [full attack history](/beaches/lido-beach-mogadishu/history)). This guide is written for the people who actually make this trip: aid workers, journalists, diaspora members, and other travelers with a professional or family reason to be in Mogadishu, arriving with organizational or security support. It is not a guide for casual tourism, because casual tourism to Somalia does not currently exist at any meaningful scale.

## Getting There

**By air.** Aden Adde International Airport (MGQ) is Somalia's primary gateway, 7.1 kilometers southwest of Lido. Turkish Airlines (via Istanbul), Ethiopian Airlines (via Addis Ababa), and Kenya Airways (via Nairobi) operate the main commercial routes; other flights are often humanitarian, UN, or AU-chartered. Since September 2025 Somalia requires a pre-arrival eVisa (etas.gov.so); sponsorship from an employer, NGO, or diplomatic mission still smooths the process.

**From the airport.** MGQ sits inside its own secured zone (AAIA), and several hotels — including The Palms Hotel, The Kiwano Hotel, and Chelsea Village Hotel — operate directly inside that perimeter, which is where most international visitors who are not moving into the city immediately choose to stay. Travel from the airport into Mogadishu proper, including to Lido, is standardly done by vehicle with organizational or security-company arrangement rather than independent taxi.

**To Lido itself.** There is no public transit route built for tourism. Visitors who go to Lido from a city hotel or the airport zone do so by private or armored vehicle, arranged in advance.

## Where to Stay

Lido has no hotels directly on the beach that currently operate as visitor accommodation — the hotels historically on its sand (Beach View, Elite Hotel, Pearl Beach) are restaurants and event venues that have also been attack sites, not a lodging base for travelers. International visitors instead stay either inside the secured AAIA zone at the airport, or in town at hotels used by the press and NGO community, such as the Sahafi Hotel, described by frequent visitors as the hotel of choice for journalists and UN staff. Accommodation should be arranged through your organization, not independently.

## Best Time to Visit

<WeatherWidget />

Mogadishu's coast runs a cool, dry window from roughly December through February, with lower humidity and the most stable conditions for any kind of travel or field work. March through May and June through September bring rising heat and humidity; wave heights on this stretch of coast also run highest around July, averaging near 2.0 meters against a 1.52-meter annual mean, so swimming conditions are calmer in the cooler months.

## Costs

There is no tourism price structure here — costs reflect a professional-travel environment, not a leisure one. Secured accommodation runs roughly $80–250 a night depending on the hotel and its security tier. Meals inside secured compounds are often included or run $5–25; ground transport is typically arranged by your organization, and independent security escort, where needed, runs into the hundreds of dollars per day. Budget accordingly — Somalia is not a cheap destination precisely because of what the security requirements cost.

## Safety

Lido's attack history is specific and repeated, not a generic warning: Al-Shabab has hit the beach's hotel row in January 2016, August 2020, April 2022, June 2023, and August 2024, the last of which killed at least 32 people. The [deep dive on that history](/beaches/lido-beach-mogadishu/history) covers each incident. The US State Department rates all of Somalia at Level 4, Do Not Travel; UK and Australian government advisories carry comparable warnings.

Practical implications for anyone visiting:

- Travel with an organizationally arranged security escort; do not go independently.
- Time visits to daylight hours — the deadliest attack on record happened on a Friday night, when the beach was at its most crowded.
- Expect and comply with military and police checkpoints on the approach roads.
- Carry minimal valuables and avoid drawing attention with cameras or visible electronics.
- Separately from the security situation, the coast itself carries real rip current risk — marine data describes it as exposed water, not a calm swimming lagoon.

## Local Notes

Somalia is Muslim-majority; modest dress is expected once away from the water's edge, and alcohol is not available. Friday is the main day off and the beach's busiest day of the week — also, notably, the timing of its worst attack. Photographing people directly requires their permission; this is treated as a real social boundary, not a formality.
