Lido Beach — Liido in Somali — is Mogadishu's best-known public beach, on the Indian Ocean coast of the Banaadir region about 2.2 kilometers east of the city center and 7.1 kilometers from Aden Adde International Airport. The name is Italian for "beach," left over from Italian Somaliland: colonial administrators developed the shorefront for Italian residents from the late 1930s, and by the 1950s a hotel there was briefly the tallest building in the city.

## What Lido Actually Is

Under President Siad Barre, Lido was Mogadishu's social landmark: women unveiled on the sand, beach clubs serving lunch a short walk from nightclubs. That ended with the state's 1991 collapse, and when Al-Shabab took hold of central Mogadishu in the years after, it banned the kind of mixed-gender gathering the beach had always hosted. Lido reopened only after the group's abrupt withdrawal from the capital on 6 August 2011.

Since then the beach has been rebuilt as a public space and repeatedly attacked for being one. Al-Shabab has bombed Lido's hotel row at least five times — 2016, 2020, 2022, 2023, and most severely in August 2024, when a Friday-night attack on the Beach View Hotel killed at least 32 people. Combined, the five attacks have killed roughly 90 people. Mogadishu has returned to the sand after each one; a Friday-dawn volunteer cleanup that started with six people in the beach's civic revival had grown to more than 300 by 2023.

## Who Uses the Beach and How

Families swim in the surf, women fully clothed, with life jackets available to rent for non-swimmers; children play in the shallows while a small fishing fleet works the old port nearby, landing tuna and occasionally shark. Tea vendors move between blankets with trays of glasses, and the beachfront hotels and restaurants that make up Lido's commercial strip serve grilled fish and seafood into the evening — the same hotels that have been the target of every documented attack. Marine reanalysis data puts the coast's annual mean wave height at 1.52 meters, peaking near 2.0 meters in July, and describes it as exposed water with real rip current risk, a hazard that exists independent of the security situation.

## The Honest Picture

This is not a resort beach and Lido does not pretend to be one. The US State Department rates all of Somalia at Level 4, Do Not Travel, and the checkpoints, blast barriers, and armed details around Lido's hotel row are heavier than anywhere else on the sand. In February 2025, security guards from the presidential compound, Villa Somalia, shot and killed a teenage boy while clearing the beach for a foreign dignitary's visit — a reminder that the armed presence protecting Lido has also, at least once, been the danger. What makes Lido notable is not that it is safe. It is that a beach banned once and bombed five times since remains, for Mogadishu residents, the place they keep coming back to anyway.
