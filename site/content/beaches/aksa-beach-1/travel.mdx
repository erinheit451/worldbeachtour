# Aksa Beach Travel Guide

Aksa Beach sits in Aksa village, part of Malad, in Mumbai's northwestern suburbs, on the Arabian Sea. This guide covers what is actually confirmed about getting there, when to go, and what the water does to people who aren't careful with it. Where the record runs out, that is stated rather than filled in.

## Seasons

The clearest seasonal fact about Aksa is a safety one, not a comfort one: the beach "gets even more dangerous during monsoon season," and roughly 15,000 people still visit on a typical weekend regardless. A separate academic drowning study found that incidents peak in the pre-monsoon season specifically, which coincides with the summer vacation crowd surge, rather than during the monsoon's worst weather itself, when crowds presumably thin out. Beyond that, no source used here gives specific month-by-month temperature, crowd, or price data for Aksa, so none is invented for this guide.

## Safety

This is the part of the guide that matters most. Wikipedia states plainly that Aksa is not safe to swim in: currents are strong, the sand shifts with wave action, and quicksand has also been documented in the water. Warning signs prohibiting swimming have been posted and lifeguards have been appointed, and none of that has stopped drowning from recurring. An academic study covering 2006 to 2018 recorded an average of 38 drowning incidents a year, 445 people rescued, and 45 deaths, across 15 identified drowning spots along the beach, with 2007 the single worst year on record. Press coverage documents individual incidents across the years since: four drowned in June 2008, three in May 2013, two college students in May 2015, and, as recently as June 2026, a 17-year-old student who entered the water near INS Hamla with two friends and did not return; a multi-agency search by police, fire brigade, and ambulance crews was called off that evening. One report on that 2026 incident noted the specific stretch involved lacked active lifeguard coverage because municipal lifeguards are stationed only at the main public beach.

There is no confirmed record of shark incidents at Aksa. The documented danger here is the water itself, not marine wildlife.
