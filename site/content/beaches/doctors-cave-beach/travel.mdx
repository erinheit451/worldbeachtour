# Doctor's Cave Beach Travel Guide

Doctor's Cave Beach is a paid-admission club on Jimmy Cliff Boulevard (formerly Gloucester Avenue) in Montego Bay, on Jamaica's north coast. Getting there is not the hard part; almost every visitor arrives within minutes of landing. The harder part to plan around is that it is a private club with fixed hours, not an open beach, and that the area is recovering from the worst hurricane in Jamaican history.

**By Air.** Sangster International Airport (MBJ) is Jamaica's busiest airport and the arrival point for most visitors to this beach. The site's dataset lists a straight-line distance of 2.4 km from the beach; the actual road distance, per routing data, is about 2.1 km (1.3 miles), roughly a 3-minute taxi ride. MBJ took the worst structural damage of Jamaica's three international airports in Hurricane Melissa on October 28, 2025, with its terminal roof torn open; the airport reopened for relief flights on October 30 and passenger service resumed over the following weeks. Jamaica's tourism ministry declared the sector 71 percent ready when the 2025/26 winter season opened on December 15, 2025, with all three international airports operational and airlines including Copa adding flights into Montego Bay. Confirm your airline's current MBJ service before booking, since schedules were still stabilizing as of that date.

**By Car.** Montego Bay sits on Jamaica's north coast road network. Kingston, the capital, is about 175 km away by road, roughly 2 hours 45 minutes' drive under normal conditions. The beach itself fronts Jimmy Cliff Boulevard in the center of Montego Bay's hotel and restaurant district, sometimes called the Hip Strip.

**By Local Transit.** Route taxis (shared minibuses) run along the main roads into central Montego Bay; licensed taxis and ride-hailing options are more straightforward for visitors unfamiliar with the shared-taxi system.

## Hours and Admission

The club is open 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m. daily. Admission, checked against the club's own site in July 2026, is $8 for adults (12 and older) and $4 for children under 12. That fee is a genuine point of difference from Jamaica's public beaches, which charge nothing but also offer none of this club's lifeguard coverage, lockers, or showers. The 5:30 p.m. closing is worth noting on its own: it falls before Montego Bay's sunset for most of the year, so this is not the place to plan a sunset session.

## Hurricane Context

Jamaica's Atlantic hurricane season runs June through November. The site's dataset records 15 tropical cyclone passages near this coastline over the past 50 years, with a documented maximum of Category 5, a threshold reached at landfall by Hurricane Melissa on October 28, 2025, near New Hope in Westmoreland Parish. Melissa became the costliest hurricane in Jamaican history at an estimated $12.2 billion in damage, killed 45 people nationally, and damaged roughly 150,000 structures. Two deaths were recorded in St. James Parish, the parish Montego Bay and this beach sit in. As of mid-2026 the beach itself reads as recovered in current condition reports, but travelers visiting during hurricane season should check forecasts and current advisories rather than assume pre-storm conditions.

## Where to Stay

The Hip Strip along Jimmy Cliff Boulevard is the walkable option: the 90-room Doctor's Cave Beach Hotel sits steps from the club's entrance, and the strip is dense with other hotels, restaurants, and Margaritaville Montego Bay, the area's best-known nightlife venue. Further east, the Ironshore and Rose Hall areas host larger resort properties set back from the town center, trading walkability for more space and quieter grounds. Current room rates vary by season and property; check individual hotels directly rather than relying on cached listings, since Hurricane Melissa's late-2025 recovery affected pricing and availability across the region into 2026.

## Safety

The reef that shelters this cove keeps water conditions calmer than the open coast; the site's safety data records zero recorded shark incidents at this location. Water quality inside a marine park with fringing-reef protection is generally better managed than unprotected stretches, though Jamaica's coastal waters, like any tropical destination near a populated coastline, warrant the standard precautions after heavy rain. General urban-safety practices apply on Jimmy Cliff Boulevard and around Montego Bay: use registered taxis or hotel-arranged transport, don't leave valuables unattended on the beach, and keep an eye on official advisories during hurricane season given the coastline's recorded storm history.

## Local Tips

**Go before mid-morning.** The heaviest crowds arrive with cruise-ship shore excursions from the Montego Bay Freeport terminal; visitors on non-cruise days or before excursion groups arrive get a quieter cove.

**Don't expect a beachfront restaurant.** The club's listed facilities are the clubhouse, lockers, showers, and lifeguard service, not a kitchen. Eat on Jimmy Cliff Boulevard before or after.

**The name is the history.** The club has been called Doctor's Cave since Dr. Alexander James McCatty's physician friends bathed here in the early 1900s; asking staff about the club's century-plus history is a better use of a slow afternoon than most beach-vendor small talk.

**Check post-hurricane status before finalizing plans.** Given Hurricane Melissa's October 2025 landfall and the region's documented storm exposure, confirm current hours, admission, and any facility changes directly with the club rather than relying on older reviews.
