Doctor's Cave Beach is a private, fee-gated cove on Jimmy Cliff Boulevard in Montego Bay, Jamaica, run by the Doctor's Cave Bathing Club since 1906. It is named for Dr. Alexander James McCatty, who founded a sanatorium in Montego Bay in 1880 and let friends bathe at his small beach, reached through a cave; in 1906 he donated the property to formalize it as a members' club with the friends — described by local tradition as fellow doctors — who bathed alongside him. Admission today runs $8 for adults and $4 for children under 12, checked against the club's own site in July 2026, and buys access from 8:30 a.m. to 5:30 p.m.

## What Doctor's Cave Actually Is

The cove sits inside a natural reef break, which keeps the water calm compared to the open Caribbean beyond it. That calm water is what built the beach's reputation in the first place, though the mechanism was marketing, not medicine: in the 1920s, Herbert Barker, an English manipulative surgeon, wrote an article praising the water's supposed curative properties. The claim was never a clinical finding, but it landed as transatlantic steamship tourism was opening the Caribbean to wealthy North American and European visitors, and it is credited with turning a local bathing club into an internationally known destination.

The beach is not free. It has run as a private club with paid day access for more than a century, which is a different arrangement than the public beaches elsewhere along the same stretch of coast. In exchange, visitors get a lifeguarded, gated, groomed cove rather than an unmanaged one. The club has funded conservation work at Montego Bay Marine Park, the protected zone its water sits inside, since 2017, and partnered with Recycling Partners of Jamaica on plastic waste since 2023.

The address itself changed identity in 2019: the strip out front, Gloucester Avenue for most of the twentieth century, was renamed Jimmy Cliff Boulevard that March, honoring the reggae musician at a ceremony at Old Hospital Park — the site local reporting says was his actual birthplace, not just his home parish — where Tessanne Chin and other artists performed his songs in his honor. Then, on October 28, 2025, Hurricane Melissa made landfall in Jamaica as a Category 5 storm, the costliest in the country's recorded history. Sangster International Airport, about 2.8 km away by road (roughly 5 minutes), took the worst structural damage of Jamaica's three international airports. The beach itself reads as recovered as of mid-2026, but the storm is recent enough that it belongs in any current account of the place, not a historical footnote.

## Most Relevant Lenses

**History** — A doctor's 1906 land donation and a British surgeon's unverified 1920s health claim are the two events that turned a private cove into a tourism landmark; neither claim was ever medically tested.

**Access and Economics** — This is a paid, gated club, not a public beach, in a country where coastal access is often privatized. The $8/$4 admission and the club's conservation funding since 2017 are both real, and both raise the same underlying question of who gets to use this water.

**Marine Conservation** — The cove sits inside Montego Bay Marine Park, Jamaica's first marine protected area, established in 1992 after a smaller 1974 protection order, and co-managed with Jamaica's Natural Resources Conservation Authority (NRCA) since 1996.

**Storm Exposure** — This coastline has recorded 15 tropical cyclone passages in 50 years, with a documented maximum of Category 5, a maximum Hurricane Melissa reached at landfall on October 28, 2025.
